# Research methodology and eligibility

**Adopted: 2026-11-15.** This document supersedes the former rule that a recording had to satisfy composition, raw-audio and rights tests before it could even be treated as a candidate. It preserves a strict historical core while making discovery proportionate to the evidence that survives in early recording archives.

## Project target

An international archive of early recorded songs in which people answer injury, injustice, betrayal, humiliation, oppression or loss through revenge, retaliation, punishment, defiance, protest, curses, feuds or other forms of grievance.

This is not an archive of generic sadness or anger. A song must articulate or dramatize a response to a perceived wrong, or preserve a socially meaningful grievance narrative. Local concepts and genres must be explained in context rather than flattened into English categories.

## 1. Historical recording window

The core collection remains recordings **published or fixed before 1926-01-01**. Record publication/issue date and recording/fixation date separately; neither can be silently substituted for the other.

Use one of four date assessments:
- **CORE DATE — VERIFIED:** primary or strong corroborated evidence establishes publication or fixation before the cutoff.
- **PROBABLE DATE:** catalogue, matrix, session, label or contemporary trade evidence strongly points to a pre-cutoff master, but one detail remains unresolved.
- **DATE UNCERTAIN:** credible range straddles the cutoff, or evidence is conflicting.
- **OUTSIDE CORE:** established post-1925 recording. Preserve as a contextual/appendix lead only when useful; do not quietly include it in the core.

A catalogue's publication date may establish an issue existed by that date, but does not automatically establish when the master was recorded. A date code must be interpreted using the correct label/format system and, where possible, a physical label/runout.

## 2. Composition and text chronology

The former author-death-before-1956 rule is no longer a discovery gate. Establish what composition or text is actually performed, and its chronology, as far as evidence allows.

- A composition/text demonstrably created or circulating before the recording cutoff may qualify on chronology, subject to version-specific authorship and rights review.
- A traditional, anonymous or orally transmitted item may qualify when its circulation, provenance or historical tradition is documented; do not invent an author or a falsely precise origin date.
- A later arrangement, translation, adaptation, added verse or newly written lyric must be identified separately. An old tune does not make a later text old.
- Unknown or disputed authorship/date means **PROVISIONAL**, not automatic rejection. Keep the question visible and research it.
- Record authors, composers, lyricists, translators, arrangers and adapters separately. A death date is useful rights evidence, not a proxy for the age of every element in a performance.

## 3. Thematic eligibility

A candidate should show at least one meaningful grievance-response mechanism, supported by lyrics, performance, dramatic context or reliable historical documentation.

**Core thematic families**
- revenge / calculated or delayed payback;
- retaliation / answering an injury;
- punishment, retribution or sanction;
- defiance, resistance or refusal in response to coercion or injustice;
- accusation, grievance testimony or public counter-speech;
- curses, feuds, vendettas, blood-debt and collective or inherited grievance;
- spite or deliberate costly harm.

**Conditional families**
- lament, resentment, betrayal, heartbreak, mourning or loss qualify only when the work also contains an accusation, demand, threat, vow, refusal, answer, grievance claim or intention to retaliate/resist.
- A narrative may qualify through a character's or community's response even when the singer does not endorse that response. Label the perspective accurately.
- Protest and grievance testimony belong in the expanded archive, but must not be mislabelled as revenge.

**Not sufficient by itself:** a title containing “revenge,” a violent plot, jealousy, generic anger, sadness, heartbreak, or a character who happens to be an outlaw. Read/listen to the actual version where possible. Distinguish documented events, folklore, literary representation, stage convention and later popular myth.

## 4. Candidate evidence levels

Candidate means **worth investigating**, not ready for release.

- **A — VERIFIED RESEARCH CANDIDATE:** exact recording/master is identified; relevant content and thematic mechanism are supported; date and version/composition chronology are established to a defensible standard; provenance is documented. This is ready for editorial consideration, not automatic commercial use.
- **B — PROBABLE CANDIDATE:** strong recording identity and thematic evidence, with one or more material questions unresolved (for example exact matrix, date, text variant, or archive transfer). Keep active.
- **C — LEAD:** promising title, narrative, performer, catalogue entry, print witness or oral tradition, but insufficient evidence linking the relevant content to an exact eligible recording. Research, do not count as a track.
- **REJECTED / OUTSIDE CORE:** a documented reason makes the item unsuitable for the core. Preserve the reason and any contextual value.

Do not use one status to conceal different kinds of uncertainty. State the unresolved fields explicitly. Earlier labels such as PARTIALLY VERIFIED and HIGH-PRIORITY LEAD may remain in historical notes; when re-auditing, map them transparently to A/B/C and preserve the original wording in the audit trail.

## 5. Audio object, provenance and access

Raw, unedited archival transfer remains the preferred production source, but it is **not a prerequisite for discovery or thematic candidacy**.

Track audio status independently:
- original carrier inspected;
- archival preservation transfer inspected;
- archival transfer available, provenance/processing incomplete;
- access derivative only (streaming MP3, compressed copy, edited excerpt, etc.);
- audio located but inaccessible / access restricted;
- no audio located.

Record carrier (wax cylinder, shellac disc, other), label/catalogue/matrix/take, archive identifier, file format, direct URL where permitted, transfer chain, stylus/speed choices, restoration, equalization, filtering, noise reduction, edits and derivative-generation details where known. Do not call a file “raw” or “unprocessed” without evidence. Do not mistake a streaming access copy for a preservation master.

A print text, stage score, later performance or oral-history account may establish a valuable thematic lead; it does not prove that the same words occur on a particular surviving record.

## 6. Rights and commercial clearance

Rights are a separate workstream, not an entry ticket for research. Record status independently for:
- underlying composition and lyrics (including translations/adaptations);
- sound recording / master / phonogram;
- performer, producer and other applicable neighbouring or related rights;
- transfer, restoration, digitization and supplied file;
- repository terms, access conditions and contractual restrictions;
- intended territories and intended uses.

Use explicit states such as **UNKNOWN**, **RESEARCH NEEDED**, **PERMISSION REQUIRED**, **PERMISSION OBTAINED**, **PUBLIC-DOMAIN EVIDENCE FOUND**, **RESTRICTED ACCESS**, and **LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED**. Public streaming is not permission to download, redistribute, sample or commercially release. A public-domain conclusion in one territory is not a worldwide conclusion. No candidate is commercially cleared by this research status; final clearance requires human legal/editorial review.

## 7. Research sequence

Search should begin with local grievance narratives and musical forms, not only English keyword searches or the contents of a handful of digitized archives:

1. Identify locally meaningful narratives, concepts, genres, performance settings and vocabulary in original languages/scripts.
2. Find printed, oral, theatrical, literary and historical witnesses; distinguish their evidentiary role and date.
3. Trace early musical settings, adaptations and commercial repertoire, preserving version distinctions.
4. Search label catalogues, trade journals, discographies, sessionographies, matrix lists and national/company archives for exact recording identities.
5. Link the content to the exact master/issue and assess date confidence.
6. Locate the carrier and archive object; inspect audio and document transfer/access conditions.
7. Assess rights independently by layer and territory.
8. Write a contextual note only when the evidence supports it; otherwise record the lead and next concrete research step.

Search local and overseas recording industries, minority-language and diaspora repertoires, original scripts and transliteration variants. Use field recordings as valuable documentation and discovery routes, but identify them as field/ethnographic rather than commercial issues. Do not impose geographic quotas or infer identity from an archive's broad geographic label. Record access gaps, colonial collecting histories, uneven digitization and restrictions.

## 8. Required record fields

For every B/A candidate, and as much as possible for C leads, record:
- stable ID, evidence level, date assessment, thematic family and proposed editorial role;
- performer(s), title as catalogued, original language/script, local genre and transliteration variants;
- recording/fixation date and publication/issue date separately, each with evidence;
- recording location, label, catalogue number, matrix and take;
- exact archive item and direct audio link where available; carrier, file format and audio/transfer status;
- performed text/version, source for transcription, original-language excerpt, literal and idiomatic translation, and language-review status;
- composition/text chronology and distinct credits for author, composer, lyricist, translator, arranger and adapter;
- rights status by layer, territory and intended use;
- taxonomic argument, context, confidence, open questions and next research action.

Never invent lyrics, translations, matrices, dates, rights conclusions or audio URLs. Cite primary evidence wherever possible and label inference.

## 9. Editorial and clearance boundary

A-level evidence means ready for editorial consideration, not selected, sequenced or cleared. Production selection, sequencing, liner notes and commercial clearance remain separate decisions. The editorial owner has final authority. Preserve local terminology, uncertainty and disagreement; do not force every grievance into “revenge.”

## 10. Mix-building flexibility (trial proposal; editorial approval pending)

Thematic and evidence standards above remain unchanged. For mix sequencing, classify tracks by editorial role rather than demanding that every recording independently contain a complete revenge plot:

- **Core:** the recording directly expresses a grievance response (revenge, retaliation, punishment, protest, accusation, counter-insult, refusal, etc.).
- **Narrative:** the recording tells a grievance story or one stage of a documented revenge/feud/betrayal cycle.
- **Context:** the recording illuminates the memory, aftermath, mourning, commemoration or social meaning of a grievance but is not itself a direct act of revenge. Label it as context.

A mix may combine these roles; the liner note must distinguish what is audible from what belongs to the larger story. A provisional 10–12 track recipe is 4–6 core, 3–4 narrative and 1–3 context tracks, with no quota: a shorter mix is preferable to padding weak material. Candidate evidence levels remain independent of editorial usefulness.

The pre-1926 historical core remains intact. A separately labelled 1926–1930 afterlife/extension track may be proposed for a mix, but must be marked OUTSIDE CORE and never counted as an eligible early recording. Later field/ethnographic recordings may serve as tradition witnesses in a distinct role, subject to cultural protocols and access/reuse conditions. See `research/mix-building-flexibility-proposal-2026-11-15.md`; this trial framework is pending editorial approval.

## 10. Collaboration and division of work

The project owner/editor sets priorities, scope, mix concepts and final inclusion decisions. The work is divided into two coordinated operational streams, following the working pattern used in `sloev/insanemusic`:

- **ChatGPT — research and evaluation:** global discovery; local-language and genre research; archive/catalogue/press/discography digging; exact recording identification; date and version cross-checks; lyric/text and thematic evaluation; candidate dossiers; global map; methodology; mix concepts; research queue; and append-only cooperation log. ChatGPT prepares precise audio targets and source URLs for retrieval; it does not claim to have downloaded or auditioned files it cannot access.
- **Grok — audio retrieval:** works from the explicit `READY FOR GROK` queue in `TRACK_MAPPING.md` and the companion `GROK_HANDOFF.md`; retrieves the specified tracks to the shared Drive folder; preserves the original file; and reports exact filenames, source URLs, retrieval result, format/size/duration where available, date and any mismatch or access obstacle. Grok is not the authority for historical/thematic promotion.
- **Project owner/editor:** sets priorities and resolves editorial decisions; approves mixes and final selection.

### Shared operating protocol
- Read this methodology and the latest `coop.log.md` before every session; also inspect `README.md`, `research_method.md`, `TRACK_MAPPING.md`, the global map and relevant dossiers.
- State search boundaries and record queries, languages and archives searched. Give source URLs, page numbers, catalogue IDs and explain what each source proves.
- Separate direct observation, source statement and inference. AI-generated translations, transcriptions, summaries and identifications remain provisional until checked.
- Keep research evidence grade (A/B/C), chronology, thematic fit, audio identity/provenance, access and rights as independent dimensions. A successful download is not evidence of thematic fit; an inaccessible file is not grounds to discard a well-supported lead.
- ChatGPT prepares a precise **READY FOR GROK** queue with stable ID, exact recording identity, source page/direct audio URL, expected filename, catalogue/matrix/archive identifiers, date confidence and caveats. Grok reports retrieval outcomes; ChatGPT reconciles those reports into the mapping and research records.
- Shared destination: https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1cOwvMN1k_bLznh-1K8bCHzRQPAJjnGRO . Treat files as **GROK REPORTED DOWNLOADED** until independently inspected or supported by verifiable file metadata; then mark **FILE VERIFIED**.
- Never claim to have listened, inspected a file/object, contacted an archive, downloaded a track or obtained permission unless actually done by the named actor.
- Append dated log entries; do not silently overwrite prior findings or erase disagreements. Before editing, fetch the latest revision of every file being changed.
- Hand-offs should include: question → sources searched → findings → evidence/uncertainty → exact retrieval queue → files changed/commits → next verification step.
- Rights clearance may be deferred during private research as a separate workstream, but archive access restrictions and download terms remain factual constraints and must not be misrepresented.

See [`GROK_HANDOFF.md`](GROK_HANDOFF.md) for the operational queue and reporting template.


## 10. International and de-centred research coverage

The project is global in scope. Discovery must not default to the United States, Britain, Western Europe, English-language sources, or the cataloguing practices of multinational labels. Actively investigate regional archives, local-language scholarship, independent labels, diasporic collections, oral traditions, and the social settings in which recordings circulated. Current research directions include South Asia; the Arab East and North Africa; Anatolia and Ottoman diasporas; East and Southeast Asia; Latin America and the Caribbean; Sub-Saharan Africa and the Indian Ocean; Eastern Europe and the Balkans; and Indigenous and diasporic archives.

This is not a quota system. Do not add weak or token entries for regional balance. Apply the same standards of exact performance identity, date, text, provenance and rights everywhere. Preserve original scripts, local genre names, transliteration variants and translation uncertainty. Do not let a Western archive's metadata become the only authority for local musical meaning. Distinguish a composition's date, a stage premiere, an original performance, a recording session, a disc issue, a later cover and an archival transfer.
