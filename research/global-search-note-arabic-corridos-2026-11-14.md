# Global archive search note: Arabic 78s and Mexican corridos

**Search date:** 2026-11-14  
**Status:** reconnaissance only; no new candidate admitted  
**Scope:** Harvard Loeb Arabic 78 Collection, Frontera Collection, Gramophone Company Discography, and Oriental HMV matrix charts.

## Findings

### Arabic / Arab-American recordings

Harvard's Loeb Music Library describes a collection of nearly 600 catalogued Arab and Arab-American recordings spanning approximately 1903–1950s. The holdings include multinational companies (Gramophone, Columbia, Victor, Odeon), regional labels (including Baidaphon and Fabrik Mechian), and US Arab-American labels. Its publicly shared Aviary subset includes recordings released before 31 December 1922 that can be streamed and downloaded; some records remain restricted. Harvard explicitly warns that dating early discs is difficult and that only a small number can be dated from surviving company records.

Useful gateways:
- Collection overview and recording examples: https://archive.blogs.harvard.edu/loebmusic/2017/09/05/arabic-78-rpm-records-collection-a-newly-catalogued-treasure/
- Public-domain subset and dating caveat: https://archive.blogs.harvard.edu/loebmusic/category/digital-collections/
- Early Arabic recordings release note: https://mideast.wisc.edu/2022/01/21/early-arabic-recordings-now-in-public-domain/
- Catalogued collection guide: https://studies.laurentfintoni.com/mji/music_history/mji239/

A broad catalog search surfaced titles such as Asmahan's “I am the daughter of the Nile” from the film *Gharām wa-intiqām* (Love and Revenge), but that film/song belongs to 1944 and is outside the 1925 master cutoff. It is not a candidate. The phrase is a useful reminder to search the original Arabic script and title variants, but must not be mistaken for an early recording.

No Arabic-language song about revenge, feud, insult, debt, or a maintained grievance has yet been tied to a specific pre-1926 disc, exact matrix/take, and qualifying archive transfer. Do not promote any item from this pass.

### Latin America / Mexican corridos

Frontera's collection is a valuable discovery index: it includes nearly 170,000 recordings, earliest c.1905, with corridos among its represented forms. But title or format alone is insufficient; many relevant revenge-themed hits are later recordings or later reissues.

Search results:
- “La Venganza De Los Tres,” Eleázar García y Los Chenchos, Falcon A845, matrix F-2535: Frontera dates the 78 recording 1958-04-02. **REJECTED** for the master cutoff.
  https://frontera.library.ucla.edu/recordings/la-venganza-de-los-tres-1
- “El Corrido De Luis Coronado,” Hermanas Padilla, Anfion 10-002-A, matrix AB-009: Frontera identifies a 78 and subjects including revenge, but the displayed record supplies no recording date. The date, original issue chronology, composition authorship, and raw archival transfer are unverified. **LEAD FOR DISCOVERY ONLY; NOT A CANDIDATE.**
  https://frontera.library.ucla.edu/recordings/el-corrido-de-luis-coronado
- “Venganza Cumplida,” Ernesto Baez y Los Cadetes de Nuevo León: format is 45 rpm, not a 78; **REJECTED** for this brief.
  https://frontera.library.ucla.edu/recordings/venganza-cumplida-5
- “La Venganza,” Los Canarios de la Costa: cassette recording; **REJECTED** for this brief.
  https://frontera.library.ucla.edu/recordings/la-venganza

The Frontera results show that keyword discovery can return recordings decades outside the target period. The next useful step is to inspect early original issue numbers, label scans and matrix evidence for pre-1926 corridos rather than assuming that a 78-format result is early.

### Discographical routes

- The Gramophone Company Discography is searchable across performer, title, matrix, issue, recording date and location, and describes its corpus as the Gramophone Company and successors' 78rpm-era recordings beginning in 1898. Its own site notes that standardisation of titles and names is imperfect, so query variants are necessary.
  https://www.gramophonecompanydiscography.com/
- The Oriental HMV matrix chart covers Turkish, Greek, Arabic, Hebrew, Sephardic, Armenian, Albanian, Serbian, Bulgarian, Kurdish and Laz recordings, with matrix ranges, locations and recording periods from 1900–1919. It is a dating aid, not itself proof of a particular title, issue or audio transfer.
  https://www.recordingpioneers.com/docs/Oriental-HMV-CHARTS-1900-1919.pdf

## Research decisions

1. No new item meets the evidence threshold for a candidate file.
2. Do not treat Frontera's subject tags as proof of lyric content or historical truth; listen and obtain a transcription before assigning taxonomy.
3. Do not treat an Arabic translation such as *intiqām* or a Turkish search term such as *intikam* as a complete cultural taxonomy. Search spelling, dialect, script and local genre variants with knowledgeable language review.
4. A publicly streamable/downloadable archive derivative is not automatically a raw, unedited archival transfer or commercially reusable master.
5. Prioritise primary catalogues, label/runout images, exact matrix/take, and a direct file with transfer provenance.
