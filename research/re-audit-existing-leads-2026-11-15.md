# Existing-lead re-audit under revised methodology — 2026-11-15

**Purpose:** map previously researched items to the new A/B/C evidence scale and the expanded grievance brief. This is a status crosswalk, not a claim that every source, audio file or physical carrier has been re-examined. The original candidate notes remain authoritative for detailed evidence and caveats. No rights clearance is inferred.

## Summary

- **A — VERIFIED RESEARCH CANDIDATE:** none assigned in this first crosswalk. This is deliberate: the current notes do not yet give a sufficiently complete combination of exact master identity, performed-version content and chronology to warrant A without further item-level checking.
- **B — PROBABLE CANDIDATE:** 8 provisional items with strong recording and thematic linkage but unresolved master/audio/text details.
- **C — LEAD:** 18 recording-linked or textual leads requiring stronger linkage, content or chronology evidence.
- **Outside core / rejected:** 1 Greek false positive. One early South Asian pass and one Arabic pass produced no item-level leads.

The apparent increase in candidates is not seven release-ready tracks. B means active, promising research candidates. Audio and rights statuses remain independent.

## Regional screenings with no item-level candidate

- **South Asia:** the 1902–03 Gramophone expedition and Indian discographies remain high-value infrastructure, but the first pass did not identify a specific song/master. Continue local-script and genre-based searches; do not call the region exhausted.
- **Arabic-language records:** AMAR and Harvard Loeb are substantial discovery routes. The first title pass found controls but no item with sufficiently supported grievance content. The Dinshaway oral mawwāl leads are separate from any commercial recording.
- **Korean Fletcher cylinders (1896):** securely documented field recordings, but the surfaced track descriptions do not currently establish grievance-response content; they are not commercial releases.
- **Greek screening:** the located *Fonias tha gino* masters are post-cutoff and thematically a possessive threat rather than revenge.

## Immediate priorities after the crosswalk

1. **Listen and transcribe** the highest-value exact items where access exists: Plaatje, Kumoemon, Victor 1769, the relevant Japanese Naniwabushi items, and any available Armenian/European archive access copies.
2. **Resolve exact master identity and chronology** for Papus Rache, Verdi, Handel and the Mexican cylinders. Do not let a printed score, later performance or catalogue title stand in for the recorded version.
3. **Translate and review original-language text** for Ewe, Yoruba, Armenian, Japanese, Yiddish and any newly surfaced South Asian/Arabic items.
4. **Maintain an independent audio and rights register.** Streaming access, an archive finding aid or a public-domain composition does not establish commercial master/transfer clearance.
5. Search anew under the broader thematic families, starting with locally documented grievance narratives and then tracing their early recorded versions. Do not treat this crosswalk as a quota or a final selection list.

## Methodological note

The crosswalk intentionally keeps several attractive historical narratives at C where the link to the exact recording is weak. Conversely, the former “Franky & Johnny” rejection based solely on Bert Leighton's death after 1955 is now B: the new policy asks whether the performed version and composition chronology are defensible, rather than using that death-date rule as a blanket discovery veto. No track is A or commercially cleared at this stage.


## 2026-11-15 follow-up — Kumoemon item-level identification

Nichibunken's Rōkyoku SP Record Digital Archive now supplies an exact entry for Tōchūken Kumoemon's *Bushidou kosui Akagaki genzou tokuri no wakare*: Lyrophone 70937–70940, two discs/four sides, published May 1912, archive register 519319. ## 2026-11-15 follow-up — Gene Greene performed-text witness

A 2025 University of Oregon thesis provides a transcription explicitly attributed to Gene Greene's 1912 recording, with the source note pointing to the *Before the Big Bang* track note/reissue. The text has Frankie discover Johnny with Alice Bly, draw a revolver and shoot him; it then includes the wounded man's response and judicial consequences. This supports immediate lethal retaliation following betrayal, while distinguishing the Greene performance from the separately reproduced Leighton Brothers sheet-music text. Because the transcript derives from a modern reissue rather than a documented original Pathé 5370 carrier in this pass, it strengthens thematic linkage but does not resolve exact master identity, matrix/take, or audio provenance. Keep B — PROBABLE. Full evidence and caveats are in `research/first-pass-screening.md`.

## Japan — expanded Yoshida Naramaru Akō series; accessible archive recordings

Nichibunken/CiNii parent record BB17632622 groups seven Naramaru SP items under *Akō Gishiden*: *Ōishi Kuranosuke Yamashina-gaeri*, *Kanzaki Yūgorō*, *Hazama Jūjirō* (including lower part), *Nanbuzaka Yuki no Wakare*, *Tonochū Jinshō*, *Akagaki Genzō*, and *Ōtaka Gengō*. Specialist discography associates 1723–1724 with *Hazama Jūjirō*, 1725–1728 with four-part *Kanzaki Yūgorō*, 1729/1732 with *Ōishi Kuranosuke Yamashina-gaeri*, and 1733–1734 with *Amanoya Rihei*. These are catalogue associations, not dates.

Two other archive items have searchable/public audio records: (1) *Akagaki Genzō Tokuri no Wakare*, Naramaru, Royal 1853, one-sided 3:42, Nichibunken reg=525671, NCID BB17636022, call no. 05084; playback condition poor; revenge content not established, so contextual/control item. (2) *Kanzaki Azumakudari*, Naramaru, Nipponophone 15572–15574, three discs, reg=525710, NCID BB17678984, call no. 05123; accessible audio interface, but date and exact thematic content unknown. Do not conflate this with the separate 1725–1728 *Kanzaki Yūgorō* sequence.

A Nichibunken 1912 booklet, *Yoshida Naramaru Kōen Naniwabushi Ohakoshū* (27 pp.; NCID BB24153492; reg=553460), lists *Nanbuzaka Yuki no Wakare* and an excerpt from *Kanzaki Yūgorō Azumakudari* in Naramaru's repertoire by 8 May 1912. It is a printed performance witness, not proof of the date or text of any specific disc. Page-image retrieval failed in this pass.

Access route: Nichibunken asks for applications at least one week in advance through a university/public library, limit 10 titles/day, requests use of available digital audio in preference to disc playback. Request call numbers 05077, 05084, 05123 and ask about the 05077 digital file. Detailed research: research/japan-ako-gishi-yoshida-naramaru-royal-record.md. ## Japan — *Denchū Ninjō*: strong 1912 thematic text; two separate recording branches

A 1912 (Meiji 45) digitized booklet, *Naniwabushi Meikyoku-sen*, includes Yoshida Naramaru's *Denchū Ninjō* text. In the passage 「殿中忍従」, Asano endures insults to protect his retainers, is called a “dog samurai” / “inhuman,” loses patience and draws his short sword against Ueno/Kira, fails to achieve his purpose, and the text explicitly says his 47 retainers later avenge their lord at Sengakuji. This establishes the story/text's thematic fit (grievance, insult, failed retaliation, collective revenge) by 1912, but is a printed text, not proof of any specific recording's contents. A second Naramaru booklet *Gishi no Issetsu — Naniwabushi Jūhachi-ban, Yuki no Maki* lists the sequence from palace attack through seppuku and the later raid / fulfillment of the retainers' purpose; its date still needs bibliographic verification.

Keep two *Denchū Ninjō* recordings distinct: (1) earlier Nipponophone/Royal-era Yoshida Naramaru no. 1651 listed by 78MUSIC and likely represented in Nichibunken/CiNii parent BB17632622; exact label, matrix and date unverified. (2) Taihei four-disc 25 cm set, Yoshida Naramaru III, CiNii BB17753999, numbers 10254–10257, Nichibunken call nos. 05193/05195; publisher date only [19--], chronology unresolved and likely separate later-generation performance. Do not merge them or assume either is pre-1926. Detailed text, scan links and caveats: research/japan-ako-gishi-yoshida-naramaru-royal-record.md.
