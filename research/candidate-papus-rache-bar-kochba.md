## Composition and narrative context

Abraham Goldfaden (1840–1908) wrote *Bar Kochba*, a Yiddish operetta first performed in Odessa in 1883. The plot is a melodramatic, historically imaginative treatment of the second-century revolt associated with Simon bar Kokhba. Papus is represented as a former friend who becomes a Roman spy; he has two named arias, “Papus' Revenge Song” and “Papus' Deceit.” The opera's conclusion includes a Jewish captain taking revenge on Papus.

Goldfaden's death in 1908 satisfies the stated composer death-date threshold, assuming the recorded composition is indeed his work and no later lyricist, translator, adapter or arranger holds a relevant composition contribution. The 1897 New York piano-vocal publication lists “Papus' Revenge Song” as a number in the opera, but the printed publication's copyright and arranger credits must be checked against the specific recorded arrangement.

## Taxonomic assessment

**Primary classification: Calculated Revenge (fictional dramatic representation).**

This classification is based on the work's explicit plot function and the named aria. It should not be upgraded to a historical example of Jewish-Roman retaliation or a record of collective grievance without analysis of the libretto. The wider operetta draws on the Bar Kokhba revolt and later Jewish national memory, but its plot is a nineteenth-century theatrical construction.

## Further verification pass — score, stage manuscript and label chronology (2026-11-15)

Additional evidence strengthens the **composition and theatrical-context** side of this lead, but not the exact Homokord master:
- The Library of Congress Irene Heskes finding aid explicitly lists **“Dinah's welcome to Jerusalem: Papus revenge song,” circa 1900s**, by A. Goldfaden, arranged by H.A. Russotta, as digitized sheet music in the collection. It also lists a complete *Bar Kochba* piano score dated 1909, arranged by Henry A. Russotto and described as words and music by Goldfaden. This independently corroborates that the Papus revenge number circulated as a named operetta number in early twentieth-century U.S. Yiddish music publishing.
- The University Library Frankfurt scan of the 1897 *Bar Kochba* score lists “Papus' Revenge Song” as No. 5 in volume 3. Goldfaden's 1840–1908 lifespan remains clear; the recording-specific question is whether the Homokord performance is the Goldfaden number, rather than another song from the same operetta or a later adaptation.
- YIVO's manuscript record for the *Bar Kochba* first violin part dates the manuscript group 1901–1911 and notes performances in Oswiecim (1901) and Brody (1907), with manuscript stamps from 1905, 1907 and 1911. This documents the work's active stage life, but is not the Papus recording itself.
- A separate Lemberg 1907–08 record, Jacob Fuchs's **“Der Bucher”**, Beka-Grand 10872, is labelled as from *Bar Kochba* in a specialist historical account. The author cannot find it in the printed editions or surviving sheet music and suggests it may be a lost/temporary addition to the opera's song corpus; its mood might fit Papus, but there is no proof it is “Papus' Revenge Song.” Keep it as a **separate unidentified Bar Kochba number**, not an alternate title for Homokord 1118.
- Label chronology is now better understood: Homophon was renamed Homokord in 1907 after a trademark dispute; from 1912 the company name was again Homophon, while its principal label retained the spelling Homokord; in 1924 the label spelling changed to Homocord. Thus the spelling “Homocord 1118” in the secondary list may be a later normalization or transcription error. This broad corporate chronology makes an early Homokord-era issue plausible, but does **not** date catalogue number 1118. Do not infer eligibility from the number alone.
- Rainer E. Lotz and Axel Weggen's 2006 *Discographie der Judaica-Aufnahmen* states that its first volume covers Jewish-related recordings in the German-speaking world and expressly excludes Yiddish and cantorial recordings made especially in Lemberg, Czernowitz and Budapest. Its failure to surface this Lemberg lead is therefore not a negative finding about the record's existence.

## Further check — Lemberg Beka 10872 and the actual Papus aria (2026-11-15)

A closer reading of the annotated Gimpel's Theatre discography, reproduced by Forgotten Galicia, sharpens the distinction between the recorded Bar Kochba excerpt and the unlocated Papus aria:

- Track 20 identifies Jacob Fuchs, “Der Bucher aus der Operette ‘Bar Kochba’ mit Chor mit Violine,” Beka-Grand-Record 10872, Lemberg, 1907–1908. Its annotation says the title is on the record label, but the song cannot be found in printed editions or surviving Bar Kochba sheet music. The author leaves open whether the label is wrong or the song was temporarily added to the performance corpus.
- The same annotation says the song's mood might suit Papus, the villain associated with money and revenge; it compares the laughter refrain and references to blind and crippled people with the separately named “Di nekome fun Papus” (“Papus's Revenge”). This is an interpretive comparison, not an identification of track 20 as that aria.
- The annotation transcribes a fragment in which the singer sarcastically seeks a philanthropist who feeds the poor and shares money, then threatens to “give him what for.” That text suggests a comic/menacing character portrait; it does not establish the plot, target, prior injury, or calculated payback of Papus's actual revenge aria.
- The page's 26-track contents list “Der Bucher” as item 20 and does not list “Di nekome fun Papus.” The revenge aria therefore remains unlocated in this source.
- The page invites Yiddish-speaking readers to help decipher texts, cautioning against overconfidence in transcription. Its audio is a listening lead, but is not established as a raw, unedited archival transfer with commercial reuse rights.

**Decision:** retain Homocord/Homokord 1118 “Papus Rache” as a separate **LEAD**. Retain Beka-Grand 10872 as a distinct Bar Kochba-labelled recording, not an alternate title for the Papus aria. No eligibility promotion.
