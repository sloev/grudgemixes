## Composition and narrative context

Abraham Goldfaden (1840–1908) wrote *Bar Kochba*, a Yiddish operetta first performed in Odessa in 1883. The plot is a melodramatic, historically imaginative treatment of the second-century revolt associated with Simon bar Kokhba. Papus is represented as a former friend who becomes a Roman spy; he has two named arias, “Papus' Revenge Song” and “Papus' Deceit.” The opera's conclusion includes a Jewish captain taking revenge on Papus.

Goldfaden's death in 1908 satisfies the stated composer death-date threshold, assuming the recorded composition is indeed his work and no later lyricist, translator, adapter or arranger holds a relevant composition contribution. The 1897 New York piano-vocal publication lists “Papus' Revenge Song” as a number in the opera, but the printed publication's copyright and arranger credits must be checked against the specific recorded arrangement.

## Taxonomic assessment

**Primary classification: Calculated Revenge (fictional dramatic representation).**

This classification is based on the work's explicit plot function and the named aria. It should not be upgraded to a historical example of Jewish-Roman retaliation or a record of collective grievance without analysis of the libretto. The wider operetta draws on the Bar Kokhba revolt and later Jewish national memory, but its plot is a nineteenth-century theatrical construction.

The Gesellschaft für Historische Tonträger handbook describes a date code stamped in the disc's mirror/runout, with month/day/year components, and says an example “E 8 C” means 8 May 1923. Another specialist reference gives the earlier Homokord sequence as January=A through December=M, with the final letter encoding year (V=1905 down to A=1925; W=1926). - The University of Surrey's page notes that Homophon discs can carry two embossed dates between label and groove, and that the company also manufactured records for other labels. A surviving disc must therefore be examined for label, matrix and both embossed dates; a stamper/pressing date is not automatically the recording date.

## Further verification — Homophon/Homokord matrix-date rule and LOC access route (2026-11-15)

A more authoritative technical reference now resolves part of the earlier dating-code uncertainty:

- The Gesellschaft für Historische Tonträger's *Diskographie-Handbuch* states that **all acoustic Homophon/Homokord records carry an alphanumeric recording-date code in the mirror/runout**. It gives the pattern as month letter + day numeral + year letter; its example “E 8 C” means 8 May 1923. The guide explains that the year sequence runs in reverse from V and that W was introduced for 1926. This is recording-date evidence, not merely a catalogue or pressing date. A physical Homokord/Homocord 1118 copy should therefore be inspected for this code before trying to date it from the catalogue number.
- The same handbook gives this corporate/label sequence: Homophon founded in 1905; renamed Homokord in 1907; company name returned to Homophon in 1912 while the principal label retained Homokord; label spelling changed to **Homocord in 1924**; electrical recording began 1 March 1926. This makes “Homocord 1118” potentially compatible with a 1924–25 acoustic recording, but does not prove it: the secondary Albis transcription may normalize the label spelling, and the matrix/date stamp remains absent. The earlier note's conflicting secondary changeover dates should be superseded by this more detailed specialist handbook while retaining the caveat that a primary disc check is decisive.
- Practical consequence: if an original Homocord-labelled 1118 disc is found and its runout code decodes to 1924 or 1925, that would strongly resolve the recording-date gate; if the code is W or later, it fails the project's pre-1926 master-fixation requirement. Do not mistake any separate stamper/pressing date for the recording date.
- The Library of Congress explains that its recorded-sound holdings are not comprehensively catalogued online, that title is the primary access point, and that its AVCMS collections database is internal to the Recorded Sound Research Center. The Heskes finding aid's blank AVCMS number therefore cannot be resolved by ordinary public catalogue searching alone. A targeted request to LOC Recorded Sound Reference Center should ask staff to check the Heskes collection inventory/AVCMS for “Papus,” “Bar Kochba,” “Papus Rache,” and any audio associated with the score/recording; this is a real archive lead, not evidence that such a recording is held.

Source: https://78opera.com/files/ARTIST_L.pdf (lines 7375–7381). This is a direct catalogue-number collision if both references mean the same Homokord catalogue series. It does not establish that Albis is wrong: a later reuse, omitted prefix/series, transcription error, or conflation remain possible. But “Homocord 1118” cannot presently identify the Papus disc uniquely.
- The specialist guide by Franz Lechleitner / Gesellschaft für Historische Tonträger explains that Homophon became Homokord in 1907, the company reverted to Homophon in 1912 while the label remained Homokord, and the label spelling changed to **Homocord in 1924**. It states that matrix number, catalogue number and an alphanumeric recording-date code are found on the label/runout; the date code is month-letter + day + year-letter, with W used for 1926. Example: E 8 C = 8 May 1923. Source: https://www.phonomuseum.at/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/diskographie_handbuch.pdf (pp./lines 2634–2656).
- Lotz-Verlag's Homocord Liliput discography separately corroborates the 1907 trademark dispute and postwar revival under Homocord, but is not evidence for this 1118 item: https://www.lotz-verlag.de/Online-Disco-Homocord-Liliput.html.

**Revised decision:** retain “Papus Rache” as **C — LEAD / identity unresolved**, not as an early recording or verified carrier. The Homocord 1118 entry may be an erroneous or incomplete catalogue reference. Do not assign the 1907 date from the colliding Homokord 1118 record to Papus; equally, do not infer a post-1923 date merely from Albis's modernized spelling. Required next evidence: original Papus disc label and runout photographs; exact catalogue prefix/number; matrix; date code; and an audio item demonstrably corresponding to that matrix. If the physical record cannot be found, ask Albis compiler Bernd Meyer-Rähnitz for the source of the 1118 entry and whether the number was copied from an original disc, a catalogue, or an earlier discography.

The **song-level evidence remains strong**: the 1897 Frankfurt score names “Papus' Revenge Song” as No. 5; the LOC Heskes finding aid lists a circa-1900s Russotta arrangement under the combined catalogue heading “Dinah's welcome to Jerusalem: Papus revenge song”; and the separate Beka 10872 “Der Bucher” recording remains unproven as that aria. None of these establishes that Homocord 1118 is the correct record number.

## Further check — Carl Gutentag's adjacent Homocord entry (2026-11-15)

A second Yiddish-music discography adds a potentially important but still unresolved clue:

- The online Abraham Goldfaden discography at YiddishMusic/Jewniverse lists **“Bucher” (Abraham Goldfaden), Carl Gutentag, Homocord M 11185, 1909**. Source: https://yiddishmusic.jewniverse.info/goldfadenavrom/ (entry at lines 14–18).
- Albis lists the separate “Bucher” entry as Homocord 11184 and “Papus Rache” as Homocord 1118, both by Carl Gutentag with orchestra. The YiddishMusic entry therefore differs by one digit from Albis's “Bucher” number, and independently supplies a 1909 date only for **Bucher**, not for Papus Rache.
- The 78opera catalogue/session listing has Homokord 1118 already assigned to “Die beiden Grenadiere” in 1907. Taken together, these sources make it increasingly likely that the Albis transcription is incomplete, normalized, or contains a catalogue-number error. It is possible that the intended Papus number has a missing prefix or digit, but there is no evidence yet to fill it in.
- The YiddishMusic site provides a downloadable archive for the Gutentag entry, but the link is a RAR archive and the public record as currently indexed does not provide a listenable stream or enough metadata to establish the Papus side. Do not claim to have heard it or equate the archive with “Papus Rache.”

**Interpretation:** 1909 is a promising date for a Carl Gutentag Goldfaden recording called “Bucher,” but it cannot be transferred to the Papus aria. The adjacency of “Bucher” and “Papus Rache” in Albis is not proof that they were paired on one disc or made in one session. Maintain the C lead and the explicit catalogue-identity warning. A useful next enquiry is to ask the Albis compiler for the original source/label behind both Gutentag entries and ask YiddishMusic/Jewniverse for the basis of the M 11185 / 1909 attribution and whether their Gutentag audio archive contains both sides.
