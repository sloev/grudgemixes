# Armenian recording lead — Mademoiselle Rosalie, “Pamp Vorodan” (Favorite Record, Constantinople, 1911)

**Status: B — PROBABLE CANDIDATE (provisional).** A surviving Favorite Record label image, an item-level collection record and an Armenian transcription identify a distinct pre-1926 Constantinople performance of the same vengeance song later recorded by Armenag Shah-Mouradian and Torcom Bézazian. The text's connection to Alishan's 1850 poem is strong. The actual Rosalie audio has not been located/listened to, and the precise date/matrix reading needs confirmation.

## Recording object

- **Performer:** Mademoiselle Rosalie (Rozali Benliyan; also Rosalie Benliyan), soprano with orchestra.
- **Label:** Favorite Record.
- **Catalogue number:** the photographed label appears to read **1-56211**.
- **Label title:** “Ermeni milli havassi” / “Pamp vorodan” (Armenian national melody / “Pamp Vorodan”).
- **Language:** Armenian, according to the collection record and label.
- **Recording location:** Constantinople, according to the item-level collection record.
- **Date:** CatalogIt collection record gives **release date 1911**. A handwritten date-like inscription on the label appears to read “6/2.11”; do not interpret it as a precise recording date without a better image or catalogue key.
- **Matrix/runout:** the runout inscription below the label appears to read **4456-1**; provisional visual reading only. Confirm from a high-resolution image and specialist matrix catalogue.
- **Format:** 78 rpm disc. The collection record says 78 rpm but labels the category “vinyl record”; carrier material has not been independently confirmed and should not be silently normalized to shellac.
- **Physical label evidence:** the CatalogIt item page supplies a high-resolution photograph showing Favorite Record branding, catalogue number, “Armenian,” soprano with orchestra, Rosalie, Constantinople and the title. This is a photographed artifact, not merely a title in a later song index.
- **Collection record:** MGK Collection, “Mademoiselle Rosalie, Pamp Vorodan/Oh Inch Anush,” created 2025-05-03, updated 2025-12-14: https://hub.catalogit.app/mgk-collection/folder/entry/mademoiselle-rosalie-pamp-vorodan-oh-inch-anush
- **Label image:** https://d8e7jbdw4fu0e.cloudfront.net/15232/75bed020-2866-11f0-b3aa-2d6b6a1423dc-vd7lgCY.lg-nt%402x.jpg

## Text and thematic fit

The collection record supplies an Armenian transcription for the Rosalie track. It includes the phrases:

- **Հայրենավրեժքդ հայկազունք** (*Hayrenavrezhk't Haykazunk*) — an appeal to the Armenian people as avengers of the homeland; exact morphology and idiomatic translation require specialist review.
- **Իզեն ի վրեժ** (*Izen i vrezh*) — “they go to vengeance / they set out to avenge” (provisional translation).

The same transcription appears on the separate 1917 Columbia item for Armenag Shah-Muradian, whose collection record identifies “Pamp Vorodan” and links a YouTube recording. This independent version supports the song-text identification, but it is not a substitute for listening to Rosalie's own Favorite side.

The song is the musical setting associated with Pietro Bianchini (1828–1905) of Ghevont Alishan's poem, published in San Lazzaro, Venice, in 1850. The poem's full title explicitly names vengeance: “Song and March of the Armenian Soldiers Under Vahan Mamikonian in the Field of Shavarshakan for Vengeance.” The poem recalls a later generation of Armenian soldiers avenging Vardan Mamikonian's defeat at Avarayr in 451. It is a historical-heroic literary commemoration, not eyewitness testimony about the fifth-century battle.

The CatalogIt Rosalie entry also identifies her as Rozali Benliyan and describes her as of mixed Greek and Bulgarian heritage rather than Armenian. If correct, this is a noteworthy instance of an Ottoman-era non-Armenian performer recording an Armenian-language national/revenge song. Treat the biographical identity as secondary collection metadata pending corroboration; do not infer ethnicity from the performance language.

1. **Mademoiselle Rosalie — Favorite Record 1-56211, Constantinople, 1911:** label photograph and collection record; runout/date inscription readings provisional; no audio yet located.
2. **Armenag Shah-Mouradian — Columbia E3459, New York, 1917:** separate physical label image, transcription and YouTube link on the MGK collection record: https://hub.catalogit.app/mgk-collection/folder/entry/armenag-shah-muradian-pamp-orodan-hayastan . Its label image is https://d8e7jbdw4fu0e.cloudfront.net/15232/603f3aa0-b6bf-11ef-818a-4dfb933f46eb-wyxsMXm.lg-nt%402x.jpg and linked recording https://youtu.be/0cE5g6U1ONU .
3. **Torcom Bézazian — Victor 69102, matrix B-18462 take 3, New York, 1916-10-11:** separate LOC National Jukebox item https://www.loc.gov/item/jukebox-19711/; LOC credits H. Sinanian as arranger. Do not assume its exact words from Rosalie or Shah-Mouradian until the Victor audio is transcribed.
4. **Torcom Bézazian — Columbia 1915:** separate matrix 39818-1 in the Recording Pioneers E-series discography; the relationship of its text and arrangement to these versions needs checking.

The 1911 Rosalie disc predates the 1916 Victor and 1917 Columbia issues, and should be treated as an independent early commercial recording, not merely a variant of the American diaspora records.

## Date and evidence assessment

- The item-level collection record gives 1911 as release date and Constantinople as recording place; the photographed Favorite label corroborates the performer, language, catalogue number and title. This is strong but secondary collection metadata, not a contemporary printed catalogue or company ledger.
- Recording Pioneers' catalogue index lists a Favorite Turkish/Armenian catalogue containing 1905, 1910 and January/February 1911 recordings, dated mid-1911. The original catalogue is a primary-source target; no item-level catalogue entry for 1-56211 has yet been transcribed. Index: https://www.recordingpioneers.com/CATALOGS-RP-2025.html (Favorite section).
- The apparent runout “4456-1” and handwritten “6/2.11” must be checked before asserting matrix/session chronology.
- Exact audio has not been located. The text transcription is associated with the item record, but no audio-to-transcript verification has occurred.
- Rights are **UNKNOWN / NOT CLEARED** for the sound recording, performance, transfer/digitization and intended territories. A public collection record or label image is not a reuse licence.

## Taxonomy

**Collective / historical revenge; patriotic grievance and mobilization.** The lyric explicitly calls for vengeance and arms, in the remembered context of an earlier defeat. It should not be called “spite” and should not be described as a contemporary documentary account of 451. The recording's role is to preserve a twentieth-century performance of a nineteenth-century Armenian poem and musical setting.
