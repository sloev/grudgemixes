## 1. “Φονιάς θα γίνω” (*Fonias tha gino*, “I will become a murderer”) — REJECTED: post-cutoff and not revenge on current text

The Virtual Kounadis Archive preserves several recordings under this title, but all located discographic versions are **1927 or 1929**, outside the project's 1926-01-01 cutoff:

| Gad Pol and Giorgos Savaris | Columbia England 18092 | 20652 | 1929, Athens | https://vmrebetiko.aegean.gr/item/?id=9652 |

The archive gives the composer and lyricist as **Panagiotis Vaindirlis** for the versions listed above. The 1927 Odeon label reportedly says “Δημώδες” (“folk”), but the archive's composer/lyricist attribution to Vaindirlis creates a provenance question; do not simply assume the folk label establishes traditional authorship.

### Text and thematic assessment

The 1929 Marika Papagika version has a full Greek lyric transcription. The singer addresses a woman as a “wicked witch,” says he cannot live without her, lists distress and bitterness, and declares:

- “Φονιάς θα γίνω / μα δε σ’ αφήνω” — “I will become a murderer, but I will not leave you.”
- “γιά θα σε πάρω, γιά θα χαθώ” — approximately “either I will take you [as mine], or I will be lost.”
- Later he threatens that anyone who provokes him will face him.

This is a jealous, possessive and coercive threat of violence in a romantic dispute. The available text does not describe a prior injury followed by calculated payback; it is not evidence of revenge as defined in this project. It may be relevant to a study of threat, possessiveness, jealousy or gendered coercion, but it is outside the recording-date cutoff in any case.

**Decision: REJECTED for this series** — all located masters postdate the cutoff; current lyrics read as possessive threat rather than retaliation/revenge. Preserve as a useful negative control, not as a candidate.

## 2. Chronological caution for Greek / rebetiko recordings

Ole L. Smith's note “The chronology of Rebetiko — a reconsideration of the evidence” warns that dates assigned to pre-war rebetiko recordings are often unreliable unless based on factual evidence. Therefore, use the Virtual Kounadis Archive's item-level dates as research leads, but seek matrix/session records, original catalogues, labels and contemporary trade notices before promoting an early date to VERIFIED.

The Kounadis archive's own historical overview notes that early recording terminology such as “rebetiko” was applied inconsistently by the record industry; recordings bearing that label in Constantinople from around 1912 include material that does not fit later bouzouki-centred expectations. Do not retroactively impose a narrow modern genre definition.
