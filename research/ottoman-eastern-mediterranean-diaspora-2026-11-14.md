# Ottoman, Eastern Mediterranean and diaspora recording routes — 2026-11-14

## 1. Oriental HMV matrix chronology

Hugo Strötbaum's *Dating Oriental Recordings of the Gramophone Company According to Matrix Number (1900–1919)* gives matrix ranges, session windows, locations and engineers for Turkish, Greek, Arabian, Hebrew, Sephardic, Armenian, Albanian, Serbian, Bulgarian, Kurdish and Laz recordings.

Examples in the chart:
- Constantinople, 28 May–June 1900: 7-inch matrices 1750–1917 A.
- Constantinople, January–February 1903: 7-inch 675–740 C; 10-inch (H) 465–545 z.
- Alexandria (tentative), February 1903: 7-inch 741–813 C; 10-inch 546–606 z.
- Constantinople, 28 April–9 May 1904: 7-inch 370–426 d; 10-inch (A) 605–700 e.
- Constantinople, late October–early November 1904: 7-inch 3495–3537 g; 10-inch (D) 2471–2561 h; 12-inch 343–373 i.

Source:
- Hugo Strötbaum, *Dating Oriental Recordings of the Gramophone Company According to Matrix Number (1900–1919)*: https://www.recordingpioneers.com/docs/Oriental-HMV-CHARTS-1900-1919.pdf

Use this as a dating and location aid, not as publication proof. Confirm each title and matrix in the Gramophone Company Discography and contemporary issue catalogues. The chart itself marks some locations as uncertain; retain that uncertainty.

## 2. Armenian and Ottoman diaspora 78s

Burcu Yıldız's 2024 article argues that commercial 78rpm records made by first-generation Armenian immigrants in the United States preserve performances and musical memories connected to Anatolia. The article highlights their value as early sound documents and as counternarratives to later national-music historiography. This is a strong reminder not to treat “Turkish” or “Ottoman” recording history as synonymous with recordings made inside the borders of the empire, nor to erase Armenian-language and other minority repertoires.

Source:
- Burcu Yıldız, “Transcultural Memory in the Study of Folk Music in Turkey: 78-rpm Records of the Ottoman American Diaspora” (2024): https://journals.uni-goettingen.de/wom/article/download/1868/1571?inline=1

Search diaspora labels, performer names and original-language catalogue spellings alongside Constantinople and Cairo sessions. For every item, distinguish the place of performance tradition, place of recording, language, label market and later national attribution.

## 3. Commercial and field recordings are different evidence classes

Nazan Maksudyan's 2024 article describes early field recording activity in the Ottoman Empire, including Paul Kretschmer's 1901 Lesbos trip and Felix von Luschan's 1902 Zincirli research. This is valuable historical context, but those ethnographic field recordings are not automatically commercial releases or commercially reusable masters.

Source:
- Nazan Maksudyan, “‘Sound-Writing’ Technologies and Early Field Recordings in the Ottoman Empire” (2024): https://dergipark.org.tr/en/pub/srtc/article/1449023

A separate project, *The Late-Ottoman Sound of Anatolia* (EAP1637), is digitising the Darülelhân archive of around 4,700 shellac discs and more than 600 unique recordings. However, its described folk-music expedition recordings date from 1926–29, so those sessions fall outside the present pre-1926 cutoff unless an individual disc is independently shown to be an earlier commercial issue.

Source:
- *The Late-Ottoman Sound of Anatolia* (EAP1637), project description: https://avesis.istanbul.edu.tr/proje/62c0fee9-ced7-4a87-b568-90f28e182ea6/the-late-ottoman-sound-of-anatolia-eap1637

## 4. Search protocol for grievance repertoire

Search original-script titles and catalogue spellings across Turkish, Ottoman Turkish, Armenian, Arabic, Greek, Ladino, Kurdish, Albanian and other relevant languages. Search not only direct words for revenge, but genre and narrative terms associated with feud, insult, betrayal, curse, lament, accusation, blood debt, compensation and settlement.

Do not treat a modern dictionary equivalent as proof of a song's meaning. Require language-specialist review of lyrics and performance context. Distinguish an operatic/theatrical revenge plot, a historical narrative song, a lament, a curse and a ritual settlement performance.
