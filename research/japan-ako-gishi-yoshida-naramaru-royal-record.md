# Japan lead: Yoshida Naramaru, *Akō Gishi-den Toiri* (Royal Record)

**Status: C — LEAD.** A specific SP disc is documented in an institutional publication and identified as held by the International Research Center for Japanese Studies (Nichibunken). Its catalogue number, issue date, matrix, exact performance text and audio access remain unresolved.

## Item identification

- **Title as printed:** 赤穂義士伝討入
- **Romanization:** *Akō Gishi-den Toiri*
- **Working translation:** “The Akō Retainers’ Raid” / “The Raid in the Akō Retainers’ Tale”
- **Performer:** Yoshida Naramaru (吉田奈良丸); the institutional publication does not specify which generation.
- **Label:** Royal Record (ロイヤルレコード)
- **Format:** SP record
- **Archive:** Nichibunken states that the disc is in its collection.
- **Catalogue number / matrix / recording date / recording location:** not supplied by the source.
- **Direct audio:** not yet located. Do not assume that the item is one of the playable files in Nichibunken’s online rōkyoku database.

## Primary institutional evidence

The International Research Center for Japanese Studies’ 2016 prospectus, in its section on the interdisciplinary study of Naniwabushi, reproduces/identifies an SP record:
- 「SPレコード 吉田奈良丸『赤穂義士伝討入』 ロイヤルレコード 日文研所蔵」

The same page separately identifies a Tōchūken Um右衛門 (桃中軒雲右衛門) SP record, *Ōishi Azamakudari*, on Lyrophone, also held by Nichibunken. These are separate objects and should not be conflated.

Source: International Research Center for Japanese Studies / National Institutes for the Humanities, *Prospectus 2016*, pp.16–17, research project “Interdisciplinary Research in the Formation and Transformation of Naniwabushi (Rōkyoku)”: https://www.nichibun.ac.jp/en/uploads/pdf/niyo_2016.pdf. The record identification appears on PDF p.17.

## Archive and access context

Nichibunken’s Rōkyoku SP Record Digital Archive describes a collection of 9,998 SP records donated by collector Morikawa Tsukasa in 2014. Its public database includes label images for all digitized records, but sound is publicly available only when copyright protection has expired; otherwise audio is restricted to on-site access. The current archive overview reports 2,237 audio titles and 5,534 label-image titles (as of February 2023). The *Toiri* record's presence in the 2016 institutional prospectus establishes a Nichibunken holding, but its inclusion in the online searchable subset and its audio status still need item-level confirmation.

## Thematic assessment

The title points to the raid by the Akō retainers, the climactic collective act of vengeance in the Chūshingura narrative. This is a strong thematic lead for **collective revenge / retaliatory justice in dramatic-historical storytelling**, not evidence that the recorded performance recounts the historical raid accurately. The 47 retainers’ revenge story is a cultural narrative with a long and shifting performance history; do not collapse the fictionalized stage/folk narrative, later national memory, and the historical events into one evidentiary category.

The institutional source identifies the performance title, but not the actual excerpt or lyrics. The precise episode, framing, and language of grievance must be checked against the disc and its performance text before promotion beyond C.

## Critical distinctions

- Keep this Royal Record item separate from the earlier lead for Yoshida Naramaru’s *Gishi no Uchiiri* / *Akō Gishiden Toiri* associated with Yamato On-ei and a 1914 catalogue/sale reference. Label and title forms may overlap, but no evidence yet proves these are the same issue or master.
- Keep it separate from Takamine Chikufu’s Nipponophone 15226–15227 (1924) *Gishi no Uchiiri*.
- Keep it separate from the Nipponophone *Akō Gishi-den* series attributed to Yoshida Naramaru, whose individual episode discs include *Tonochū Kinjō*, *Akagaki Genzo*, and others. A later catalogue record for an SP title is not a date for this Royal Record disc.
- A secondary local history reports that a Yoshida Naramaru *Akō Gishi-den* record released in 1910 sold very widely, but the source does not establish that this is the Royal Record item, and it may refer to the second-generation Naramaru. Do not attach the 1910 date or sales figures to this disc without label/catalogue corroboration.

## Discographical cross-check — multiple plausible issues, number conflict (2026-11-15)

A specialist Japanese SP discography page (78MUSIC, “Nipponophone / Royal / American”) separately lists **赤穂義士討入**, performer “大阪 吉田奈良丸,” catalogue number **1645**, under the shared label section. This is a plausible match to the Nichibunken Royal Record title, but the page’s merged label heading does not, by itself, prove that 1645 is the exact disc shown in the Nichibunken prospectus. The same discography lists other related Naramaru titles and series numbers, including *Akō Gishi-den Hazama Jūjirō* 1723–1724 and *Gishi no Toiri* / *Gishi no Hikiage* 15139-A/B (the latter assigned July 1923 in the catalogue table). These are distinct entries and must not be treated as the same recording.

A Kushiro City Museum / local collection survey independently lists **1649 (one-sided), *Akō Gishi-den*, Yoshida Naramaru, Royal**. This conflicts with 1645 as the plausible Royal catalogue number for *Akō Gishi Uchiiri*, and may refer to another title/issue or reflect a different catalogue transcription. It does not resolve the Nichibunken disc’s number. Treat **1645 and 1649 as competing catalogue leads**, not confirmed identity. Physical label images and matrix/runout comparison are needed.

Sources:
- 78MUSIC, “Nipponophone / Royal / American”: https://78music.jp/other5.html (also https://www.78music.jp/other5.html).
- Kushiro City Museum collection survey, “本館所蔵の音盤資料について (II) 一坂井徳治氏収集資料を中心に”: https://www.city.kushiro.lg.jp/_res/projects/default_project/_page_/001/018/314/8_1-30.pdf.

This new evidence makes the physical disc more traceable, but the date of the Royal 1645/1649 entries remains unestablished in these sources. Do not infer pre-1926 solely from the 1923 Nipponophone 15139 issue; it is a separate catalogue item. The candidate remains C pending exact label/matrix linkage and date evidence.

## Expanded Naramaru Akō repertory and an openly playable archive item (2026-11-15)

The Nichibunken / CiNii parent record BB17632622 groups seven separate Naramaru SP entries under *Akō Gishiden*: *Ōishi Kuranosuke Yamashina-gaeri*, *Kanzaki Yūgorō*, *Hazama Jūjirō* (including the lower part), *Nanbuzaka Yuki no Wakare*, *Tonochū Jinshō*, *Akagaki Genzō*, and *Ōtaka Gengō*. The parent record itself gives no dates and only says Nipponophone / SP / Japanese lyrics. Do not assume all seven items are on the same issue or from the same session. 78MUSIC provides a more granular number sequence: 1723–1724 *Hazama Jūjirō*; 1725–1728 *Kanzaki Yūgorō* (four parts); 1729 and 1732 *Ōishi Kuranosuke Yamashina-gaeri*; 1733–1734 *Amanoya Rihei* (upper/lower). The specialist listing notes 1723's reverse as Nipponophone 1724. These are catalogue-number associations, not recording dates.

### A directly accessible Naramaru archival audio record — *Akagaki Genzō Tokuri no Wakare*

Nichibunken's rōkyoku archive has a separate public record for *Akagaki Genzō Tokuri no Wakare* (赤垣源藏徳利の別れ):
- Performer: Yoshida Naramaru
- Label/number: Royal Record 1853
- One-sided disc; A-side 3:42
- Nichibunken archive record: https://kutsukake.nichibun.ac.jp/rsp/?reg=525671
- NCID BB17636022; call no. 05084; material ID 525671; Morikawa Tsukasa collection
- Archive metadata says playback condition poor, transfer made with laser turntable / phono equalizer / analogue-digital converter, and the sound is internet-accessible because the archive considers the copyright term expired.

This is a valuable proof that the archive sometimes exposes Naramaru audio directly, unlike the Royal 1723 record page that could not be retrieved in this research pass. But *Akagaki Genzō Tokuri no Wakare* is a farewell episode within the Akō narrative, not self-evidently a revenge song. It is a control / context recording until its actual text demonstrates grievance, vow, accusation or retaliation. Archive release does not equal commercial-use clearance, and the record has no issue date in its metadata.

### Separate, publicly accessible Naramaru item — *Kanzaki Azumakudari*

The same archive indexes *Kanzaki Azumakudari* (神崎東下り), performer Yoshida Naramaru, Nipponophone 15572–15574, three discs, archive record https://kutsukake.nichibun.ac.jp/rsp/?reg=525710, NCID BB17678984, call no. 05123, material ID 525710. It has six sides, each around 3:28–3:41, and an internet-playable audio interface. Do not conflate it with the earlier 1725–1728 four-part *Kanzaki Yūgorō* entry: the title, catalogue numbers and extent differ. Date and thematic content remain unverified.

### A 1912 printed performance witness

Nichibunken also digitizes *Yoshida Naramaru Kōen Naniwabushi Ohakoshū* (吉田奈良丸講演 浪花節十八番集), published by Naniwabushi Shinbunsha on 8 May 1912, 27 pages, NCID BB24153492, archive record https://kutsukake.nichibun.ac.jp/rsp/?reg=553460. Its catalogue notes list Naramaru's *Nanbuzaka Yuki no Wakare* and an excerpt from *Kanzaki Yūgorō Azumakudari* among the contents. This establishes that these works were part of Naramaru's documented repertoire by 1912, but it does **not** date any specific 1723/1725/15572 disc session or prove that the text of a particular disc matches the printed excerpt. The digitized page images returned 403 on attempted retrieval, so the actual printed text has not yet been transcribed.
