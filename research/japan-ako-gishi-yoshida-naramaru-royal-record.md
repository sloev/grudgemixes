# Japan lead: Yoshida Naramaru, *Akō Gishi-den Toiri* (Royal Record)

**Status: C — LEAD.** A specific SP disc is documented in an institutional publication and identified as held by the International Research Center for Japanese Studies (Nichibunken). Its catalogue number, issue date, matrix, exact performance text and audio access remain unresolved.

## Item identification

- **Title as printed:** 赤穂義士伝討入
- **Romanization:** *Akō Gishi-den Toiri*
- **Working translation:** “The Akō Retainers’ Raid” / “The Raid in the Akō Retainers’ Tale”
- **Performer:** Yoshida Naramaru (吉田奈良丸); the institutional publication does not specify which generation.
- **Label:** Royal Record (ロイヤルレコード)
- **Format:** SP record
- **Archive:** Nichibunken states that the disc is in its collection.
- **Catalogue number / matrix / recording date / recording location:** not supplied by the source.
- **Direct audio:** not yet located. Do not assume that the item is one of the playable files in Nichibunken’s online rōkyoku database.

## Thematic assessment

The title points to the raid by the Akō retainers, the climactic collective act of vengeance in the Chūshingura narrative. This is a strong thematic lead for **collective revenge / retaliatory justice in dramatic-historical storytelling**, not evidence that the recorded performance recounts the historical raid accurately. The 47 retainers’ revenge story is a cultural narrative with a long and shifting performance history; do not collapse the fictionalized stage/folk narrative, later national memory, and the historical events into one evidentiary category.

The institutional source identifies the performance title, but not the actual excerpt or lyrics. The precise episode, framing, and language of grievance must be checked against the disc and its performance text before promotion beyond C.

## Critical distinctions

- Keep this Royal Record item separate from the earlier lead for Yoshida Naramaru’s *Gishi no Uchiiri* / *Akō Gishiden Toiri* associated with Yamato On-ei and a 1914 catalogue/sale reference. Label and title forms may overlap, but no evidence yet proves these are the same issue or master.
- Keep it separate from Takamine Chikufu’s Nipponophone 15226–15227 (1924) *Gishi no Uchiiri*.
- Keep it separate from the Nipponophone *Akō Gishi-den* series attributed to Yoshida Naramaru, whose individual episode discs include *Tonochū Kinjō*, *Akagaki Genzo*, and others. A later catalogue record for an SP title is not a date for this Royal Record disc.
- A secondary local history reports that a Yoshida Naramaru *Akō Gishi-den* record released in 1910 sold very widely, but the source does not establish that this is the Royal Record item, and it may refer to the second-generation Naramaru. Do not attach the 1910 date or sales figures to this disc without label/catalogue corroboration.

## New pre-1926 text witness and separate Taihei *Denchū Ninjō* recordings (2026-11-15)

### The 1912 text gives explicit grievance and revenge language

A digitized NDL-contributed booklet, *Naniwabushi Meikyoku-sen* (浪花節名曲選), has a section headed *Denchū Ninjō* under Yoshida Naramaru / Yoshida Naramaru-ke Tsugiyū. Its bibliographic header gives M45 (Meiji 45 = 1912), and the scan's text is legible enough to establish the plot and thematic content (NDL scan hosted at Wikimedia Commons, PDF pp. approx. 2–3; searchable text at https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/NDL891714_%E5%90%89%E7%94%B0%E5%A5%88%E8%89%AF%E4%B8%B8%E3%83%BB%E6%9D%B1%E5%AE%B6%E6%A5%BD%E9%81%8A%E6%B5%AA%E8%8A%B1%E7%AF%80.pdf).

The passage titled 「殿中忍従」 says that Asano Naganori (Takumi-no-kami) endures because drawing a sword in the palace would bring the house's extinction and his own seppuku, and he fears for his retainers. At the corridor entrance he is insulted as a “dog samurai” and “inhuman” and accused of theft; his patience then breaks. He draws the short sword and attacks Ueno/Kira, but the blow fails to achieve his purpose. The text then says his forty-seven retainers avenge their lord on the snowbound night at Sengakuji. This is direct textual evidence for grievance, insult, a failed immediate retaliation, and a later collective revenge. It is a printed performance text, not a recording; it does not prove that a given SP contains this exact wording.

A second digitized Naramaru booklet, *Gishi no Issetsu — Naniwabushi Jūhachi-ban, Yuki no Maki* (義士の一節 浪花節十八番 雪の巻), includes the chapter sequence “Asano Takumi-no-kami's attack in the palace,” Tamura residence, seppuku, the Akō castle oath, and later “the raid on Kira's mansion” / “the fulfillment of the retainers' purpose.” It therefore documents the episode as part of a larger performance cycle, but the current scan metadata and date still need bibliographic verification before assigning a publication year. PDF: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/83/NDL891540_%E7%BE%A9%E5%A3%AB%E3%81%AE%E4%B8%80%E7%AF%80_-_%E6%B5%AA%E8%8A%B1%E7%AF%80%E5%8D%81%E5%85%AB%E7%95%AA_%E9%9B%AA%E3%81%AE%E5%B7%BB_part2.pdf.

## 1651 chronology materially narrowed by label-family classification (2026-11-15)

A close reading of the section headings on 78MUSIC's Nipponophone/Royal catalogue changes the chronology assessment for no. 1651. It is not merely listed in a generic Nipponophone list: it appears under the specific heading **ROYAL RECORD — red-label eagle mark / NIPPONOPHONE** (ローヤル(ROYAL RECORD) - 赤レーベル鷲印 / NIPPONOPHONE). The entry is *Akō Gishiden: Denchū Ninjō*, Osaka Yoshida Naramaru, no. 1651. The same section includes 1645 *Akō Gishi Toiri*, 1646–1648 *Ōtaka Gengō*, 1649–1650 *Akagaki Genzō*, and 1653–1654 *Nanbuzaka Yuki no Wakare*.

78MUSIC's company/label history describes Royal as an early label, initially one-sided, and says it was discontinued in Taishō 4 (1915). It distinguishes the later Nipponophone 15000 series, introduced April 1923, and the Nipponophone 1–9000 sequence. This provides a potentially decisive terminus: if the catalogue's Royal red-label eagle classification for 1651 is correct and applies to the actual surviving disc, it should be a pre-1916 issue, comfortably before the project's 1926 cutoff. But this is still secondary discographical classification, not a dated contemporary advertisement or physical label inspection. 78MUSIC does not print a release month/year for 1651, and the row itself has no matrix.

**Revised status:** 1651 moves from undated C lead to **B — PROBABLE chronology**, conditional on the catalogue's Royal label classification being accurate. The thematic side is independently strong from the 1912 Naramaru texts. It is not A/verified until a surviving label image or physical disc confirms the Royal red-eagle label and the audio/text is inspected. Do not claim an exact year. The 1912 anthology establishes repertoire, not recording date.

## National Diet Library catalogue routes for resolving Royal 1645 / 1649 (2026-11-16)

The National Diet Library's Research Navi page, updated 12 November 2026, identifies several concrete reference tools for dating and identifying Japanese SP records. This is a more actionable route than broad label-history inference.

### 1. Company sales catalogues — early Royal label variants

The NDL's prewar record-company sales-catalogue holdings list includes the Japanese Gramophone Company / Nipponophone group and explicitly names its associated labels **Universal, American, Royal, Symphony and Momotarō**. It identifies two 1912 company catalogues:

- **YM2-M2337** — 新音譜追加目録 明治45年3月5日売出 (New sound-score supplement, offered for sale 5 March 1912), noted as containing the red-eagle label (赤鷲印).
- **YM2-M2339** — 新音譜 明治45年5月 (New sound-score catalogue, May 1912), noted as containing Universal (blue paper / hemisphere mark), American (black paper / eagle mark), **Royal black (black paper / lion mark)**, **Royal red (red paper / red-eagle mark)**, and Symphony (red paper / angel mark).

The NDL list marks these two catalogue copies with (*) meaning the NDL holds a copy version rather than the original; the list does not mark them as digitally accessible. The existence of distinct black and red Royal label types is significant: a label photograph of 1645 or 1649 could be compared against these label families. The specialist 78MUSIC table describes Royal 1645 as “正面鷲” (front-facing eagle), while the Kushiro survey's 1649 entry is described as a one-sided Royal disc. This is a promising comparison clue, not a secure match: the icon descriptions may not use the same taxonomy, and neither item has been matched to a surviving label image in this research pass.

The NDL's same company chronology states that the Royal, Symphony, American, Universal and Globe one-sided labels were withdrawn in the 1915 transition, when the company recalled its single-sided records and standardised on Nipponophone (eagle mark) double-sided discs. This supports a broad pre-1915 context for an original one-sided Royal disc, but it does not date catalogue number 1645 or 1649 to 1912, nor prove that either appears in the May 1912 catalogue.

### 2. Best performance-discography reference — Engei Record Hatsuibai Mokuroku

The NDL's Research Navi recommends Kurata Yoshihiro / National Theatre Research Office, 演芸レコード発売目録 (Engei Record Hatsuibai Mokuroku, 1990; Entertainment Materials Series 4; NDL call no. KD1-E21). It covers Japanese domestic entertainment SP releases from **1909 to 1945**, including rōkyoku, and is arranged by release order. The NDL says it records release date, title/subject, performer, author and record company/number. This is the strongest single next reference to check for the exact 1645/1649 titles and issue month.

Important limitation: a secondary library report on the book says its entries were compiled principally from advertisements in 16 newspapers and 14 magazines, with company monthly catalogues and flyers used where available, and that the book's data were not checked against physical discs. Treat it as a strong published discographical index, not a substitute for label/matrix inspection.

The book is listed as NDL in-library-only access; CiNii lists copies at about 30 libraries. The National Diet Library's current Research Navi page also identifies the 2003 SP Record 60,000 Songs General Catalogue (昭和館監修『SPレコード60,000曲総目録』) as a company-organised title/number/performer index, although it does not provide issue dates. For content/lyric clues, it points to the 1996–97 Taishō-period SP records: performance, lyrics and language facsimile set, which covers records released from December 1913 to August 1922 and therefore may be too late for a 1912 issue.

## 2026-11-17 — 78MUSIC table-structure cross-check: Royal 1645 versus Nipponophone 1649
