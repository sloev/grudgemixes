## Screened lead: “Franky & Johnny” — Gene Greene, with Charley Straight

**Status: REJECTED for the current composition cutoff; retain as a historical research lead only.**

- Performer/title: Gene Greene (credited as “Mr. Gene Greene (the Rag Time King)”), piano accompaniment by Charley Straight; “Franky & Johnny.”
- Date/location: reported as recorded in London in December 1912 while the American performers were touring Britain. This date/location is currently supported by discographical secondary sources, not yet by a primary session ledger.
- Label/catalog: Pathé 5370. The Pathé catalog listing independently associates the title, performer and issue number, but gives an approximate date of 1913.
- Matrix/take: not established.
- Direct approved-archive audio file: not located in this pass. A commercial Archeophone release/stream or a secondary-site player is not an acceptable substitute for the requested raw archival transfer.
- Composition: the 1912 “Frankie and Johnny” sheet music credits Leighton Bros. and Ren Shields. Ren Shields died in 1913. However, Bert Leighton (one of the credited Leighton Brothers) died in 1964, so the supplied rule that every credited composer/lyricist must have died before 1956 is not met. The precise division of music/lyrics and the relationship to earlier “Frankie and Albert” / “He Done Me Wrong” versions also require careful bibliographical treatment.
- Taxonomy: the narrative includes betrayal, quarrel, lethal retaliation and subsequent punishment/legal process. It should not be simplified to “revenge”: Frankie’s shooting of Johnny is immediate lethal retaliation in the ballad narrative, while the song's framing and moral judgment vary by version. It is also not evidence that the historical event occurred exactly as the popular lyrics describe.
- Lyrics/context: the 1912 popular sheet-music version and the earlier St. Louis ballad tradition are distinct documentary layers. A historical account of the 1899 St. Louis killing, later ballad variants, and the commercial vaudeville adaptation must be distinguished. Do not lift a lyric excerpt from a modern transcription without checking it against the 1912 printed sheet music or the audio.

## Useful archive pathways confirmed

- UCSB Cylinder Audio Archive contains more than 15,000 digitized cylinders, including global commercial recordings. Its collection description confirms streaming and downloads, but each individual item's date, commercial status, and rights still need verification: https://www.library.ucsb.edu/special-collections/performing-arts/cylinders
- UCSB's Discography of American Historical Recordings (DAHR) contains extensive master-level discographical data and streams many historical recordings. Its coverage is strongest for specified US company catalogs; it is not a global catalog: https://www.library.ucsb.edu/special-collections/performing-arts/victor
- The Library of Congress Songs of America guides identify traditional-ballad families and later field recordings. These are useful for tracing song histories, but a later field recording cannot satisfy the master cutoff: https://www.loc.gov/collections/songs-of-america/articles-and-essays/musical-styles/traditional/traditional-ballads/
- Library of Congress explains that some National Jukebox recordings are streams of masters under agreement with Sony. Availability to stream is not proof that a transfer is raw, unedited, or independently reusable for a commercial compilation.

## Follow-up under revised methodology — performed-text witness (2026-11-15)

A 2025 University of Oregon Folklore and Public Culture thesis by Natalie Rose Ivy supplies a transcription specifically headed **Gene Greene, “Frankie and Johnny”**, citing the *Before the Big Bang* track note for the 1912 recording. The thesis reproduces the recorded version's narrative: Frankie learns that Johnny is with Alice Bly, finds them together, draws a revolver and shoots him; the closing verses include the wounded man's response and a courtroom/judicial consequence. This supports the earlier classification as **immediate lethal retaliation following betrayal**, not calculated/delayed revenge. The transcription also shows that the recorded version is not simply interchangeable with the separate Leighton Brothers sheet-music text reproduced in the same thesis appendix.

Important evidence boundary: this is a modern transcription associated with a commercial reissue/track note, not a direct transcription made from a documented original Pathé 5370 carrier in this research pass. It materially strengthens thematic linkage but does not settle matrix/take, exact issue/master provenance, or archival transfer status. The 1912 London recording and c.1913 Pathé 5370 issue remain supported by specialist discographical sources; the exact original carrier/audio and rights still need direct verification. Retain **B — PROBABLE**, not A.

The thesis also gives useful contextual caution: it discusses the ballad's circulation and the racialized/gendered framing of Frankie Baker, and distinguishes the song's evolving popular versions from the 1899 St. Louis event. Do not describe the commercial ballad as a transparent documentary account of the historical killing.
