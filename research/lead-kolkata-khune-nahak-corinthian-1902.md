# Kolkata theatre recording lead — “Khune Nahak: Gana Gana Gana” (1902)

Status: **LEAD ONLY / NOT A CANDIDATE / NOT CLEARED**.

## Recording lead

The Madan Theatres Research Group lists “Khune Nahak: Gana Gana Gana” as CHARM catalogue work **14561**, among early recordings by the Corinthian Theatre Chorus. Its account states that the chorus recordings were made in November 1902 at the Corinthian Theatre, Kolkata, during a Gramophone Company recording tour led by Frederick William Gaisberg and coordinated locally by John Watson Hawd. The Corinthian was then a major venue for Hindu, Urdu and Gujarati plays and classical music.

## Why it merits follow-up

“Khune Nahak” appears to be a Persianate/Urdu expression associated with unjust killing or wrongful bloodshed. This makes it a potentially relevant dramatic title, but **does not establish that the recorded song describes revenge**. The listed work title and song incipit (“Gana Gana Gana”) do not supply the lyrics or dramatic context. Do not classify it as revenge, retaliation or grievance without the text and play synopsis.

The same Corinthian recording list includes:
- “Dil Farosh: Arre Koi Aan Mile Ya Sajni” (14562)
- “Haseen Chatra: Tajan Ke Raj Aaye” (14563)
- “Haseen Chatra: Chand Mehtaab Doobi Gande Talab” (14564)
- “Laila: Bharke Pilade Sharab Yaar Sakiya” (14568)
- “Khudadad: Song” (14570)

These are not candidates from title evidence alone.

## Wider Bengali theatre sources

- The British Library Endangered Archives Programme catalogue lists *Br̥hat thiyēṭār saṅgīt / Thiyēṭār saṅgīt* (1920), a four-volume collection of popular songs from contemporary Bengali theatre, shelfmark EAP261/1/5/36: https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002724773 .
- The same archive documents earlier Bengali theatre songbooks, including *Thiyēṭār saṅgīt* (19th century–early 20th century): https://searcharchives.bl.uk/?f%5Brelated_places_ssim%5D%5B%5D=India%2C+Asia&f%5Brelated_subjects_ssim%5D%5B%5D=Bengali+Culture&page=3&per_page=100&sort=hierarchy .
- The University of Chicago's *Gramophone Celebrities* article reports that the 1902 Gramophone expedition recorded over 500 items, around 200 connected with Classic Theatre artists; it notes that songs, instrumental selections and even spoken dramatic scenes were recorded. It lists “Khune Nahak” among Classic Theatre's plays. This is a secondary lead; corroborate with contemporary playbills, scripts and catalogues: https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/e/su/southasia/record-news/trn2006printable.pdf .
- India Foundation for the Arts documents a project to collect and annotate Bengali theatre 78s from 1900–1930 and notes that many early theatre discs have not survived or are difficult to locate: https://indiaifa.org/node/451 .

## Open questions / verification plan

1. Open CHARM work 14561 and extract its complete metadata: performer credit, language, label, catalogue number, matrix, recording date/location, issue date and surviving audio link.
2. Find the original Gramophone Company catalogue and label/runout image; distinguish recording date (reported November 1902) from issue/publication date.
3. Identify the play *Khune Nahak*: author, original language, plot, printed script/playbill and any later summaries. Verify whether the song “Gana Gana Gana” is actually from that play and what it says.
4. Search the 1920 theatre songbook and related Bengali/Urdu theatre songbooks for an exact lyric or textual match. A play title alone is not enough.
5. Establish lyricist/composer or documented traditional provenance and death dates.
6. Locate an original-shellac archival transfer and determine whether it is an unedited transfer. Assess composition, master, transfer and repository rights separately, including EU/UK territories.
