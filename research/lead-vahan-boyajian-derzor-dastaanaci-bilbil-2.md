# Candidate research lead: Vahan Boyajian, “Derzor Dasdaanaci” (Bilbil 2)

**Status: LEAD — strong thematic relevance; date/audio/rights gates open.**

## Identification
- Performer: Vahan Boyajian (also transliterated Boyacıyan).
- Title: “Derzor Dasdaanaci” / “Derzor Destanaci”; translated in a later compilation as “The Ballad-Singer of Der Zor.”
- Label/catalogue: Bilbil Record, No. 2.
- Format: 78 rpm shellac, label image reproduced in Burcu Yıldız (2024).
- Language: Turkish, according to Yıldız.
- Recording location: Armenian-American independent-label context in the United States; exact studio/city not established by the sources reviewed.
- Exact recording date: unresolved. Yıldız identifies the performer and label image but does not provide an item-specific date for this disc. A commercial retrospective compilation is titled *A Diamond Ring: Armenian-American Independent Releases ca. 1922–27*, but that broad span does not establish that Bilbil 2 was fixed before 1926.

## Why it matters
Yıldız identifies “Derzor Dasdaanaci” as a popular song with many versions by different singers. She describes it as narrating the long and painful deportation of Armenians to Deir ez-Zor in Syria, and as a lament that symbolically depicts violence committed around 1915. She interprets the song as a site of memory and “postmemory,” carrying familial and cultural memory across generations and borders.

**Provisional taxonomy:** Collective grievance testimony / lament / diasporic memory. Not revenge, not retaliation, and not automatically protest in the narrow political sense. The song's memorial function is evidenced by Yıldız's analysis; do not claim it is a verbatim eyewitness report or a complete historical account.

## Open gates
- **Date/publication:** Find Bilbil's original catalogue, advertisement, label/runout evidence, or a dated company/collector ledger. Do not infer an individual date from the compilation's ca. 1922–27 span.
- **Audio:** Locate the exact original disc transfer and document the transfer chain, processing, source disc and whether the file is unedited. The Canary Records reissue is for discovery/listening only, not the requested raw archival master.
- **Composition:** Establish whether this is traditional, who authored/adapted the text and melody, and whether the recorded version contains later-added verses. The fact that the song has many versions makes version-specific authorship important.
- **Lyrics:** Obtain and verify the full Turkish lyrics from the exact Bilbil 2 performance, with literal and idiomatic translations. Yıldız's article establishes the song's broad subject but the full transcription was not located in this pass.
- **Rights:** Assess original phonogram/master ownership, performer/producer rights, transfer rights, archive terms, and target territories separately. No commercial clearance established.

## Editorial caution
The title “Derzor” points to Deir ez-Zor, but the song must be treated as a culturally situated lament and memory practice, not a neutral historical chronicle. Keep the Turkish-language performance, Armenian diaspora context, Harput/Anatolian repertoire connections and U.S. recording context distinct. Do not collapse performer identity, language, subject community and recording geography into one national label.

## Recommendation
Keep this as a high-priority LEAD for the collective-grievance / memory sequence, but do not count it toward a verified batch until an exact pre-1926 issue/fixation date, the original performance's lyrics, a qualifying archival transfer and commercial rights evidence are secured.
