# Global early-recording research map

**Purpose:** prevent the project from defaulting to European and North American repertoires. This is a living discovery map for early recordings across local markets, languages and archives. It is not a list of already eligible grudge songs.

## Search principles

1. Search all regions from the first pass, in parallel. Search by language and script as well as translated concepts.
2. Include local labels, local performers, overseas recording expeditions and export catalogues. Do not assume “world music” means only ethnographic field recordings.
3. Separate commercial releases from field recordings. A field recording may be historically valuable but does not automatically meet the project's intended commercial-recording brief.
4. Search for multiple grievance mechanisms, not just the English words “revenge” and “grudge”: insult, betrayal, feud, debt, dispossession, punishment, protest, lament, vengeance, curse, shame and remembered injustice. Use local terms and ask regional specialists to validate semantic equivalence.
5. Keep exact dates, matrix/take, issue and audio provenance separate. A digitized recording is not necessarily an unedited transfer or commercially reusable.
6. Do not force regional quotas or infer a community's identity from an archive's broad geographic tag.

## High-value global gateways and what they contain

### South Asia

- **Early Gramophone Company India sessions:** the 1902–03 Far Eastern expedition recorded 554 masters in Calcutta in November 1902, then continued through Tokyo, Shanghai, Hong Kong, Singapore, Bangkok and Rangoon. Keep recording/fixation date distinct from issue date: masters were shipped to Hannover for pressing and finished records returned to Asian markets months later.
- **Kinnear's Indian discographies:** *The Gramophone Company's First Indian Recordings, 1899–1908* (1994) and *The Gramophone Company's Indian Recordings, 1908 to 1910* (2000; matrix-serial numerical catalogue) are key specialist references for early Indian sessions and matrix verification.
- **UCSB Ethnic and Vernacular Music guide:** useful chronology and bibliography for India and related regional repertoires.
- **Archive of Indian Music:** modern preservation/listening route for early commercial recordings, theatre and multilingual material; verify source-disc provenance, transfer chain and reuse terms before treating any restoration as eligible audio.
- **Young India / EAP190:** useful archive of catalogues and digitised recordings, but the label operated 1935–1955 and is therefore outside the current pre-1926 cutoff.
- Search local labels and regional repertoires, not only Gramophone/HMV; use original scripts and specialist language review. Do not infer a recording's place, language or issue date from the expedition itinerary alone.

- **UCSB Benno Häupl Collection:** 1,200 78s from India, Pakistan and Ceylon/Sri Lanka among its international commercial holdings; further material from Afghanistan/Pashtun and other regions.
- **Gramophone Company Discography / EMI Archive Trust:** searchable company-wide database of over 430,000 recordings, including global catalogues and recording sessions from 1898 onward. Search language, performer, title, matrix, issue, date and location, not only artist name.
- **Early Indian commercial recording history:** the Gramophone era in India begins in 1902; research literature documents the early recording campaigns and later local-label catalogues. Use local-language title searches and specialist discographies.

### Middle East and North Africa

- **Oriental HMV matrix chart (1900–1919):** gives session windows, locations and matrix ranges for Turkish, Greek, Arabian, Hebrew, Sephardic, Armenian, Albanian, Serbian, Bulgarian, Kurdish and Laz recordings. Use it to narrow sessions, then verify exact titles/issues in the Gramophone Company Discography and contemporary catalogues. Some locations are explicitly uncertain.
- **Ottoman/Armenian diaspora 78s:** commercial recordings made by first-generation Armenian immigrants in the United States preserve Anatolian musical memory and minority-language repertoires. Search diaspora labels and original-script spellings, and keep recording location, language and later national attribution distinct.
- **Ottoman field recordings:** 1901 Lesbos and 1902 Zincirli expeditions are important sound-history leads, but field recordings are a separate evidence class and are not automatically commercially reusable or within the commercial-release brief.
- **Late-Ottoman Sound of Anatolia (EAP1637):** digitising Darülelhân shellac holdings; the described folk-music expedition recordings date 1926–29 and are outside the current pre-1926 cutoff unless an individual item has independent earlier issue evidence.

- **AMAR Foundation:** 9,000+ 78rpm discs and a public listening room with selected recordings; its archive documents Gramophone/Zonophone campaigns in Egypt from 1903 and later regional and local labels including Baidaphon, Orphéon-Blumenthal and Méchian.
- **Harvard Loeb Music Library Arabic 78 Collection:** nearly 600 catalogued Arab and Arab-American recordings, roughly 1903 onward; a subset of early pre-1923 recordings is streamable/downloadable through its digital collection. Harvard's own dating notes stress that exact recording dates are often difficult to establish.
- **UCSB Häupl holdings:** substantial Turkish, Arabic and Maghrebi commercial 78 collections.
- Search local repertoires and labels as well as transnational companies; include Arabic, Ottoman Turkish, Persian, Kurdish, Armenian, Greek, Hebrew, Sephardic and Amazigh language variants where appropriate.

### East and Southeast Asia
- **Berliner Phonogramm-Archiv / Beka expedition:** the 1905–06 Beka expedition travelled from Constantinople to Yokohama and made over 1,600 recordings for markets including Turkey, Egypt, India, Burma, Singapore, Dutch East Indies, China and Japan. Surviving samples in the Hornbostel collection have been digitized and catalogued.
- **Japan — Nichibunken Rōkyoku SP Record Digital Archive:** nearly 10,000 Naniwabushi/rōkyoku SP records, with disc images and selected playable audio; the corpus spans the Meiji 30s to Showa 30s. It is a high-value search route for Akō Gishi / Chūshingura and other revenge-story recordings. Audio is public only where copyright has expired; otherwise on-site access applies. https://www.nichibun.ac.jp/ja/db/category/rokyoku/ ; archive/about: https://kutsukake.nichibun.ac.jp/rsp/about/
- **Japan — Gishi/Naniwabushi recording chronology:** Henry D. Smith dates the first commercial Gishi-tale record to August 1910. A Japanese academic article identifies a 1910 Naniwabushi *Akō Gishiden* recording with reported sales exceeding 200,000 by 1915; a local-history source attributes it to Yoshida Naramaru II, still needing primary discographic corroboration. The National Theatre's Cultural Digital Library documents Naramaru's 1910 printed *Yamato Sakura Gishi no Omokage*. A separate reference dates Tōchūken Kumoemon's *Gishiden* record to 1912. Distinguish these from Naramaru's 1914 Yamato On-ei and 1923 Nipponophone issues; do not collapse recordings without matrix/master comparison. Sources: https://www.columbia.edu/~hds2/pdf/2006_Ako_Gishi_media_and_politics.pdf ; https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jasi/23/0/23_0_108/_pdf ; https://www2.ntj.jac.go.jp/dglib/contents/learn/edc21/hirogari/wagei/wa2.html ; https://japanknowledge.com/introduction/keyword.html?i=972
- **Korea — Alice Fletcher cylinders (1896):** six wax cylinders recorded in Washington, D.C., with Korean students are preserved by the Library of Congress. These are field/academic recordings, not commercial issues; do not confuse them with the unverified surviving-master claim for the 1899 Gamunjeong event. https://blogs.loc.gov/folklife/2023/04/the-alice-fletcher-korean-cylinder-recordings-a-small-part-of-d-c-history/
- **China / Cantonese:** the Chinese Historical Society of Southern California has a 250-disc collection, mostly Cantonese opera 10-inch shellac discs, with a dated 1902 Victor item and later Golden Star titles. Early G&T/Edison commercial Cantonese recording campaigns began in 1903. https://chssc.libraryhost.com/repositories/2/resources/21 ; Yung Sai-shing, 2001: https://www.cuhk.edu.hk/ics/journal/articles/v41p511.pdf
- **UCSB Häupl Collection:** includes Chinese, Afghan/Pashtun and other Asian commercial discs.
- Search Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Burmese, Thai/Siamese, Malay, Javanese and other local-language labels and titles; verify script/transliteration variants.

### Latin America and Caribbean
- **Berliner Phonogramm-Archiv / Ibero-Amerikanisches Institut:** digitized historical recordings made in Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Mexico and Peru between 1905 and 1934; only recordings fixed/published before the project cutoff can qualify.
- **UCSB Häupl and Mexican/Cuban cylinder holdings:** commercial 78s and early cylinders with regional material.
- **Frontera Collection / Arhoolie Foundation:** a very large archive of Latin American and Mexican-American recordings, with earliest recordings around 1905, including early corridos and narrative ballads. Search pre-1926 entries and establish exact issue/master rather than relying on collection-level date.
- Corridos are a promising form for grievances, betrayal, violence, punishment and public memory, but each song's narrative and historical basis must be checked individually.
- **Mexico — Edison cylinder corridos (1904):** *Edison Phonograph Monthly* lists a Mexico City commercial campaign including Rafael Herrera Robinson's two-part *Ignacio Parra* (Edison 18512–18513), *Heraclio Bernal* (18515), and *Jesús Leal* (18518), alongside other corridos. These are wax-cylinder leads, not shellac 78s; check UCSB's Mexican-cylinder holdings and the Thomas Edison National Historical Park cylinder catalogue for surviving originals and item-level transfers. The Bernal/Parra narratives concern late-1880s outlaws/rebels and may encode grievance or resistance, but exact 1904 lyrics and revenge mechanisms are unverified. Research note: `research/latin-american-corridos-screening-2026-11-15.md`. Sources: https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-All-Audio/Edison-Phonograph/Edison-Phonograph-Monthly-1904-Vol-2.pdf ; https://www.library.ucsb.edu/news/early-20th-c-mexican-cuban-cylinders-added-cylinder-audio-archive ; https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/historyculture/search-the-catalog.htm

### Sub-Saharan Africa and Indian Ocean
- **UCSB Häupl and African 78rpm collections:** substantial commercial African holdings, including regional labels and export issues.
- **British Library Sound and Vision:** commercial releases, field recordings and rare items issued only in countries of origin.
- Search local labels, colonial-era export catalogues and performers recorded in regional studios; document colonial collecting and marketing context rather than treating archive categories as neutral.

### Europe, diasporas and cross-border markets
- **Phonobase / Gallica:** early commercial discs and cylinders, including French and European labels and Italian Fonotipia recordings.
- **Yale Berliner Gramophone Disc Collection:** over 700 digitized early Berliner discs, 1887–1912.
- **Lemberg / Gimpel's Yiddish Theatre (1904–1913):** the 78rpm record industry preserved hundreds of Yiddish theatre songs from Gimpel's permanent Lemberg troupe; sources identify about 800 titles recorded by Favorite, Beka and Gramophone, with roughly 354 of 560 so far identified recorded in Lemberg. Use Michael Aylward's research and the *Wandering Stars* disc/label reissue documentation as discovery aids, then verify each original label/matrix and exact lyric. https://forgottengalicia.com/shund-on-shellac-or-gimpels-theatre-lemberg/ ; https://doi.org/10.3828/liverpool/9781906764739.003.0008 ; https://www.iemj.org/en/wandering-stars-songs-from-gimpels-lemberg-yiddish-theater-1906-1910/
- **YIVO Max and Frieda Weinstein Sound Archive:** 15,000+ recordings, including 78rpm discs, cylinders, spoken word and record catalogues; earliest item a wax cylinder dated 1900. YIVO online material terms are often personal/research/education only; do not assume commercial clearance. https://www.yivo.org/Sound
- **UCSB Häupl / Ackerman holdings:** Eastern European, Balkan, Greek, Albanian, Armenian, Yiddish/cantorial, Ukrainian/Lemko and related repertories.
- **Ottoman American / Armenian diaspora 78s:** commercial discs can preserve early repertoires and counternarratives that are missed by national sound histories. Do not treat diaspora material as a simple proxy for recordings made in the place of origin.

## Immediate research assignments

1. **Arabic / Arab-American:** use Harvard's downloadable pre-1923 subset and AMAR's listening room. Search Arabic words and song-form terms related to vengeance, feud, betrayal, debt, insult, curse and lament; identify exact tracks, not just artists.
2. **South Asia:** search the Gramophone Company Discography by early regional-language titles, performers and matrix ranges; inspect HMV/Gramophone India catalogues and private-collection digitization projects.
3. **Turkey / Ottoman / Eastern Mediterranean:** search matrix-based sessionographies and local-language title variants; distinguish Istanbul, Cairo, Alexandria and diaspora sessions.
4. **Latin America:** search Frontera and national archives for pre-1926 corridos and narrative songs; establish exact issue and publication date.
5. **East / Southeast Asia:** mine Beka/Hornbostel and local national archives; look for commercial releases, not only field recordings.
6. **Africa / Indian Ocean:** inspect UCSB commercial holdings and British Library country-of-origin releases; search local labels and languages.

Each researcher should log search terms, archive coverage, inaccessible/restricted items and negative findings, so the team can see which regions have actually been investigated.
