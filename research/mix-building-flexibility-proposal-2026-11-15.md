# Mix-building flexibility proposal — 2026-11-15

**Status: PROPOSAL FOR EDITORIAL APPROVAL; does not alter the historical core cutoff or evidence standards.**

## The problem

The current project scope is already wider than revenge: it includes punishment, protest, defiance, accusation, counter-speech, curses, feuds and collective grievance. But the practical habit of building mixes around only the most explicit revenge tracks has made the candidate pool feel much narrower. Early recording archives also preserve fragments, stage excerpts, ballad variants and context-rich performances rather than complete narrative arcs.

The solution should be to loosen **mix architecture**, not source criticism.

## Proposed three-lane track model

1. **Core tracks — direct grievance response.** The recording itself clearly expresses revenge, retaliation, punishment, refusal, protest, accusation, curse, feud or another response to a perceived wrong.
2. **Narrative tracks — grievance story.** The song narrates a betrayal, feud, injustice, punishment or revenge cycle; the recorded excerpt may capture only one stage, provided the relationship to the wider story is documented.
3. **Context tracks — the social memory around the grievance.** A lament, aftermath, commemorative song, heroic outlaw ballad, mourning song or loyalty/fidelity episode may be used when it illuminates the same grievance cycle. It must be labelled as context, not falsely described as a revenge song.

A track may have more than one role. The liner note must state what is actually heard and what belongs only to the larger story.

## Practical mix recipe (trial)

For a 10–12 track mix, aim for 4–6 core tracks, 3–4 narrative tracks and 1–3 context tracks. This is a flexible target, not a quota. If evidence supports only 2 core tracks, do not pad the mix with weak material; instead make a shorter, focused episode or a two-part sequence.

Use a wider set of episode titles and mechanisms: **The Betrayal**, **The Public Accusation**, **The Insult**, **The Debt**, **The Feud**, **The Sentence**, **The Protest**, **The Inherited Account**, alongside **The Revenge**. These are editorial doors into the archive, not claims that every track describes revenge.

## Date flexibility without category confusion

- Keep the historical core **pre-1926**.
- Permit a separately labelled **afterlife / extension track** recorded 1926–1930 only when it materially strengthens a mix and no eligible earlier version is available or the later version is itself historically significant. Mark it OUTSIDE CORE and never count it as a core early recording.
- Later ethnographic/field recordings may document an older tradition or variant, but belong in a clearly marked **tradition witness** role, not the early commercial-recording core. Check consent, cultural protocols, repository restrictions and reuse rights carefully.
- A later recording of an older song is not proof that the exact words or melody existed in the earlier period; date each layer separately.

## Evidence flexibility — what may and may not change

**May loosen for mix inclusion:** the recording need not contain the whole revenge plot; a track can be included for its narrative stage or contextual role; an access derivative can be auditioned provisionally while transfer provenance remains open; an item with uncertain exact day/year may remain active if the pre-1926 range is secure.

**Must not loosen:** exact recording identity; honest date classification; original-language/text checks before strong thematic claims; distinction between history, folklore, literary invention and stage convention; clear labelling of unverified audio; rights and repository review before publication or commercial reuse. Candidate A/B/C grades remain evidence grades, not aesthetic ratings.

## Test case: Bentley Ball, “Jesse James” (Columbia A3085)

The 1910 Lomax text repeatedly calls Robert Ford a coward and accuses him of betraying Jesse's hospitality. The 1920 Smithsonian object record and FAU Columbia A3085 catalogue identify Bentley Ball's recording; a Commons access derivative also exists, although its metadata gives 1919 rather than the Smithsonian's 1920. This is not a direct revenge act. It is an unusually clear **public accusation / betrayal counter-speech** track, suitable for a mix called *The Public Answer* or *The Betrayal*. The candidate dossier keeps the date conflict, matrix gap, audio provenance and rights open.

This is a useful test of the new architecture: it broadens the musical sequence without weakening the factual description.

## Approval / review questions

- Should the 1926–1930 extension lane be permitted, or should all such tracks remain outside the mix sequence?
- Is the 4–6 / 3–4 / 1–3 recipe useful, or should the mix be shorter and evidence-led?
- Which episode title/mechanism should be tested first: *The Public Answer* or *The Betrayal*?
