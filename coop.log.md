# Cooperative research log

Purpose: coordinate archival research, discographical verification, rights review, and editorial work for the *spitebites.party* early-recordings series.

## Editorial authority and tone
- The companion reference is `sloev/grudgebook`. Its governing premise is a serious, straight-faced biological, historical, anthropological and practical study of grievances. The central conceit is the existence of a rigorous archive devoted to resentment; the writing itself remains rigorous.
- Do not moralize, prescribe forgiveness, ridicule grudge-holders, or use forced puns.
- Distinguish **spite** (deliberately imposing a cost on another while accepting a cost to oneself), **retaliation** (a response to a prior injury), **punishment** (a sanction imposed for a perceived or established violation), **revenge** (payback, often calculated or delayed), **resentment** (a durable response to perceived wrong), and a **grudge** (the maintained record/stance of an unresolved grievance). Do not infer motive from a song title alone.
- Retain local genres, terms, language names and social contexts. Explain limits of translation. Never treat a genre or custom as uniform across a country or timeless.
- Separate documented events, folklore, literary representation, commercial stage convention, and later popular myth.

## Coordination protocol
1. Read this file and the relevant notes in `sloev/grudgebook` before contributing.
2. Append a dated entry identifying agent, task, files created/edited, sources consulted, status, unresolved questions and handoff.
3. Prefer additive files and candidate records. Do not overwrite another contributor's work or silently upgrade a provisional candidate to verified.
4. Each candidate must have a stable ID and a source trail. Record negative findings and reasons for rejection.
5. Mark evidence explicitly: **VERIFIED**, **PARTIALLY VERIFIED**, **LEAD**, or **REJECTED**. A missing date, matrix, composition author/death date, direct audio object, or rights basis must remain visible.
6. Editorial decisions and final commercial clearance require human review. No agent may promise worldwide clearance from a single date or repository label.

## Candidate acceptance and rights gates
- Initial master cutoff: published or fixed before 1926-01-01. Record both publication and fixation dates where known; do not conflate them.
- Composition gate: traditional with documented provenance, or author/composer/lyricist death before 1956-01-01. Identify translators, arrangers and later adaptations separately.
- Audio gate: link to the exact archival audio object/file and document whether it is an unedited transfer of the original cylinder/disc. A repository page is not proof that a file is raw or that the transfer carries no separate rights.
- Rights gate: assess master, composition, performer/producer/phonogram rights, archival digitization/transfer terms, repository terms, and territory. US and EU status must be assessed separately; publication/fixation status and national rules matter.
- Approved first-pass repositories: Internet Archive / Great 78 Project; Library of Congress / National Jukebox; UCSB Cylinder Audio Archive; Europeana; national sound archives with explicit rights statements.
- Never describe a recording as globally public domain or commercially cleared unless the relevant rights and territories have actually been checked. Use **NOT CLEARED** where appropriate.

## Required candidate record
- Candidate ID; status; proposed mix/sequence.
- Performer(s); title as catalogued; original language; genre/local term.
- Recording date and basis; recording location and basis.
- Label; catalog number; matrix/take number.
- Direct audio URL (not merely a search page); archive item URL; exact file/format where available.
- Publication date evidence; fixation date evidence; composition author/traditional status and death date evidence.
- Rights notes by territory and by layer (master, composition, transfer, repository).
- Taxonomic mechanism and evidence in lyrics/performance/context.
- Original-language excerpt, verified transcription, literal translation, idiomatic translation, and source for each. Do not invent lyrics or translate from an unverified secondary transcription.
- Liner note (150–250 words) in the book's editorial voice.
- Open questions, confidence, and decision.

## Taxonomy (non-exclusive; explain the evidence)
- Calculated revenge / delayed payback
- Immediate retaliation
- Punishment / sanction
- Public shaming, insult or counter-insult
- Spite / costly harm
- Maintained personal grievance
- Intergenerational or collective grievance
- Protest / grievance testimony (not automatically revenge)
- Betrayal / lament (not automatically a grudge)

## Working series architecture
A geographically and linguistically varied sequence, not a country-by-country checklist. Sequence by mechanism and sound-world while preserving local context. Possible volumes/episodes: The Insult; The Debt; The Betrayal; The Feud; The Public Answer; The Inherited Account. These are provisional editorial groupings, not claims about local genres.

## Research queue
- Search by local vocabulary and genre as well as English keywords.
- Prioritize recordings with legible catalog metadata and a direct downloadable archive file.
- Search the same title across catalogs to triangulate performer, matrix, label, date and lyric variants.
- Do not force a geographic quota when the evidence is weak. Report gaps honestly.
- Build a separate rights/clearance register before commercial release.
