A mixtape about what happens when someone in power decides a song is dangerous.

It opens in Cairo in 1919, where a fruit-seller's cry carried a banned man's name. Then come a samba that teased the police chief and a raï singer her own new state took off the air. Next, music bootlegged onto hospital X-rays, and a wartime cantata that survived only on tape. Two Baltic bands follow: one whose recordings the radio destroyed and the band itself kept, and one banned for a train its fans wrecked. It ends in London in 2019, with the first song a British court punished someone for performing.

Each time the music changes carrier, code or country. It does not go away.
