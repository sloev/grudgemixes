Vahan Boyajian’s “Derzor Dasdaanaci” is a Turkish-language lament about the deportation of Armenians to Deir ez-Zor in Syria during the violence of 1915. Boyajian recorded it around 1925 on Bilbil Record 2, one of the small Armenian-American labels that filled the gap after the major companies stopped making new Armenian-language records.

The disc is more than a song preserved by immigrants. Musicologist Burcu Yıldız describes it as a site of memory: the melody and words carried the story across generations and borders. Boyajian was born in Hüseynik, near Harput, and later founded Bilbil. The recording was made in the New York area, where Armenian musicians built their own tiny record industry. A shellac disc becomes a portable memorial, with a voice carrying a catastrophe that official histories often tried to silence.
