The Ballad of Hicks Carmichael retells the 1888 Knoxville murder trial and the public anger surrounding it. Folk-song collectors Edward Capers Kirkland and Mary Neal Kirkland made the first known recording in 1937; the recording later appeared on *The Kirkland Recordings* (Tennessee Folklore Society TFS-106, 1984). Legal historian William Davenport Mercer argues that the ballad is more than a reflection of the case: it works as a parallel performance of popular justice, preserving community response to a trial conducted under intense public pressure. The surviving descriptions do not identify the singer, so this post treats it as an anonymous archival performance rather than inventing an artist. It is a rare Appalachian ballad in which a community’s version of justice outlives the court record.
