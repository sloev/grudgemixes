In 1888, Edison’s London agent Colonel George Gouraud staged demonstrations of the new phonograph. One of the first recordings was made at the Handel Festival in Crystal Palace. His colleague Hugh DeCoursey Hamilton put the machine in the press balcony, about a hundred yards from the performers, and captured a chorus of roughly four thousand voices singing from Handel’s *Israel in Egypt*.

There were no microphones or modern amplification to rescue the experiment. It was a horn, a mechanical recording surface and a huge choir at a distance. The Library of Congress calls it probably the largest number of participants captured on a single recording in the National Recording Registry. The result is less a tidy concert document than a spectacularly ambitious early attempt to make a crowd stay in a groove forever.
