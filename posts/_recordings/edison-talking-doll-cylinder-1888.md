The doll itself has disappeared. What survives is a tiny, warped tin cylinder ring, made in 1888 for an experimental talking doll from Edison’s company. Inside the doll, the cylinder was meant to play a woman singing “Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star”. The recording was made by an anonymous employee; it may be the earliest surviving commercial recording and the earliest known recording of a woman’s voice.

The little ring was found in 1967 in the desk of Edison’s secretary, where it had sat for nearly eighty years. It was badly damaged and could not be played with an ordinary stylus. In 2011, researchers at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory used three-dimensional surface scanning to recover the sound. The result is not a polished nursery record. It is a tiny mechanical ghost: a voice from 1888 that was almost thrown away with a failed toy.
