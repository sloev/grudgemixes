George W. Johnson was the first African American to make commercial phonograph records, beginning in 1890. His best-known number, “The Laughing Song”, was a hit in the 1890s and was recorded again and again for different companies. The performance is built around a gruff baritone, a ragtime-tinged piano part and Johnson’s bursts of laughter. It is catchy, odd and hard to forget.

This particular version is not the first issue: it is a ten-inch Victor disc, catalogue 583, recorded in Philadelphia on 5 May 1903, matrix 583, take M-12. Johnson’s career was shaped by a recording industry that often sold racist caricature. The Library of Congress notes how unusually free his performances were of the forced dialect and minstrel-style exaggeration that marked much contemporary material. Listen closely to the laugh: it is part of the composition, not a sound effect pasted on afterwards.
