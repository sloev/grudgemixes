The Canary Records title gloss is “I Am Darkened.” Armenian lexical sources confirm that *խաւարել / խավարել* can mean to darken, become gloomy, or be overwhelmed by sorrow, but this is only a lexical clue, not a translation of the song. The Panosian project includes translated song texts and historical notes, but the full lyric of this item has not yet been independently checked in this research pass.

**Performer:** Zabelle Panosian, soprano, with orchestra.

**Title:** “Khavarel Yem” (publisher's English gloss: “I Am Darkened”).

**Original label / catalogue:** Columbia E4010.

**Matrix / take:** 84458-1 is listed for “Khavarel Yem” in the Columbia E-Series discography. Canary Records' later digital reissue contains two performances labelled take 1 (2:49) and take 2 (3:18); do not assume take 2 is the same matrix or issue without …

**Recording date/location:** June 1918, New York, per Columbia E-Series discography and the Armenian Mirror-Spectator's reproduction of the original Columbia label.

**Issue:** Columbia E4010; the Mirror-Spectator identifies the original record and dates it June 1918.

**Exact later access copy:** Canary Records, *I Am Servant of Your Voice: March 1917–June 1918*, digital album. Bandcamp lists “Khavarel Yem (I Am Darkened) take 1” (track 8, 2:49) and take 2 (track 13, 3:18), with streaming and high-quality MP3/FLAC …


Zabelle Panosian was one of the best-known Armenian-American singers of the 1910s, appearing at concerts and relief events during the Armenian Genocide. Columbia recorded her repeatedly between 1917 and 1918; “Khavarel Yem” was issued in June 1918 on E4010, matrix 84458-1. Canary Records’ reissue preserves two takes, and the listening link opens take 1. The publisher glosses the title as “I Am Darkened.” Armenian lexical sources support meanings around darkening, gloom and sorrow, but that is not a verified translation of the full lyric, so the song’s precise narrative remains open. The image shown is the later reissue artwork, not the original Columbia label.
