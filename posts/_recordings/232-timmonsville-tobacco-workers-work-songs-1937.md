Edwin C. Kirkland and Mary Kirkland made field recordings in the American South between 1937 and 1939. A 1985 survey of folk-music releases notes one exceptional 1937 item: Black women singing while working in the tobacco industry in Timmonsville, South Carolina. The recording had not previously been published; it later appeared on the Tennessee Folklore Society LP *The Kirkland Recordings* (TFS-106), alongside other regional singers and instrumentalists. The published description does not give the women’s names or a song title, so this entry leaves both unidentified rather than filling the gaps with guesses. Its value is the rare trace of women’s work music in a rural industry, preserved outside commercial recording culture.
