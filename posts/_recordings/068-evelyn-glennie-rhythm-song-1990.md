Evelyn Glennie’s first solo album, *Rhythm Song*, appeared on RCA Victor in April 1990. Its repertoire jumps from Scott Joplin’s “Maple Leaf Rag” and Rimsky-Korsakov’s “Flight of the Bumble Bee” to Glennie’s own “A Little Prayer”. The National Philharmonic Orchestra appears alongside her tuned percussion, but the record’s centre of gravity is unmistakably the soloist: marimba, rhythm and a startling range of colour.

Glennie is profoundly deaf. As a teenager she learned to distinguish pitches through vibration, associating different sounds with sensations in her body. She often performs barefoot so she can feel more of the floor’s vibration. The point is not that she somehow plays despite being deaf. This is a record by a percussionist who developed her own way of listening, and who made the instrument family large enough to fill the room.
