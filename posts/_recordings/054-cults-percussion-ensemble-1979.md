The name sounds like a sinister secret society. It is actually a suburb of Aberdeen. In 1976 percussion teacher Ron Forbes gathered pupils from schools around Aberdeenshire and wrote music for them. Their average age was fourteen. After concerts and tours, they made one privately pressed LP in 1979.

The record is an unlikely little universe of glockenspiels, xylophones, vibraphones, marimba and timpani. Pieces such as “Baia”, “Circles” and “Polymers” move between bright, chiming minimalism and something almost dreamlike. One of the players was thirteen-year-old Evelyn Glennie, who had become profoundly deaf by this point and would go on to become a celebrated solo percussionist. This was her first record, not a later tribute to her: a rare, self-contained school ensemble LP with a future star tucked inside it.
