# Mix concept — The Public Answer: accusation, memory, revenge

**Status: PILOT CONCEPT / NOT A NUMBERED MIX / NOT RELEASE-READY.** This outline tests the proposed core–narrative–context model. It deliberately uses a short three-track form rather than padding to a conventional album length.

## Editorial premise

A grievance becomes public when it is sung. This miniature sequence moves from a historical-heroic call for collective vengeance, through a literary performance that incites revenge, to a ballad that publicly condemns a betrayer. The tracks do not all describe the same moral mechanism: that difference is the point.

## Provisional sequence

### 1. Torcom Bézazian — “Pamp Vorodan” / “Bam Porotan” (Victor 69102, 1916)
- **Role:** CORE — collective / inherited vengeance rhetoric.
- **Evidence:** Existing candidate GM-AM-001; Library of Congress item gives matrix B-18462 take 3, 11 October 1916, New York. Poem published in 1850; exact recorded text and access-transfer provenance still need comparison.
- **Context:** The Armenian poem recalls the defeat of Vardan Mamikonian and calls later Armenian fighters to avenge it. It is commemorative and mobilizing, not eyewitness testimony of the fifth-century event.

### 2. Malcolm McEachern — “Revenge! Timotheus Cries” (Vocalion X-9560, 1924/25)
- **Role:** CORE / NARRATIVE — retaliatory incitement through performance.
- **Evidence:** Existing research lead `lead-handel-alexanders-feast-revenge-timotheus.md`; 1926 UK Vocalion catalogue retrospectively lists the issue among records issued through November 1925; sessionography suggests October 1924. Matrices 03724 / 03725 are reported but need physical-copy verification; exact archive audio not located.
- **Context:** Handel's 1736 setting of Dryden/Hamilton presents Timotheus's music as summoning the Furies and the Greek dead, preparing Alexander's revenge on Persepolis. This is literary/stage representation, not proof of Alexander's actual motive.

### 3. Bentley Ball — “Jesse James” (Columbia A3085, 1919/1920)
- **Role:** NARRATIVE / CONTEXT — public accusation, counter-insult and betrayal grievance; **not direct revenge**.
- **Evidence:** New candidate GM-US-001. *The Talking Machine World* (15 Aug 1919) contemporaneously lists A3085 and “Jesse James,” verifying publication by that date. Recording/fixation remains uncertain: Smithsonian gives 1920, while Commons Ogg metadata says 15 May 1919. FAU item confirms A3085 and both tracks; matrix 90038 is the other side, so Jesse James side matrix remains open. The Commons Ogg has no documented source-carrier/transfer chain. Lomax 1910 and Bentley Ball's own *The Song-a-logue of America* (p. 31) both print “dirty little coward” and accuse Robert Ford of betraying Jesse's hospitality. Ball's printed text does not establish the exact recorded wording.
- **Context:** The ballad's moral answer is public condemnation of Ford. It also romanticizes Jesse James; preserve the contradiction and avoid repeating the song's claims as historical fact.

## Why this works under the flexible model

- It combines direct revenge rhetoric, revenge incitement and accusation without labelling all three as revenge.
- Each track's role is stated plainly; the context track is not required to contain a retaliatory act.
- The sequence is short because only three distinct, plausible recordings are currently available. It can remain a compact episode or grow when further eligible tracks are verified.
