# GM-AM-011 — Tashjian Brothers, “Tashnagtsagan Khump” (Columbia E665-4962, 1910)

## Identification

- Performer: Tashjian Brothers — Mardiros, Nishan and Levon Der Sarkis.
- Title as printed/transliterated by the Armenian Museum: “Tashnagtsagan Khump”; Armenian title: Դաշնակցական խումբ; modern transliteration commonly “Dashnaktsakan Khumb” / “Dashnagtsagan Khoumb”.
- Label/catalogue: Columbia E665-4962.
- Session: New York, autumn 1910; released 1911, according to the Armenian Museum's specialist discography.
- Language: Armenian.
- The Museum describes the title as “The Regiment of Armenian Revolutionary Federation” and includes it among the brothers' patriotic songs and odes to fedayi freedom fighters.

## Text and thematic evidence

A useful later Armenian-language text witness appears in *Armenian National Songs*, second edition (Armenian Youth Federation of America, 1962), contents p. 1 and song text/commentary around pp. 7–8 (PDF pages/printed pagination should be checked against the physical edition). It includes a song titled “Dashnagtsagan Khoumb” and describes the song as telling of Armenian Revolutionary Federation fedayi bands fighting in Sassoun under Antranik's leadership.

The OCR-rendered Armenian transliteration includes references to “Khembabednere” (group leaders), “Fedayi,” Antranik, and a line rendered “Jamanag e verej loudzel” (approximately, “It is time to take revenge”). The OCR around the lyrics is visibly corrupted and the adjacent Armenian script is not reliably aligned with every transliterated line. Do not quote this as a verified transcription or treat the 1962 printing as proof that every stanza was sung on the 1910 Columbia side.

This is a strong song-family lead for collective resistance, revolutionary memory and retaliatory justice, but the precise Columbia performance must be compared with the later printed text. Political framing should identify the Armenian Revolutionary Federation context and the Sassoun resistance tradition rather than flattening the song into generic “nationalism.”
