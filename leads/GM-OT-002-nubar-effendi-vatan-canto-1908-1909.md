## Lyrics and translation

The archive publishes Turkish lyrics and an English translation. The refrain reads:

“Hürriyet, adalet, müsavat, millet / Yaşasın vatan”

The archive translates this as:

“Freedom, justice, equality, nation / Long live the homeland.”

Other lines call citizens to rise and rejoice, praise the Ottoman army, and say that “our night has turned into day.”

The archive translation is a useful guide, but Turkish-language review is needed for period-specific meanings of “hürriyet,” “adalet,” “müsavat” and “millet.”

## Historical and thematic assessment

The recording is dated 1908–1909 in Constantinople and celebrates the constitutional-era language of freedom, justice, equality and nation. It belongs alongside the 1908 “Marche Huriet” as evidence of the musical rhetoric circulating around the Young Turk / constitutional revolution.

Unlike “Marche Huriet,” however, this text does not explicitly describe oppression, informers, bondage or a specific grievance. It is primarily celebratory and patriotic. Do not upgrade it to a core revenge, accusation or protest track solely because it uses the words freedom and justice.

The song praises the army and Ottoman homeland. Preserve that political orientation; do not assume it represents all communities or political factions in the empire, and do not collapse the 1908 constitutional moment into the later history of the Committee of Union and Progress.
