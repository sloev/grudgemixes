**Region:** Armenian diaspora / New York  
**Status:** B — exact Columbia issue and song title confirmed by Armenian Museum; no exact audio transcription yet  
**Proposed role:** Collective resistance / refusal of silence  
**Retrieval:** RESEARCH TARGET — request original disc and authorized audio from Armenian Museum

## Recording identity

- Performer: Mardiros Der Sarkis Tashjian (solo; distinct from the later Tashjian Brothers performance).
- Title: “Himi El Lrenk” (Armenian title; transliteration varies; roughly “Should We Still Remain Silent?”).
- Label/catalogue: Columbia E529-4100.
- Session: New York, 1909.
- The Armenian Museum's discography identifies this as a single-sided record paired in its listing with “Marseillaise”; the museum notes that at least one disc was pressed as a single-sided record featuring this song.
- Primary specialist source: Armenian Museum of America, “The Incomplete Story of Mardiros Der Sarkis Tashjian & His Brothers”: https://armenian-museum.squarespace.com/sound-archive/tashjian-story
- The same museum article lists a separate 1910 Tashjian Brothers recording “Himi El Lrenk” on Columbia E668/4953. These are two distinct performances and issues; do not merge them.

## Text and thematic assessment

Armenian online songbooks attribute a song under this title to Raphael Patkanian's nineteenth-century poem “The Death of Vardan Mamikonian.” A published song-family text asks whether Armenians should remain silent while their land and arms are taken, chains are forged, and the community is driven from its homeland; it ends by rejecting silence and calling for resistance.

Sources for song-family text, not yet verified against the 1909 master:
- Yerkaran song index: https://yerkaran.org/index/
- Armenian text witness: https://ergir.am/himi_el_lrenq/
- English translation witness: https://tert.nla.am/archive/NLA%20TERT/Armenian%20weekly/2014/31_ocr.pdf

The song-family content is a strong match for collective grievance and refusal of silence. However, do not assume the 1909 Columbia side contains the full modern printed text or the same stanza order. The exact master must be transcribed.

## 2026-11-18 research audit — 1909 solo version

The Armenian Museum of America’s expanded Tashjian discography identifies Mardiros Der Sarkis Tashjian’s **Himi El Lrenk** as Columbia **E529-4100**, a **single-sided** 1909 issue. The same article distinguishes this from the brothers’ 1910 trio issue **E668-4953**. The museum dates Mardiros’s first six-song session to the early summer of 1909; it does not supply an exact day or a matrix/take number for E529-4100. The current museum page displays a label scan for the 1910 trio issue, but I did not locate a corresponding scan for E529-4100 in the pages checked. Thus the 1909 issue identity is supported by the museum’s researched discography, not by a newly inspected 1909 label image.

The museum article discusses the Adana massacres of April 1909 and a Boston Armenian protest organized by a committee that included Nishan and Rev. Sarkis Tashjian. It places Mardiros’s early-summer 1909 recording session in that wider moment, but does not establish that **Himi El Lrenk** itself was commissioned for the protest or that the disc was sold for relief. The article explicitly presents the fundraising idea as a conjecture; it also says there is no evidence of a concert or public singing performance by Mardiros. I therefore retain the Adana connection as contextual chronology only, not as a proven purpose for this recording.

The song-family text witness in this dossier remains separate from the exact 1909 take. No audio, matrix/take, label scan, full recorded text or performance comparison was obtained. The 1910 trio recording is a separate performance and must not be merged with this 1909 solo issue.

**Access and rights:** the museum’s discography is publicly viewable. No audio audition, download, physical inspection, museum contact, permission request or rights clearance was performed. The museum’s digitization acknowledgement does not establish reuse permission. Recording and composition rights remain unresolved.

**Next exact action:** locate a primary E529-4100 label or Columbia matrix ledger, obtain an authorized transfer, and compare the recorded Armenian text against the printed song-family witness. Retain B evidence and RESEARCHED-OPEN status until that comparison and rights/access questions are addressed.
