## Archive/listening lead

The Library of Congress Pete Welding Collection finding aid lists “Woman's Suffrage” by George Reneau on an archival tape, item AFC 2011/053: SR439, at 00:28:14. The tape is described as “Hillbilly 78s, #7,” with a 1:05:22 analog mono reel. This is a collection-level listing of a dub/compilation, not a direct original-carrier transfer or a direct-download URL. Consult the finding aid and LOC access policy before requesting or using the item: https://findingaids.loc.gov/exist_collections/ead3pdf/afc/2021/af021010.pdf

The LOC's Women's Suffrage guide documents that the era's recorded and popular music included advocacy and public performance in support of enfranchisement, but that general context does not establish Reneau's position: https://guides.loc.gov/womens-suffrage-mbrs

## Thematic assessment

The title identifies the political issue, not the singer's stance. The performance could be advocacy, ridicule, anti-suffrage satire, comic role-play, or something more ambivalent. Do not infer opposition from the performer, genre, title or the era's gender politics. Do not classify this as protest, grievance or counter-protest until the actual lyrics and performance are inspected.

The contrast with Pauline Russell Browne's 1913 *Woman's Suffrage Songs* is useful background only: Browne's printed collection explicitly includes “Taxation without representation,” “Persistence in the cause of right,” and other suffrage advocacy songs. It is not a text witness for Reneau's recording: https://www.loc.gov/item/2017562124/
