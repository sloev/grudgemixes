# GM-MX-002 — “Ignacio Parra” (parts 1–2), Rafael Herrera Robinson, Edison two-minute cylinders (1904)

**Project:** Grudge Mixes  
**Research status:** RESEARCHED-OPEN (2026-11-18)  
**Evidence grade:** C — qualified lead; not promoted to a main candidate  
**Date assessment:** the March 1904 Edison trade issue lists the two catalogue entries, establishing commercial catalogue presence by 1904. The exact session day is not known; the Frontera Collection’s specialist history dates the cylinder recordings to 1904.  
**Counting unit:** one two-part corrido/cylinder sequence. Edison catalogue numbers **18512** and **18513** are two parts of one work, not two independent leads.

## Identity and catalogue evidence

The March 1904 issue of *Edison Phonograph Monthly* (Vol. II, no. 1; available online as Wendell Moore’s 1976 “exact reproduction”) contains a section headed “Seventy-One Mexican Records.” It says the records were made in Mexico City under the direction of the National Phonograph Company’s recording department. Under “Baritone Solos by Sr. R. Herra Robinson,” it lists:

- **18512** — “IGNACIO PARRA,” 1a. Parte, “Corrido. Cancion Popular.”
- **18513** — “IGNACIO PARRA,” 2a. Parte, “Corrido. Cancion Popular.”

The contemporary catalogue spelling is **R. Herra Robinson**. The Frontera Collection article identifies the singer as Rafael Herrera Robinson and states that the two-part “Ignacio Parra” corrido was recorded in Mexico City in 1904 on Edison cylinders. The 2025 peer-reviewed chapter “El corrido en México: historiografía e instituciones” independently confirms that the two cylinder catalogue numbers were 18512 and 18513 and attributes both parts to Herrera Robinson. The Edison numbers are catalogue record numbers, not verified matrix numbers.

## Thematic qualification and limits

The Frontera Collection’s 2017 specialist history describes “Ignacio Parra” and “Heraclio Bernal” as early corridos about rebels active in the late 1880s during the Díaz dictatorship. It reports that Herrera later re-recorded many early cylinder titles for Victor and Columbia but did not reprise these two rebel ballads; the article relays corrido scholar James Nicolopulos’s interpretation that political pressure and a “seditious undercurrent” explain the omission. This supports a **probable defiance/resistance** connection and justifies a research lead.

It does **not** establish the exact 1904 recorded stanzas, narrator, grievance, or any specific act of revenge. No transcription of the 1904 “Ignacio Parra” cylinder was located. The lead therefore remains evidence grade C and is not admitted as a confirmed track or playlist item. Do not describe it as a revenge song unless the exact text supports that classification.

## Version and deduplication controls

- This is distinct from baseline **GM-MX-001**, Trio Nava’s 1921 “Heraclio Bernal” 78: different title, performer, date and carrier.
- The 1904 Edison parts 18512 and 18513 are one two-part corrido, not two leads.
- A later “Ignacio Parra” recording by **Los Alegres de Terán** appears in the 1996 *The Mexican Revolution* compilation/finding aid. It is a separate later performance and must not be substituted for the 1904 Herrera Robinson cylinders.
- The 1996 compilation’s Rafael Herrera Robinson cylinder transfer is **“Jesús Leal,”** not “Ignacio Parra.” Do not transfer the “Jesús Leal” Edison numbers or audio to this lead.

## Provenance, access and rights

The 1904 catalogue entry establishes that the two parts were listed for commercial sale, but I did not locate an item-level surviving cylinder, authorized transfer, original label image, matrix ledger or current custody record for the 1904 “Ignacio Parra.” The Frontera and Smithsonian pages checked document later recordings/compilation context, not the original 1904 carrier. No audio was auditioned, downloaded or copied; no archive was contacted; no rights or reuse permission were established. The 1904 composition/arrangement and any surviving cylinder-transfer rights remain unresolved.

## Next exact action

Search the Edison two-minute cylinder catalogues and Mexican/National Sound Archive holdings for **18512** and **18513**; seek a carrier-level record or authorized transfer; obtain a full Spanish transcription of the 1904 performance; compare it with later versions only after the 1904 text is independently established. Reassess C evidence and core thematic status only after that work.
