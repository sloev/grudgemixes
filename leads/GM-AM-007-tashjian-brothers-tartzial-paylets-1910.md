# GM-AM-007 — Tashjian Brothers, “Tartzial Paylets” (Columbia E658/4964, 1910)

**Region:** Armenian diaspora recording of a song associated with Sassoun / Western Armenia  
**Status:** C — exact performer/title/catalogue association documented; a strong same-title song text exists in a regional collection, but the text has not been matched to the Columbia performance  
**Proposed role:** Collective resistance / retaliation / armed defence  
**Retrieval:** RESEARCH TARGET — request Columbia E658/4964 audio and label/runout evidence from the Armenian Museum

## Recording identity

- Performers: Tashjian Brothers — Mardiros, Nishan and Levon Der Sarkis Tashjian.
- Title: “Tartzial Paylets” (Դարձեալ փայլեց; modern orthography Դարձյալ փայլեց; transliteration also Dardzyal Paylets / Tartsyal Paylets).
- Label/catalogue: Columbia E658/4964.
- Session: New York, autumn 1910; released 1911.
- The Armenian Museum's specialist discography lists “Tartzial Paylets” paired in the E658 group with “Mer Hayrenik” (E658-4954). Its article says the brothers' 1910 performances are three-part vocal harmonies.
- Primary identity source: https://www.armenianmuseum.org/sound-archive/tashjian-story
- Discography/label context: https://www.armenianmuseum.org/sound-archive/the-tashjian-brothers

## Song-text witness and thematic fit

Houshamadyan's Armenian-language Sassoun collection publishes a song titled “Դարձեալ փայլեց” (“It Shone Again”) in the Alahaydoyan collection. The text describes a freedom flag raised over Sassoun, calls for aid to the homeland, names Gurgen, Vahan, Hrayr Tzhokhk and Andranik in the mountains of Dalvorik, describes an order from the Sultan's palace to destroy the fedayi positions, and includes the explicit cry “Ժամանակն է վրեժ լուծել” (“It is time to take revenge”). The closing stanza describes gunfire from Armenian positions and enemy casualties.

Primary regional text source: https://www.houshamadyan.org/arm/mapottomanempire/bitlispagheshvilayet/sassoun/sassounlocalcarachteristics/alahaidoyancollectionsongs.html

A separate modern song-lyrics page gives a longer version and attributes words to Dikran Bezirgenian: https://www.paroles-musique.com/eng/Armenian-Patriotic-Songs-Collection-Tartsyal-paylets-lyrics%2Cp10242679. Treat that attribution and extended text as a secondary witness, not definitive authorship or evidence for the Columbia master.

Houshamadyan cites a 1930 *Hairenik* magazine source for material on the same page. This is a later collected text witness, not a pre-1910 publication and not an audio transcript. The title match and thematic relevance make this a high-priority comparison, but they do not prove the Tashjian performance uses these exact verses.

## Thematic assessment

Strong potential fit for explicit retaliation and collective armed resistance, rather than merely patriotic sentiment. The text's perspective is historically situated in Armenian fedayi resistance in Sassoun and should be presented with that context. Its depiction of violence is part of the source's historical-political narrative; avoid treating it as generic revenge entertainment or flattening the named actors and conflict.

The Houshamadyan page also includes songs and testimonies from a regional oral tradition. Song texts can vary by locality, collector, date and performance. The exact 1910 commercial master must be transcribed before the lyrics are quoted as belonging to this record.
