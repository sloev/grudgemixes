# Ford and Ford — “Skeeg-A-Lee Blues” (Paramount 12244-A, 1924)

**Project ID:** GM-US-007  
**Status:** C — LEAD / first documented commercial vocal version of the Stagger Lee song family  
**Session:** Chicago, 1–3 October 1924  
**Matrix:** 1899-2  
**Label:** Paramount 12244-A  
**Performers:** Zollie Ford and Ethel Ford, vocal duet; Tommy Ladnier, cornet; Johnny Dodds, clarinet; Lovie Austin, piano.

## Identity and chronology

The Tommy Ladnier discography gives the session, personnel, matrix 1899-2 and issue Paramount 12244-A. It notes that the label credits Ford and Ford, although some sources attribute the performance to Lovie Austin. Do not conflate the pianist/bandleader with the credited vocal act. https://www.jazzresearch.se/upl/website/page-1/LadnierDiscographyDecember620222.pdf

A scholarly history of American folk/blues records describes this as the first vocal version of the Stagger Lee song, recorded by an African-American male-female duo in October 1924. It distinguishes earlier 1923 “Stack O'Lee Blues” instrumentals. https://socialhistoryofamericanmusic.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/42-The-Bigger-Bang-pt.-3-Folk-Blues.pdf

## Theme and caution

The Stagger Lee/Stack-a-Lee narrative concerns a deadly confrontation between Stagger Lee and Billy Lyons, commonly framed around a hat dispute. Later versions vary substantially in motive, sequence, and moral framing. The song family can be relevant to retaliatory violence, but do not infer the exact narrative or lyrics of matrix 1899-2 from later versions. The actual performance must be transcribed before assigning a precise grievance mechanism.

This is a Black American blues/ballad tradition. Preserve that cultural context; do not reduce it to a generic outlaw story or treat violence as an uncomplicated heroic act.
