## Work and recording identity

- Arabic title: قوم يا مصري
- Transliteration: “Qum yā Miṣrī”
- English gloss: “Rise, Egyptian”
- Lyrics: Badīʿ Khayrī; music and original performance: Sayyid Darwīsh
- Work emerged in 1919 in the context of the Egyptian Revolution against British occupation; it was written for the stage work “Qūlū Lahu” and became a widely used patriotic anthem.
- The opening summons Egyptians to rise and serve the country; the lyric calls for restoring lost dignity and glory, and includes a notable appeal to neighbourly solidarity across Muslims, Christians and Jews in some text witnesses. Verify the precise stanza sequence on the recording before quoting it.

## Thematic assessment

The work is a strong collective defiance / anticolonial solidarity lead. It is not simply a generic patriotic song: it emerged in the 1919 uprising and uses an imperative call to collective action. The solidarity stanza is particularly important because it can be read as an explicit appeal across religious communities; verify its presence in the actual Odeon issue rather than importing it from a later printed lyric.

This is not revenge in the narrow sense. Its relevance is collective grievance, resistance to occupation, and the restoration of dignity. The project should preserve the Egyptian Arabic wording and the performance's stage / street circulation context.

## Date and provenance caution

The 1919 date establishes the composition/performance context, not the recording date. The CMAM record establishes that an Odeon 78-rpm item exists in the archive, but its recording-date field is empty and its access type is none. The collection's overall 1919–1932 period cannot establish this item was recorded before 1926. Since Darwīsh died in 1923, a genuine recording of his own voice would necessarily be pre-1924, but the archive metadata does not yet prove the singer on this specific carrier is Darwīsh rather than a later performer of his composition.

**Date assessment:** DATE UNCERTAIN until the actual carrier/label and vocal identity are verified.
