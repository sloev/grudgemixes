# GM-AM-005 — Tashjian Brothers, “Menk Bedk E Grvenk” (Columbia E661/4971, 1910)

**Region:** Armenian diaspora / New York; Armenian revolutionary-national song repertoire  
**Status:** C — exact Columbia issue and a strongly relevant Armenian text witness identified; recorded take not transcribed  
**Proposed role:** Collective resistance / armed recovery / anti-subjugation  
**Retrieval:** RESEARCH TARGET — request original Columbia audio and label/runout metadata from Armenian Museum

## Recording identity

- Performers: Tashjian Brothers — Mardiros, Nishan and Levon Der Sarkis Tashjian.
- Title: “Menk Bedk E Grvenk” (Մենք Պէտք Է Կռուենք; transliteration variants include “Menk Bedk Eh Grvenk”).
- Label/catalogue: Columbia E661/4971.
- Session: New York, autumn 1910; release 1911.
- Language: Armenian.
- The Armenian Museum's Tashjian discography lists this title in the E661 pairing, alongside “Azniv Enger.”
- Primary identity source: Armenian Museum of America, Tashjian Brothers discography: https://www.armenianmuseum.org/sound-archive/tashjian-story

## Text witness

The Armenian Online Songbook Yerkaran publishes a text under the title “Menk Bedk E Grvenk | Menk Bed E Grvenk Yev Votch Latz Linenk,” crediting Mgrdich Mgrdichyan. The opening reads:

“Մենք պէտք է կռուենք եւ ոչ լաց լինենք,
Ազգին կորուստը զէնքով ետ խլենք.
Այսքան դար լացինք, ո՞վ է լըսողը,
Մեզ ի՞նչ շահ բերեց արցունքի ծովը։”

A cautious English rendering:

“We must fight and not weep;
take back by arms what the nation has lost.
For so many centuries we wept—who listened?
What benefit did a sea of tears bring us?”

Source: https://yerkaran.org/page/45/

This is a songbook text witness, not proof of the exact words on the 1910 Columbia master. Armenian-language transcription of the recording is required before quotation or mix approval.

## Thematic assessment

The text is unusually explicit about collective loss, unheard grievance, and armed recovery. It directly contrasts passive lament with organized resistance. This is a strong conceptual fit for the project's collective grievance / defiance strand, but it is not necessarily a revenge narrative in the narrow sense.

The song is part of Armenian revolutionary-patriotic repertoire. Preserve its specific historical and political context; do not flatten it into a generic anti-authoritarian slogan or assume all Armenian listeners shared one political interpretation.
