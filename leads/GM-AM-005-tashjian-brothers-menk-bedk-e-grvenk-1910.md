# GM-AM-005 — Tashjian Brothers, “Menk Bedk E Grvenk” (Columbia E661 / matrix 4973-1, 1910)

**Region:** Armenian diaspora / New York; Armenian revolutionary-national song repertoire  
**Status:** B — PROBABLE CANDIDATE; exact Columbia side and strong pre-1926 thematic text witnesses are identified, but the recorded take is not transcribed  
**Proposed role:** Collective resistance / armed recovery / anti-subjugation  
**Retrieval:** RESEARCH TARGET — request original Columbia audio and label/runout metadata from Armenian Museum

## Recording identity

- Performers: Tashjian Brothers — Mardiros, Nishan and Levon Der Sarkis Tashjian.
- Title: “Menk Bedk E Grvenk” (Մենք Պէտք Է Կռուենք; transliteration variants include “Menk Bedk Eh Grvenk”).
- Label/catalogue: Columbia E661; matrix-side 4973-1. The Armenian Museum page repeats E661-4971 for both titles in the pairing; the independent Columbia E-Series table assigns 4971-1 to “Aznif Ungert” and 4973-1 to “Menk Bedk E Gurvink-Fedayi Yeraze.” Use E661 / matrix 4973-1 provisionally, pending physical label/runout confirmation.
- Session: New York, autumn 1910; release 1911.
- Language: Armenian.
- The Armenian Museum's Tashjian discography lists this title in the E661 pairing, alongside “Azniv Enger,” but prints the same number E661-4971 for both titles. The independent Columbia E-Series discography distinguishes them: “Aznif Ungert” is matrix 4971-1, while “Menk Bedk E Gurvink-Fedayi Yeraze” is matrix 4973-1. This supports E661 as the catalogue number and 4973-1 as the target side matrix; retain the museum's duplicated number as a documented source typo/discrepancy.
- Primary identity source: Armenian Museum of America, Tashjian Brothers discography: https://www.armenianmuseum.org/sound-archive/tashjian-story

## Text witness

The Armenian Online Songbook Yerkaran publishes a text under the title “Menk Bedk E Grvenk | Menk Bed E Grvenk Yev Votch Latz Linenk,” crediting Mgrdich Mgrdichyan. The opening reads:

“Մենք պէտք է կռուենք եւ ոչ լաց լինենք,
Ազգին կորուստը զէնքով ետ խլենք.
Այսքան դար լացինք, ո՞վ է լըսողը,
Մեզ ի՞նչ շահ բերեց արցունքի ծովը։”

A cautious English rendering:

“We must fight and not weep;
take back by arms what the nation has lost.
For so many centuries we wept—who listened?
What benefit did a sea of tears bring us?”

Source: https://yerkaran.org/page/45/

This is a songbook text witness, not proof of the exact words on the 1910 Columbia master. Armenian-language transcription of the recording is required before quotation or mix approval.

## Thematic assessment

The text is unusually explicit about collective loss, unheard grievance, and armed recovery. It directly contrasts passive lament with organized resistance. This is a strong conceptual fit for the project's collective grievance / defiance strand, but it is not necessarily a revenge narrative in the narrow sense.

The song is part of Armenian revolutionary-patriotic repertoire. Preserve its specific historical and political context; do not flatten it into a generic anti-authoritarian slogan or assume all Armenian listeners shared one political interpretation.

## 2026-11-18 — pre-1926 text chronology and thematic-grade refinement

Harutyun Marutyan's Armenian-language study, published by Enlight Studies on 23 April 2020, identifies “Մենք պետք է կռվենք” (“We must fight”) as a fedayi/revolutionary song and reproduces its opening couplet: “Մենք պետք է կռվենք և ոչ լաց լինենք / Ազգի կորուստը զենքով ետ խլենք” (“We must fight and not weep / take back by arms what the nation has lost”). Marutyan documents the couplet on six different posters at the 24 April 1990 commemoration and describes the song as approximately 80 years old at that point, an approximate early-twentieth-century chronology rather than a precise composition date.

Crucially, footnote 32 cites an earlier printed witness: T. Varjabedyan (compiler), *Քնար. գրպանի նոր երգարան* [Knar: Pocket New Songbook], New Jersey, 1918, p.105. Marutyan compares it with A. S. Ghaziyan (compiler), *Հայ ժողովրդական ռազմի և զինվորի երգեր* [Armenian Folk War and Soldier Songs], Yerevan, 1989, pp.105, 113, 146–148, 291, noting small textual variants (“չգանգատվենք” / “չլանք”; “թշնամին” / “տաճիկը”; “սովից” / “մահից”). The 1918 book itself was not directly inspected in this pass; the citation and comparison are mediated through Marutyan's study.

This materially strengthens the thematic and composition-text chronology: the same title and armed-recovery refrain were in print by 1918, and the 1910 Columbia matrix title includes the subtitle “Fedayi Yeraze.” It does **not** prove that the 1910 take contains the 1918 wording. The exact-master text link therefore remains probable, not verified. Keep the 2009 Yerkaran text as a separate witness and preserve its Mgrdich Mgrdichyan attribution; do not treat the 2017 ERGIR performance credit as proof of composition authorship.
