# GM-AM-006 — Mardiros Tashjian, “Dalvorig” (Columbia, 1909)

**Region:** Armenian diaspora / New York; song refers to Dalvorig in the Sassoun region  
**Status:** B — exact performer, title and session documented; early text witness strongly supports defiance and armed resistance, but exact recorded lyrics and catalogue/matrix remain unresolved  
**Proposed role:** Anti-subjugation / armed defiance / collective liberation  
**Retrieval:** RESEARCH TARGET — request the 1909 solo Columbia “Dalvorig” side from the Armenian Museum

## Recording identity

- Performer: Mardiros Der Sarkis Tashjian, solo.
- Title: “Dalvorig.”
- Label: Columbia Phonograph Company, foreign-language E series context.
- Session: New York, 1909.
- The Armenian Museum's specialist discography lists “Dalvorig” among the six solo recordings Tashjian made in 1909. The discography does not give a catalogue or matrix number for this side; its paired title is listed as “Star Spangled Banner.”
- Primary source: Armenian Museum of America, Harout Arakelian, “The Incomplete Story of Mardiros Der Sarkis Tashjian & His Brothers”: https://www.armenianmuseum.org/sound-archive/tashjian-story
- Do not conflate this solo 1909 performance with the separately listed Tashjian Brothers “Dalvorig” in the 1910 discography, Columbia E656.

## Text and historical context

A pre-1909 English-language text witness is Alice Stone Blackwell's *Armenian Poems* (Boston: Roberts Brothers, 1896), which includes “The Son of Dalvorig,” attributed to Mihran Damadian, pp. 135–138. The poem's speaker identifies himself as a free son of Dalvorig, refuses to bow before the Turk, calls Armenian brothers to join him for liberty, rejects foreign domination, and describes armed struggle. In its closing movement, the speaker says that for every blow dealt him, he returns a thousand.

Source: https://archive.org/details/cu31924026917231 (Cornell copy also available via https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/7e/Armenian_poems_%28IA_cu31924026917231%29.pdf)

The 1896 book's preface explains that Blackwell's English verse translations were made from prose translations supplied by Armenian intermediaries; it explicitly warns of possible errors. This is therefore a valuable pre-recording English text witness, not an exact Armenian lyric transcription and not proof that every stanza appears on Tashjian's 1909 master.

A Turkish-language institutional biography of Mihran Damadyan quotes a different short passage about Dalvorig, freedom and calling Armenian brothers forward: https://satemer.sakarya.edu.tr/tr/icerik/19869/102034/mihran-damadyan-ve-itiraflari. Treat this as contextual corroboration, not an independent recording transcript.

Houshamadyan's Sassoun song collection documents the wider regional tradition of fedayi and patriotic songs, including a later collected song “Dalvorigi Zavage.” Its introduction emphasizes that oral songs circulate, evolve, and acquire variant verses; this reinforces the need to distinguish song-family text from a particular commercial master: https://www.houshamadyan.org/mapottomanempire/vilayet-of-bitlispaghesh/sassoun/local-characteristics/alahaidoyan-coll-songs.html

## Thematic assessment

Strong fit for anti-subjugation, collective defiance and armed resistance. The text is not a neutral folk outlaw song: it belongs to a specific Armenian political and historical repertoire. The archive should contextualize its revolutionary language, identify the historical actors as carefully as possible, and avoid turning a song-family text into a claim about the exact 1909 performance before transcription.

## 2026-11-19 research audit — solo 1909 issue and evidence boundaries

The Armenian Museum of America's item-specific discography was reopened and checked directly. It lists six solo Mardiros Tashjian recordings made in 1909 and names “Dalvorig” with an **unknown matrix number**, paired with “Star Spangled Banner”. The same article distinguishes these six solo recordings from the 28 Tashjian Brothers songs recorded in 1910; its separate 1910 list gives the brothers' “Dalvorig” as E656. This independently reinforces the dossier's essential object distinction: the 1909 solo performance and 1910 trio performance are not one recording. The article also describes Columbia's E-series foreign-language context and double-sided disc practice, but it does not supply a matrix, label scan or recording transcript for the 1909 “Dalvorig”.

The 1896 Alice Stone Blackwell / Mihran Damadian “The Son of Dalvorig” text remains a pre-recording thematic witness, not an exact lyric transcription of this Columbia side. No claim is made that all of its stanzas occur on the 1909 master. The text witness and the recording identity support a strong defiance lead, but the recording's exact performance and matrix remain unknown.
