# GM-US-018 — Julius Sutton, “Single Girl” (Gordon cylinder A79, Item NC117; 28 October 1925)

**Status:** B — probable core candidate; exact field cylinder, performer, place/date and a grievance-bearing text are documented by the Library of Congress presentation  
**Date assessment:** CORE DATE — VERIFIED FIXATION DATE (28 October 1925)  
**Thematic family:** grievance testimony / domestic defiance / cautionary song  
**Recording type:** field recording on wax cylinder; later presented on the Library of Congress 1978 LP and online

## Recording identity

The Library of Congress American Folklife Center's *Folk-Songs of America: The Robert Winslow Gordon Collection, 1922–1932* identifies the item as **“Single Girl,” Gordon cylinder A79, Item NC117**, sung by **Julius Sutton** in **Dillsboro, North Carolina**, on **28 October 1925**. The liner notes say Sutton was recorded by Robert Winslow Gordon and that the song was also collected in other Southern versions; another North Carolina cylinder version is listed as A93 / NC137. Cousin Emmy's later commercial recording dates from the mid-1940s and is not the same performance.

The Library of Congress presentation states that the recordings were originally made on wax cylinders and that the online MP3 is presented as it appeared on the 1978 Archive of Folk Culture LP. Thus the web audio is a later transfer/presentation, not a direct cylinder file.

**Primary archive record and transcript:** https://wwws.loc.gov/folklife/Gordon/sideAband8.html  
**Full collection liner notes / track index:** https://wwws.loc.gov/folklife/Gordon/GordonFolkSongLinerNotes.pdf

## Thematic reading

The recorded text opens by warning young women against marriage. It contrasts the singer's former freedom and decent clothing with the labour and deprivation of married life, then describes a husband returning with a bottle and wishing she were dead, followed by children crying for food. The singer repeatedly wishes she were single again.

This is not a revenge narrative and should not be tagged as one. It is a first-person grievance account that combines accusation of domestic neglect/abuse with a refusal-like warning against marriage. The thematic case rests on the actual transcribed text, not on the title. The song's speaker is a performed persona; the text is not evidence about Sutton's own life or gender identity.

## Chronology and version control

- Fixation: 28 October 1925, before the project's 1 January 1926 cutoff.
- The song is documented in multiple regional versions; Sutton's text is one specific witness.
- The Library of Congress notes a separate North Carolina cylinder version (Gordon cylinder A93, Item NC137) and a later commercial Cousin Emmy version (Decca 24215, 1947). Do not merge these with A79 / NC117.
- The recording's traditional composition history is not established by this item. The 1925 date establishes the fixation of Sutton's performance, not the song's origin.

## Access and rights

The LOC page provides a playable MP3 presentation derived from the 1978 LP. The original wax cylinder and the LP transfer are distinct carriers. No independent listening was performed for this research note; the lyric assessment is based on the archive's published transcript. Rights and reuse conditions for the original cylinder, LP transfer and any publication remain to be checked with the Library of Congress.
