# GM-US-014 — Tobe Little [Vernon Dalhart], “Chain Gang Song” (Okeh 40328, 1925)

**Status:** C — lead; exact issue/side identified, lyric content unverified  
**Proposed role:** Penal labour / punishment / possible grievance testimony

## Exact recording identity

- Performer credit: Tobe Little (Okeh pseudonym for Vernon Dalhart)
- Title: “Chain Gang Song,” side 2 of Okeh 40328
- Paired side: “The Prisoner's Song,” side 1
- Matrix: 73222-A (specialist Okeh discography / matrix listings)
- Recording date: 1925; one secondary discography places the session on 1 March 1925. Smithsonian NMAH confirms a 1925 Okeh 40328 shellac carrier.
- Smithsonian NMAH object 1996.0320.19744: https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1327855
- Contemporary trade listing: *Talking Machine World*, 15 April 1925, lists Okeh 40328 and describes “Chain Gang Song” as Tobe Little baritone solo with guitar and harmonica: https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Talking-Machine/20s/Talking-Machine-1925-04.pdf
- Matrix/label discography lead: https://www.78discography.com/OK40000.html
- Sheet-music catalogue: Kent State's crime-related sheet music inventory lists “The Chain Gang Song,” words and music by Robert E. Massey, Shapiro, Bernstein & Co., New York, 1925: https://www.library.kent.edu/special-collections-and-archives/crime-related-sheet-music
- A digitized 1924 Shapiro, Bernstein GEM advertisement identifies “The Chain Gang Song” by Robert E. Massey as one of six works “introduced and recorded by Vernon Dalhart”: https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5b11858da2772cf01402ee6e/t/5bd31e300852296af8ed920a/1540562517150/Massey-PrisonersSong-1924.pdf

## Thematic assessment

The title and contemporary catalogue place the song in the penal-labour / chain-gang subject area. That makes it worth screening for a representation of punishment, coercion, forced labour, or grievance about incarceration. However, neither title nor catalogue description proves that the singer protests the system, complains of wrongful punishment, or expresses retaliation. The lyric text has not yet been located or linked securely to the Okeh master.

A Smithsonian Ralph Rinzler Folklife Archives catalogue entry describes a later 45-rpm archival copy of Okeh 40328 in the Moses and Frances Asch Collection. Its terms explicitly state: “No duplication allowed”; listening and viewing are for research purposes only, and permission is required for publication. This is an identification/listening lead, not a permitted download route: https://collections.si.edu/search/results.htm?fq=name%3A%22Folkways+Records%22&q=%22Song%22 . Grok must not duplicate or retrieve that restricted item.
