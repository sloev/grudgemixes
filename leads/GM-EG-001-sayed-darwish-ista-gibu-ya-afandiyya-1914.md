# GM-EG-001 — Sayyid Darwīsh, “Istaʿgibū yā Afandiyya” (1914-listed; 78 rpm issue unresolved)

**Region:** Egypt / Arab East  
**Status:** C — strong thematic lead; pre-1926 recording date not established  
**Proposed role:** Economic grievance / public accusation / social protest  
**Retrieval:** RESEARCH TARGET — exact item-level record located; access unavailable in archive catalogue

## Work and text

- Arabic title: استعجبوا يا أفندية (ليتر الكاز بروبية)
- Transliteration: “Istaʿgibū yā Afandiyya (Litr al-jāz bi-rūbiyah)”
- English gloss: “Be astonished, gentlemen (a litre of kerosene for a rupee)”
- Composer / lyricist: Sayyid Darwīsh (music); Badīʿ Khayrī (words)
- Earliest appearance in Masters of Tarab timeline: 1914 (year in a list; event unspecified)
- Genre is variously classified as monologue and taqṭūqah; retain both labels rather than forcing one translation.
- The available transcription complains about wartime price rises and black-market profiteering in kerosene, scarcity and the police patrol. It names the officials (“afandiyya”) in a pointed address and describes ordinary people left in darkness. This is a direct economic grievance, not merely a generic complaint.

- Item title: “قوم يا مصري : سيد درويش” — separate item, not this song; do not conflate.

- Collection includes an item titled “لحن البرابرة . لحن السياسة” (D-3050), but that is not “Istaʿgibū ya Afandiyya”; do not conflate by political theme.
- Masters of Tarab work page: https://x.tarab.ai/s/D0004
- Masters of Tarab gives 1914 as earliest appearance, performer Sayyid Darwīsh, lyrics by Badīʿ Khayrī, and a 78-rpm disc medium, but does not identify a label, catalogue number or matrix. It lists multiple online performances, none independently authenticated as the original 78.
- The CMAM collection page documents 39 78-rpm items associated with Darwīsh, deposited by Baidaphon, Odeon and Mechian, with collection recording period 1919–1932; that range is collection-level and does not date this specific recording.

**Date assessment:** DATE UNCERTAIN. Darwīsh died in 1923, and the work is listed from 1914, but the specific commercial 78-rpm recording's issue/fixation date and master identity have not been established. The recording could be a posthumous issue of a pre-existing performance, or a later rendition by another singer. Do not put it in the pre-1926 core until exact audio/label/matrix evidence resolves this.

## Thematic assessment

This is a particularly valuable non-Anglophone lead because the text itself—not just the title—describes an economic grievance: kerosene price inflation, black-market selling, shortages, and the presence of police officials. It may fit collective grievance and accusation better than revenge or personal retaliation. It also records a specific urban experience of scarcity and price exploitation around the First World War.

The lyric is transmitted through a modern compiled work page and needs checking against a reliable Arabic text or the actual recording. The English gloss above is interpretive, not a line-by-line translation. Preserve the original Arabic and consult an Arabic-speaking researcher before publication. Do not flatten “afandiyya” into a generic “government” without explaining its social register.
