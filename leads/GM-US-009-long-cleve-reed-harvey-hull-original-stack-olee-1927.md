# Long “Cleve” Reed and Little Harvey Hull — “Original Stack O'Lee Blues” (Black Patti 8030, 1927)

**Project ID:** GM-US-009  
**Status:** C — RARE-CARRIER LEAD / exact issue identified; source audio access and matrix unknown  
**Session:** Chicago, circa May 1927  
**Label/catalogue:** Black Patti 8030, side B  
**Performers:** Long “Cleve” Reed and Little Harvey Hull, Down Home Boys; vocal and guitar personnel described in specialist discography.

## Identity and rarity

The illustrated Papa Harvey Hull / Long “Cleve” Reed discography identifies the May 1927 Chicago session, the title “Original Stack O'Lee Blues,” and Black Patti 8030. It states that only one known copy was in Joe Bussard's possession at the time the discography was compiled. https://www.wirz.de/music/hullph.htm

45cat documents the Black Patti 8030 two-sided issue, with “Mama You Don't Know How” on side A and “Original Stack O'Lee Blues” on side B. Its collector comments also describe the Joe Bussard copy as the only known example. https://www.45cat.com/78rpm/record/8030us

An *Oxford American* feature recounts Joe Bussard's discovery and the extraordinary rarity and collector interest of Black Patti 8030. It notes that Bussard believed his copy was the only surviving example, while another collector had heard of a possible second copy. Treat “only surviving copy” as a historically reported claim, not an absolute current census. https://oxfordamerican.org/magazine/issue-85-summer-2014/black-patti-8030

## Thematic assessment

This is an early, distinct vocal interpretation of the Stagger Lee / Stack O'Lee murder-ballad family. It is highly relevant to research on retaliatory violence and outlaw memory. However, its exact lyric, narrative sequence and stance toward the killing must be checked against the recording; do not substitute later lyrics or assume the title alone establishes a revenge motive.

The recording belongs to a Black American blues tradition. The historical rarity of the disc should not eclipse the cultural context of the performers and song tradition.
