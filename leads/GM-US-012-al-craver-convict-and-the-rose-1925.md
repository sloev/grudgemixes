## Textual witness and thematic assessment

A transcription from a 1928 Ontario text witness gives a first-person prisoner awaiting execution. The narrator says the judge did not believe his story, the jury imposed punishment, and he maintains his innocence; the rose sent by his sweetheart becomes a token of their shared past and a final keepsake. The transcription and its provenance are described here: https://oldtymesongs.wordpress.com/2008/12/08/the-convict-and-the-rose/ .

This is a strong thematic lead for disputed guilt, punishment and a grievance against judicial judgment. But the 1928 text is not yet proven to reproduce Dalhart's Columbia master. Do not quote it as the 1925 recording's lyrics, and do not infer that the historical prisoner was actually innocent. The song's narrator's claim is a dramatic voice, not evidence about a real case.

The 1925 song also circulated in other Dalhart releases. UCSB's list of 1925 recordings digitized and entering the public domain in 2026 names “The Convict and the Rose” by Vernon Dalhart and Company on Edison 51643, and Dalhart on Banner 1653, Regal 9959 and Silvertone 2706. These are separate issues/recording identities until matrix and performance crosswalks establish equivalence: https://www.library.ucsb.edu/1925-recordings-digitized-ucsb-entering-public-domain-january-1-2026 . A YouTube upload labelled “original” is a discovery lead only, not a provenance record: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=npEWSiXatOw .
