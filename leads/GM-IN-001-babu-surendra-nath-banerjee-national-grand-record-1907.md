# GM-IN-001 — Babu Surendra Nath Banerjee, “Bande Mataram and Swadeshi” / “Partition of Bengal” (National Grand Record / Beka, catalogue 20290–20291)

## Identification

- Speaker / performer: Babu Surendra Nath Banerjee (also spelled Surendranath Banerjea in specialist discography).
- Object title in the Berlin museum catalogue: “Speech on ‘Bande Mataram and Swadeshi’”.
- Language: Bengali, according to Michael Kinnear's Beka discography.
- Format: shellac disc.
- Label: National Grand Record, associated with Beka's Indian repertoire; specialist discography says this was a special “NATIONAL” GRAND RECORD, not a standard Beka Grand Record label.
- Catalogue / side numbers: 20290 and 20291. Kinnear's discography gives 20290 / 20291 and identifies “Bande Mataram/Swadeshism” and “Partition of Bengal”; the Berlin object catalogue titles the surviving side “Speech on ‘Bande Mataram and Swadeshi’”.
- Matrix: not independently confirmed. Kinnear notes Beka catalogue and matrix numbers often coincide, but the physical runout needs inspection; do not assert a confirmed matrix from the catalogue number alone.
- Recording date/location: unresolved. Kinnear places the 20,000 series within the Beka Grand Record repertoire associated with the 1907 Indian recording tour. Beka's 1907 tour included Calcutta. This is context, not proof that this specific item was recorded in Calcutta in 1907. Scroll's 2014 account says Beka recording experts recorded the political disc in Calcutta in November 1907, but this needs primary corroboration.
- Issue date: exact date not established. The recording belongs to Beka/National Grand Record's early Indian repertoire and is discussed in connection with the pre-1910 Swadeshi record history. Do not assign a precise release date without a dated catalogue or trade notice.
- Physical holding: Ethnologisches Museum, Staatliche Museen zu Berlin, inventory VII 78/1107.
- Digital access: Berlin museum / Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek item record and object images; no audio playback or downloadable sound file identified.

## Historical and thematic evidence

The Berlin museum's collection record identifies the physical object as a shellac disc, attributes the performance to Babu Surendra Nath Banerjee, and names National Grand Record as maker and publisher. Its title is “Speech on ‘Bande Mataram and Swadeshi’”.

Michael Kinnear's specialist Beka label history and discography identifies a special “NATIONAL” GRAND RECORD featuring Banerjee reciting two nationalist pieces:
- 20290: “Bande Mataram/Swadeshism”
- 20291: “Partition of Bengal”
Kinnear notes that Beka's Indian 20,000 series is associated with the 1907 recording tour and that Beka's catalogue and matrix numbers often coincide, but exact matrix suffix/runout evidence for this record remains unverified.

A contemporary trade-press article in *The Talking Machine World* (15 April 1909) explains that the anti-partition boycott in Bengal was organized because people had little political voice and grievances remained unredressed; it describes Swadeshi as the positive programme of fostering indigenous industry and records that sales of certain Swadeshi songs had been prohibited / withdrawn. It specifically discusses “Bandematram” as a widely sold national song and quotes a protest against its proscription. This establishes contemporary political context and commercial controversy, but does not itself identify Banerjee's National Grand Record by catalogue number.

A 2014 Scroll article reports that Beka recording experts recorded a political speech in Calcutta in November 1907, on a National Grand Record, with Banerjee speaking about Bengal's partition and the reverse carrying a speech on Bande Mataram. This is a useful secondary account and possible recording-session clue, not a substitute for a dated Beka session sheet or matrix source.

The surviving museum item is a rare tangible trace of anticolonial political speech entering the commercial record market. It is spoken-word recitation, not a song. The actual speech transcript / exact words delivered on each side have not yet been established, so do not quote or paraphrase the recording's contents beyond its documented titles.
