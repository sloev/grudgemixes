## Thematic assessment

This is the familiar “Jesse James” ballad family (Laws E1), not the separate, more historically critical “Jesse James II” song sometimes also recorded under that title. The common ballad condemns Robert Ford as a “dirty little coward” for killing James after gaining his trust. That makes the song a possible case of betrayal accusation and counter-insult, rather than a direct narrative of revenge carried out.

A 2022 scholarly chapter on railways and music notes Puckett's version includes “Dear old Jesse, poor old Jesse” and a verse in which Ford falls to his knees and hands over keys to the bank he had robbed. That is a secondary account of a lyric variant, not a verified transcription from the exact 15033-D master; do not use it as the final transcript without listening. The chapter also shows how the ballad's outlaw-hero narrative can encode hostility toward railways and financial institutions, but that context must not be mistaken for a statement of Puckett's personal beliefs.

## Date and source caveats

- Columbia matrix discography gives 11 September 1924 for matrix 140016; the commercial catalogue number 15033-D is a later issue, listed in June 1925.
- Do not conflate matrix date with issue date.
- Do not conflate Riley Puckett's performance with Bascom Lamar Lunsford's Okeh 40155 or Bentley Ball's Columbia A3085.
