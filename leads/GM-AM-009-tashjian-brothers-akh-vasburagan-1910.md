# GM-AM-009 — Tashjian Brothers, “Akh Vasburagan” (Columbia E666/4974, 1910)

## Status

**Grade C — strong song-family thematic lead; exact 1910 recorded lyric not yet established.** The Armenian Museum of America identifies the Tashjian Brothers' Columbia side and describes the title as “Akh Vasburagan — Voghp Vasburagani” (“Oh, Vaspurakan — The Lament of Vaspurakan”). A separately preserved Armenian text under the title “Akh Vaspurakan” contains explicit collective revenge language and a call to take up rifles. That text is not yet matched to the Columbia master.

## Recording identity

- **Performer:** Tashjian Brothers — Mardiros, Nishan and Levon Der Sarkis.
- **Title:** “Akh Vasburagan” (also transliterated “Akh Vaspurakan”).
- **Label / catalogue:** Columbia E666/4974.
- **Session:** New York, autumn 1910.
- **Release:** 1911.
- **Paired side:** “Zartir Vortiag” (Columbia E666/4975).
- **Language:** Armenian.
- **Matrix/runout:** Not independently established; request label and runout photographs.
- **Audio / transfer:** No exact Columbia audio located or auditioned by ChatGPT.

The Armenian Museum places the recording among the Tashjian Brothers' 28 patriotic and sentimental sides from the 1910 session. Its account calls the song a lament for Vaspurakan. This supports identity and broad subject, not the detailed lyrics below.

## Text witness and thematic evidence

The Armenian-language ERGIR page “ԱԽ ՎԱՍՊՈՒՐԱԿԱՆ” (“Akh Vaspurakan”) attributes the words to S. Jamkochyan. Its stanzas:
- mourn Vaspurakan and the Armenian dead;
- condemn captivity, foreign dependence and disunity;
- call for Armenian unity and explicitly say “Պէտք է մենք լուծենք վրէժ հայկական” — approximately, “We must exact Armenian revenge”;
- describe Van and its districts filled with bodies and fields stained with Armenian blood;
- denounce the Ottoman army and artillery;
- end with a call to take up rifles as Armenian communities and homes are attacked.

This is a clear thematic match **if** the same text is present on the 1910 side. The ERGIR page also links two audio files credited to an unknown performer, but does not identify either as the Tashjian Columbia record. A separate Armenian Public Radio Archive item for “Akh, Vaspurakan” is a 1991 recording by Varduhi Khachatryan, not a pre-1926 source. Neither should be passed off as the 1910 master.

## Historical and editorial caution

The text is a nationalist memorial / mobilising song shaped by mass violence, dispossession and armed resistance. Its “revenge” language should be translated and contextualised, not detached as a generic vengeance slogan. The recording date (1910) is not necessarily the composition date; the lyric attribution and publication history need independent dating. The Tashjian Brothers' Columbia master may use a shorter, altered or different text.

**No mix approval.** Retain Grade C until the exact Columbia performance is obtained, transcribed in Armenian, and compared line-by-line with the ERGIR text. Seek review from an Armenian-language specialist and preserve uncertainty about the lyric's author/date.
