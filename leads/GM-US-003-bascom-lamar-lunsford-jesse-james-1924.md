## Thematic assessment

The traditional “Jesse James” ballad frames Robert Ford's killing of James as betrayal by a trusted associate and repeatedly condemns Ford as a coward. It can therefore serve as a public accusation/counter-insult and betrayal-grievance text. It is not straightforward revenge: the familiar text condemns the killing but does not narrate a retaliatory act against Ford. Later folklore and popular culture sometimes append or associate revenge for James's death with Edward O'Kelly, but that is not evidence that this particular 1924 performance does so.

The Library of Congress describes the ballad as focusing on James's murder by a trusted friend and distinguishes the ballad's heroic framing from historical outlawry: https://www.loc.gov/collections/songs-of-america/articles-and-essays/musical-styles/traditional/traditional-ballads/ . Pete Seeger's Folkways notes identify Lunsford's Okeh 40155 as the first documented commercial recording (1924) and discuss betrayal/death at the hands of a trusted friend: https://folkways-media.si.edu/docs/folkways/artwork/SFW40155.pdf . Ballad Index lists the Lunsford recording under Okeh 40155 (1924): https://balladindex.org/Ballads/LE01.html .

The exact words and verse order on Lunsford's master are not yet established. Do not copy a later standard text into this performance's transcript. Candidate remains C until the DAHR audio or a matched carrier transfer is examined.

## Chronology and source caveats

- The song is traditional and circulated in print before this recording. John A. Lomax included it in *Cowboy Songs and Other Frontier Ballads* (1910); the Library of Congress describes the ballad's historical and folkloric context.
- DAHR's Joe Bussard collection page supplies the specific matrix/date and states the digitization route.
- Secondary discographies corroborate the Okeh issue and 1924 recording, but the exact label/runout and transfer provenance have not been inspected.
- Do not conflate this recording with Bentley Ball's earlier Columbia A3085 performance (GM-US-001); they are separate performances of the same ballad.
