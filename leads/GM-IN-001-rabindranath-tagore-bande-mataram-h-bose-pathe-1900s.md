# GM-IN-001 — Rabindranath Tagore, “Bande Mataram” (H. Bose cylinder / Pathé-H. Bose disc; 1890s–1908, date disputed)

**Region:** Bengal / South Asia  
**Status:** C — major anticolonial protest-history lead; recording chronology and surviving audio unresolved  
**Proposed role:** Collective grievance / anticolonial defiance / protest  
**Retrieval:** RESEARCH TARGET — surviving fragment and original carrier provenance require investigation

## Work and recording history

- Work: “Bande Mataram” / “Vande Mataram,” from Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's novel *Anandamath* (1882); Tagore sang it in his own tune.
- Performer: Rabindranath Tagore.
- Recording entrepreneur: Hemendra Mohan Bose, a Bengali recording pioneer who issued H. Bose's cylinders and later partnered with Pathé for disc transfers.
- H. Bose cylinder catalogue references include cylinder no. 250 (also listed as 36250 in later accounts); Pathé-H. Bose disc catalogue reference 36250 is reported in historical catalogues.
- Chronology is disputed: published accounts variously place Tagore's recording in 1896, 1898, 1905/1906, or 1907. One scholarly account says Tagore recorded the song in 1905 and that some H. Bose cylinders were transferred to Pathé vertical-cut discs in 1908. Another account traces it to 1898 and gives Pathé-H. Bose catalogue 36250. A later journalistic account says the surviving fragment is the only audible remnant after colonial police raids and destruction of H. Bose's nationalist recordings.
- Do not select a single date until the original catalogue, cylinder register, carrier and transfer history are reconciled.

## Political context and thematic assessment

“Bande Mataram” became a major song of the Swadeshi movement and resistance to the 1905 partition of Bengal. Its circulation was entangled with political meetings, anti-colonial mobilisation, police searches and destruction/confiscation of nationalist recording material. The gramophone/cylinder was not simply a neutral imported technology: local entrepreneurs and activists used it to circulate political sound.

This is a strong collective protest / anticolonial defiance context. It is not a simple revenge song. The song's political and religious imagery has also been contested, including debate over its devotional language and its place in a religiously plural national movement. Any use must explain that history and avoid presenting one political interpretation as universal or uncontested.

## Retrieval and provenance

- Find the surviving cylinder/disc or an authorized digital transfer; do not assume a modern short clip is a complete side or an authenticated transfer.
- Establish whether the extant audio is the original H. Bose cylinder, a Pathé vertical-cut transfer, a later copy, or a fragment assembled from another source.
- Verify the actual catalogue number and distinguish H. Bose cylinder 250 / 36250 from Pathé-H. Bose disc 36250; numbering is inconsistently reported.
- Search Indian sound archives, H. Bose family collections, National Film Archive / Sangeet Natak Akademi / NCPA holdings, and collector research; request permission where needed.
- Keep the Tagore performance separate from other early “Bande Mataram” issues by Narain Chandra Mukherjee, Beka, National Grand, and later performers.

## Decision

Retain as a C research lead with major historical significance. It is a clear collective anticolonial protest context, but the actual recording date, carrier identity, surviving audio and provenance remain unresolved. No audio has been auditioned or downloaded by this research agent.
