# About Spite Frequencies

**Spite Frequencies** collects music made where it wasn't wanted, and songs that kept the score.

Each post is one recording, or one lead on a recording. They come from the research behind two archival mixtape series that go with the book *grudgebook* and the site spitebites.party:

- **Grudge Mixes**: recorded grievances from before 1926. Revenge vows, accusations, feuds, and debts handed down a family.
- **Music in Spite**: music made where it wasn't expected or tolerated: under bans, in voices people laughed at, on home tapes, on found cassettes.

## The thin red line

A grudge is a wrong someone refuses to let go of, and music is one of the oldest ways people keep that refusal alive. Every post answers three questions:

1. What was the wrong, or the refusal?
2. Who stood on each side of it?
3. What does the music do about it: keep the record, defy, annoy, or strike back?

Posts are tagged by where the spite sits:

- **The Account**: in the words.
- **Out of Turn**: in a voice that wasn't meant to be heard.
- **Made Anyway**: in the act of making the music at all.
- **Unwanted**: in how the music was received.
- **Fighting Words**: in who the music is aimed at.
- **Found**: in the accident that saved it.

## What is not here

Some of what we research will never become a post. That includes recordings of patients, prisoners or Indigenous communities whose consent we don't have, private speech found on tapes, and anything that could identify private people. We never mock the people who made this music. The joke, when there is one, is on taste.

Many posts are generated from our research notes and are rougher than the hand-written ones. Recording links go to their owners: archives, labels, blogs and collectors. We host no audio. The cover images are generated, not photographs.

## Corrections and finds
