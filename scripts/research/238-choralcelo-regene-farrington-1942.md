The Choralcelo was an immense electrical keyboard instrument built by Melvin Severy and George Sinclair. Its magnets could set piano strings and separate banks of metal, wood and other resonators vibrating, giving the player a private orchestra of strange, sustained colours. The company installed instruments in wealthy homes, hotels, churches and even a yacht, but the machine never became a normal part of musical life.

This is a rare chance to hear one. Regene Farrington, trained as a Choralcelo player and wife of the company president, recorded “Poor Little Butterfly” in the New York demonstration studio in 1942. The surviving transfer comes from a scratched glass master. It is not a modern reconstruction: it is the actual instrument, played by someone who knew it.
