The Choralcelo was an immense electrical keyboard instrument built by Melvin Severy and George Sinclair. Its magnets could set piano strings and separate banks of metal, wood and other resonators vibrating, giving the player a private orchestra of strange, sustained colours. The company installed instruments in wealthy homes, hotels, churches and even a yacht, but the machine never became a normal part of musical life.

A short recording survives from a glass master made in the Choralcelo Company's New York demonstration studio. It was played by Regene Farrington, a trained Choralcelo performer and wife of the company's president. The transfer is badly scratched, but it is not a modern reconstruction: it is the actual instrument, played by someone who knew it.

There is a small archival puzzle here. One AMICA page identifies the excerpt as “I’m Called Little Buttercup” from *H.M.S. Pinafore*; another AMICA-derived listing and the 120 Years of Electronic Music page call it “Poor Little Butterfly”. A later mirror even gives a different recording year. The clip itself must be checked before assigning a definitive title.
