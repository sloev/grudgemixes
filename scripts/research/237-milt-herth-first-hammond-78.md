Milt Herth saw the Hammond organ demonstrated in Chicago and decided that was what he wanted to play. He was hired as a radio organist in 1935, then made a series of solo Decca records that showed off the new machine as a novelty instrument.

On 11 June 1936 he recorded “Stompin’ at the Savoy”. The disc, Decca 911-A, matrix C90766, is often identified as the first Hammond-organ recording issued on a 78 rpm record. Herth does not play it like a church organ or a later jazz-organ trio: the sound is bright, busy and a little uncanny, as if a radio studio had acquired its own electric animal.

Herth later worked with Willie “The Lion” Smith and drummer O’Neil Spencer, and recorded a long run of novelty and swing sides. He died in 1969, leaving a small but revealing trace of the Hammond's first public life.
