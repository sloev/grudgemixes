Susette D’Alcorn, billed as Dot D’Alcorn, was a singer, pianist and male impersonator who became the stage electrician in *Mephisto*, an electromusical act she performed with her husband H. F. Juleene (John Parsons). They appeared as a demon and his electrical accomplice, playing a console that triggered musical and theatrical effects around the room. Advertisements called her “the one and only lady electrician in the world”.

The act borrowed heavily from Johann Baptist Schalkenbach’s electrical music-hall apparatus. Its particular twist was D’Alcorn herself: a woman performing the role of a practical electrician in a field marketed as scientific spectacle and masculine technical mastery. Juleene’s advertising even boasted that the act owed nothing to foreign instructors, a wonderfully defensive boast for an entertainment built on borrowed tricks and an exotic stage name.

The Cambridge research identifies D’Alcorn as an early professional female electric musician. Her name is misspelled as “Lusette” on the couple’s Wandsworth Cemetery headstone.
