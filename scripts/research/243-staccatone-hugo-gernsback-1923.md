Hugo Gernsback is remembered as a science-fiction publisher, radio evangelist and enthusiastic promoter of amateur electronics. In 1923 he and C. J. Fitch built the *Staccatone*, an electronic instrument with a sixteen-note keyboard and a single vacuum-tube oscillator. It was designed to be built by readers from the pages of Gernsback’s *Practical Electrics*, rather than bought from a musical-instrument shop.

Gernsback said he first played it publicly over New York radio station WJZ in November 1923. In April 1924 the Australian radio press described its tones as radically different from familiar musical sounds, while Gernsback later recalled lending it for a performance at New York’s Rialto Theatre. The Staccatone developed into the polyphonic *Pianorad* in 1926.

This is a rare early instrument with a DIY electronics culture around it: a science-fiction publisher turning his magazine readers into potential electronic musicians. It also points toward a different kind of outsider music — not a conservatory instrument, but a build-it-yourself noise machine.
