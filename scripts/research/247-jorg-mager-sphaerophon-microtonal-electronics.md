Jörg Mager was a Bavarian schoolteacher who became obsessed with the notes between the notes. After hearing an organ playing out of tune in the summer of 1911, he began building instruments for quarter-tone and microtonal music — pitches that the standard keyboard could not reach. He eventually abandoned the acoustic harmonium and turned to radio technology.

His 1921 *Elektrophon* used vacuum-tube oscillators and a hand-controlled pitch dial rather than a conventional keyboard. The later *Sphäraphon* and *Kurbelsphärophon* added timbral controls and a second tuning handle, but remained monophonic. In 1926 Mager demonstrated the instrument at the Donaueschingen festival, where its gliding, uncanny tone was overshadowed by the more fashionable mechanical-music concerts.

Mager had been involved in the failed 1918 Bavarian communist uprising and struggled to fund his research amid Weimar-era inflation and postwar shortages. He died in 1939. His instruments were built for a musical language that the available institutions and markets were not ready to support.
