Frédéric Kastner's *pyrophone* made music with fire. Glass pipes acted like organ pipes, but their sound came from hydrogen or coal-gas flames vibrating inside them. Kastner discovered that two flames could be made to sing when separated and fall silent when brought together. A keyboard controlled the flames; the pipes did the rest.

Kastner was born in Strasbourg, the son of composer Jean-Georges Kastner. He patented the instrument in 1873 and demonstrated it in Paris, London and at the 1876 Loan Collection of Scientific Apparatus. His father’s musical world and his own scientific experiments collided in a machine that was literally an organ of burning gas. The Science Museum still holds an 1873–76 example. Theodore Lack composed pieces specifically for it between 1876 and 1881.

Kastner also devised an electromagnetic mechanism that could operate the flame organ at a distance. This is a spectacular early electromechanical curiosity, but the sound itself was acoustic flame resonance, not electronic tone generation.
