In 1888 the journal *Iron* reported that an Italian engineer and performer named Carlo Bozza had invented a new musical instrument that could be played only by electricity. It was made of porcelain, and Bozza reportedly produced “excellent music” from it. That is nearly the whole surviving description.

The report is tantalising because it sits in the wake of Johann Baptist Schalkenbach’s electrical music-hall acts, when travelling performers were building strange hybrid instruments and theatrical effects. Bozza may have made a true electrical sound generator, an electrically activated resonator, or something else entirely. The source does not tell us.

The instrument is not yet a reliable catalogue entry: its mechanism, dimensions, repertoire, performance history and even whether it was a keyboard remain unknown. But the phrase “porcelain instrument, playable only by electricity” is exactly the sort of lost invention worth chasing through the 1888 trade press.

## Why this is not a public recording post yet

No surviving instrument, score, patent or recording has been identified. The Cambridge article cites the original report in *Iron*, 4 May 1888, page 31, issue 799. The next step is to locate the full periodical issue and trace Bozza through contemporary advertisements and exhibition reports.
