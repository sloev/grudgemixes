Charles William Holcroft (born 1858) appeared on the British stage as Herr Renier in 1887, one of a wave of electrical music-hall performers who followed Johann Baptist Schalkenbach. These acts turned a keyboard into a control panel for sounds scattered around a theatre: bells, drums, gongs and other effects could appear to play themselves, while the performer stayed at the console.

The details of Holcroft’s apparatus and repertoire remain frustratingly thin. The strongest current source identifies him as one of the near-identical electromusical acts that proliferated in the 1880s, after Schalkenbach advertised that he would supply instruments and instruction to imitators. That makes Renier a promising archival subject: a performer who borrowed a new technology and stage language, then disappeared from the standard histories.
