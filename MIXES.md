## What the series is for

A song can be a grievance put on the record by someone who had no court, newspaper or committee to file it with. The book (sloev/grudgebook) treats keeping a grudge as a practice with archives, committees and heirs; the mixes are the sound of that archive.

- **Grudge Mixes** is the formal register: commercial and field recordings made before 1926 in which an injury is named, an offender accused, a vow made or a debt carried down the generations.
- **Insane Music**, published as *Music in Spite*, is music made where it was not expected or not tolerated, by people who paid a price to make it anyway: in hospitals and settlements, under bans, in voices the culture resisted, on home tapes, or surviving only as found recordings.

What we want the listener to take away:

1. Resentment has a history and a craft; people organised and passed on grievances long before anyone studied it.
2. Who gets to keep a grudge in public has never been equal. A Red Seal tenor sang revenge for a living; a courtesan, a patient or a banned band paid to be heard.
3. Spite is not only small. On spitebites.party it is a joke; in these recordings it is also refusal and a record kept against forgetting.
4. No moral, no mockery. The mixes never tell anyone to forgive and never make fun of the singers.
5. The whole world, told precisely, with original-language titles and no culture standing in for a country.

## Curation rules

- **Diversity first.** Each volume spans at least three regions and two languages, and includes women's voices wherever the material exists. No volume may be more than half US or more than half one kind of setting.
- **Prisons and institutions are one strand, not the backbone.** At most one volume is mainly about confinement (Vol. 7), and it stays short.
- **Exact recordings only.** A track enters a volume only with an identified recording (label and matrix, or archive item). Leads without that go to reserves.
- **Tone.** Vols. 1–5 can share the spitebites wink. Vols. 6–10 keep the book's sober voice and are never sold as joke merchandise.
- **Consent.** Recordings of patients, prisoners, Indigenous communities and living people need the makers' or their community's consent, not only archive permission.
- **Clearance.** The publisher's list is built from these tracklists; ⚠ marks likely licence needs.

## Vol. 10 — Found (*Music in Spite*)

Recordings that survived by accident. Found-tape rule: publish only after a documented attempt to trace the maker, with a public "is this yours?" notice and royalties held; never private speech.

Anchors: Piłsudski's Ainu cylinders (1902–05, found in Poland 1977; ⚠ Ainu consent); "A FITA" protest cassette, Copacabana (IM-043); Ata Kak (IM-046); G.D.E. demo (IM-040). Remaining slots open, to be chosen with found-tape collectors (The Cassette Archive, Intertapes).
