# Candidate: “Jesse James” — Bentley Ball, Columbia A3085 (1919/1920)

**Evidence level: B — PROBABLE CANDIDATE. Publication: CORE DATE — VERIFIED (Columbia A3085 listed in contemporary trade press by 1919-08-15). Recording/fixation date: DATE UNCERTAIN (Smithsonian says 1920; Commons metadata says 1919). Audio: access derivative located; transfer provenance incomplete. Rights: not cleared.**

## Why it matters

This is not a revenge song in the narrow sense. It is a strong example of **public accusation and counter-insult**: the ballad condemns Robert Ford as a “dirty little coward,” foregrounds his betrayal of the hospitality and trust Jesse extended to him, and repeatedly names him as the person who killed Jesse. This is the narrator/community's public moral answer to a perceived betrayal. It fits the project's accusation, public shaming and betrayal-with-accusation categories, but should not be described as a literal retaliatory act or as proof of Jesse James's historical virtue.

This is precisely the sort of track a more flexible mix architecture can use: it broadens the emotional and rhetorical palette without pretending every song is about calculated revenge.

## Recording and object evidence

- **Performer:** Bentley Ball, vocal.
- **Title:** “Jesse James” (side 2 of Columbia A3085).
- **Carrier:** Columbia A3085, 10-inch 78rpm, paired with “The Dying Cowboy.”
- **Publication/issue evidence:** *The Talking Machine World*, 15 August 1919, lists Columbia **A3085** under “Bentley Ball Recordings for Educational Department,” naming “Jesse James (Cowboy Song)” and “The Dying Cowboy (Cowboy Song)” as a 10-inch record. This contemporary trade listing verifies publication by that date. https://www.worldradiohistory.com/Archive-Talking-Machine/10s/Talking-Machine-1919-08.pdf
- **Recording/fixation date:** Smithsonian National Museum of American History assigns **1920** as the recording date. Wikimedia Commons metadata for its Ogg file states **15 May 1919**. The August 1919 trade listing proves publication by that date, but does not establish when the master was recorded. Keep the fixation date **DATE UNCERTAIN**.
- **Matrix:** FAU Recorded Sound Archives item 19490 lists matrix 90038 for the album record page, which corresponds to the other side, “The Dying Cowboy.” Do **not** assign 90038 to “Jesse James.” The Jesse James side's matrix is not yet verified here; 90039 appears in secondary online discographical references and needs a primary label/runout or stronger discographical check.
- **Institutional object:** Smithsonian National Museum of American History object 1996.0320.11342 identifies Bentley Ball, Columbia A3085, 78rpm shellac, and a 1920 recording date. https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1282202

## Text and thematic evidence

John A. Lomax's *Cowboy Songs and Other Frontier Ballads* (New York: Sturgis & Walton, 1910), pp. 27–28, prints the ballad before the recording. It repeatedly calls Ford a “dirty little coward” and says he had eaten Jesse's bread and slept in his bed before killing him. Bentley Ball's own *The Song-a-logue of America* (digitized by the University of Rochester's Sibley Music Library) prints “Jesse James” on p. 31 and likewise reads “that dirty little coward.” This primary printed witness contradicts a secondary web account claiming Ball changed “dirty” to “mean”; it does **not** prove the exact words on the Columbia master. The text's moral argument is accusation through betrayal: the wrong is not merely the killing but the violation of intimate trust. The ballad also romanticizes James as a friend to the poor despite recounting his robberies; preserve that contradiction rather than repeating it as fact.

## Taxonomy and editorial role

**Primary:** public accusation / counter-insult; betrayal grievance.  
**Secondary:** outlaw commemoration and popular moral myth.  
**Not established:** revenge, retaliation by Jesse's supporters, or a historical verdict on Ford's motives.

Best placed in a sequence about **The Betrayal**, **The Public Answer**, or **The Accusation**, adjacent to more direct revenge and punishment tracks. It should not be used to claim that the project has found another literal revenge song.
