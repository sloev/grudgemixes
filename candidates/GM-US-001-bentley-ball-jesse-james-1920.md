# Candidate: “Jesse James” — Bentley Ball, Columbia A3085 (1919/1920)

**Evidence level: B — PROBABLE CANDIDATE. Date: CORE DATE — VERIFIED (year-level pre-1926; exact date conflict). Audio: access derivative located; transfer provenance incomplete. Rights: not cleared.**

## Why it matters

This is not a revenge song in the narrow sense. It is a strong example of **public accusation and counter-insult**: the ballad condemns Robert Ford as a “dirty little coward,” foregrounds his betrayal of the hospitality and trust Jesse extended to him, and repeatedly names him as the person who killed Jesse. This is the narrator/community's public moral answer to a perceived betrayal. It fits the project's accusation, public shaming and betrayal-with-accusation categories, but should not be described as a literal retaliatory act or as proof of Jesse James's historical virtue.

This is precisely the sort of track a more flexible mix architecture can use: it broadens the emotional and rhetorical palette without pretending every song is about calculated revenge.

## Text and thematic evidence

John A. Lomax's *Cowboy Songs and Other Frontier Ballads* (New York: Sturgis & Walton, 1910), pp. 27–28, prints the ballad before the recording. It repeatedly calls Ford a “dirty little coward” and says he had eaten Jesse's bread and slept in his bed before killing him. The text's moral argument is accusation through betrayal: the wrong is not merely the killing but the violation of intimate trust. The ballad also romanticizes James as a friend to the poor despite recounting his robberies; preserve that contradiction rather than repeating it as fact.

## Taxonomy and editorial role

**Primary:** public accusation / counter-insult; betrayal grievance.  
**Secondary:** outlaw commemoration and popular moral myth.  
**Not established:** revenge, retaliation by Jesse's supporters, or a historical verdict on Ford's motives.

Best placed in a sequence about **The Betrayal**, **The Public Answer**, or **The Accusation**, adjacent to more direct revenge and punishment tracks. It should not be used to claim that the project has found another literal revenge song.
