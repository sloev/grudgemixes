# Candidate lead: “Vengeance at Last” — Saint-Saëns, *Samson et Dalila* (1908)

**Status: PARTIALLY VERIFIED — exact archival item located; direct audio file and transfer/rights review still open. NOT CLEARED.**

## Why it matters

This is a strong early recording of an explicit revenge aria. Victor issued “Vengeance at Last” from *Samson and Delilah*, with baritone Emilio de Gogorza and contralto Louise Homer, accompanied by orchestra. The aria's original French incipit is “À moi l'honneur de la vengeance” (“The honour of vengeance is mine”); a contemporary Victor catalogue advertised the selection **in English**, so the actual recorded text should be checked against the digitized sound rather than inferred from the French libretto.

The scene is a fictional operatic conspiracy: Delilah and the High Priest plot to bring Samson down. It is not historical testimony about an actual grievance.

## Taxonomy

**Calculated revenge / conspiracy; betrayal as the precipitating grievance.** The High Priest and Delilah conspire to use Delilah's intimacy with Samson to discover the source of his strength and bring about his defeat. The aria frames this as vengeance for the Hebrew leader's opposition to the Philistines. It is a theatrical representation of revenge and political-religious conflict, not a documentary account.

Do not describe Delilah as merely “betraying Samson” without retaining the High Priest's role and the political conflict in the scene. The exact recorded excerpt and English translation must be verified from the sound.

## Liner-note direction (draft only; revise after listening)

In Saint-Saëns's *Samson et Dalila*, revenge is not a sudden response but a negotiated project. The High Priest seeks to neutralize Samson, whose strength and leadership threaten Philistine authority; Delilah offers the means. The duet's title, “Vengeance at Last,” makes the mechanism unusually legible, though the plot is more than a private love betrayal. The recording brings together Emilio de Gogorza and Louise Homer, two major operatic voices of the early Victor catalogue, in a performance recorded in Camden in May 1908. A contemporary Victor advertisement lists the selection in English, while the opera's French libretto gives Delilah the line “À moi l'honneur de la vengeance.” The recording therefore belongs to the history of operatic translation as well as to the history of recorded sound: the language heard on the disc must be transcribed from the actual transfer, not assumed from the source libretto. This is a staged revenge plot, not a historical report. Its value to the series lies in the explicit conversion of resentment into a coordinated plan.
