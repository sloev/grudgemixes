# Baltic pass: weirdos, home tapes and banned bands (Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania), 2026-11-20

Owner prompt: "I've heard Baltic nations have tons of weirdos." This is a first survey. Nothing has been listened to. Bands are living or have estates, so ⚠ on all.

## Why the Baltics fit the thin red line

In the Soviet Baltics, unofficial music was made against the state's tolerance. It circulated as *magnitizdat* (self-published tape copied hand to hand), and some bands were banned for what their audiences did. This covers three strands at once: *Made Anyway* (home reels), *Unwanted* (art-weird collectives) and *Fighting Words / music treated as violence* (bans after riots). It is the non-UK case the Hostile strand lacked.

## Leads

| Lead | Country | What happened | Strand | Visibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Propeller**, 1980 recordings (album *Propeller*, Fugata 1995) | Estonia | 22 Sept 1980: punk/art band at a Radio-vs-TV football match, Kadriorg Stadium, Tallinn. Officials cancelled the second set; a riot followed. The band was forced to disband, and Estonian Radio destroyed its recordings. The members had kept the original 1980 tapes, issued 1995. The unrest led to the "Letter of 40" intellectuals. Volkonski was banned as musician and actor until 1985. | Hostile (music treated as violence) + Found (tapes the state destroyed, kept privately) | HIGH PRIORITY |
| **Pērkons** ("Thunder"), home-studio recordings, 1981–85 | Latvia | Banned in 1983; carried on as the "Ensemble of the Soviet Latvia Collective Farm". After the Ogre concert of 6 July 1985, teenagers wrecked two train carriages; the band was banned again though not involved, and there were show trials. Filmed in Juris Podnieks's *Is It Easy to Be Young?* (1986). Early home-studio recordings spread hand to hand. Keyboardist Juris Kulakovs used a home-made synthesiser. | Hostile + Made Anyway | HIGH PRIORITY (well known in Latvia, little abroad) |
| **NSRD** (Nebijušu Sajūtu Restaurēšanas Darbnīca, "Workshop for the Restoration of Unfelt Feelings"), e.g. *Invalīdu tramvajs* ("Invalids' Tram", 1983), *Medicīna un māksla* (1985) | Latvia | Artist collective (Hardijs Lediņš 1955–2004, Juris Boiko 1954–2002) making reel-to-reel and tape-only experimental pop; annual Bolderāja railway walks; "Approximate Art". Unreleased December-session reels on **Underbed Storage**, a small Riga archive label that also reissues the old DIY label **T.U.M.S.A. Home Rec.** (1986–94). | Unwanted / Made Anyway | PROMISING (cult; STROOM compilation) |
| **T.U.M.S.A. Home Rec.** (1986–94) via Underbed Storage | Latvia | A home-recording DIY label; artists to identify. | Made Anyway / Found | **HIGH PRIORITY**: the obscure layer under NSRD |
| **Dzeltenie Pastnieki** ("Yellow Postmen"), home reels 1979–87 | Latvia | Home-recorded on consumer reel machines; spread by *magnitizdat* on reels and cassettes with no artwork. | Made Anyway | CONTEXT (now canonical) |
| **Ir Visa Tai Kas Yra Gražu Yra Gražu** ("And Everything That Is Beautiful Is Beautiful"), home tapes from 1987 | Lithuania | Painter Artūras "Šlipas" Šlipavičius and collector Artūras "Baras" Barysas (d. 2005). Many home tapes existed in a single copy. The rejected band name was *Užmušk Savo Artimą, Nes Ateis Tavo Tolimas Ir Užmuš Tave* ("Kill the close one, before the far one comes and kills you"). Strut box of home tapes. | Unwanted / Found | PROMISING |
| **Echidna Aukštyn** (Ramūnas Jaras), tape c.1993 | Lithuania | Described as a bizarre tape of creepy whispers, dark instrumentals, free improvisation, field recordings and flutes. In Don Campau's Living Archive of Underground Music. | Unwanted / Found | HIGH PRIORITY (very obscure) |

## Discovery sources

- Felix Kubin, *Parasol Elektroniczny: Rumours from the Eastern Underground*, #1 Estonia, #3 Latvia (RWM / MACBA podcasts).
- Radikaliai.lt, "A Place to Play Another Music", interviews with twelve Latvian and Lithuanian underground musicians. Estonian Melodiya branch cassettes; night-time studio sessions.
- Don Campau, Living Archive, "Northern Europe" cassette page.
- Tõnu Trubetsky, *Eesti punk 1976–1990* (2009); Vangonen, BA thesis on punk in the Estonian SSR (Tartu, 2015).
- Anthology of Estonian Traditional Music (folklore.ee), village singers who sang "at home", for any pre-1926 grievance songs.
