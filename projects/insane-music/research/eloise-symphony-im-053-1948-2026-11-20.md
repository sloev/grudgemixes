# IM-053 — “Selections from Eloise Symphony” (Ira Altshuler, 1948)

**Programme status:** IN PROGRESS  
**Catalogue status:** PARTIALLY VERIFIED  
**Pathway:** A — Institutional music  
**Environment:** Wayne County General Hospital / Eloise Hospital, Michigan, USA  
**Recording date:** 1948 (archive item date; exact session date unknown)  
**Recording identity:** CSU Archives item “Altshuler, Ira, Selections from Eloise Symphony, 1948,” Box 45, Folder 2, Subseries 1.13; linked digital-object record 2174. The collection's audio subseries says audiotapes are cassette unless otherwise noted, so cassette is the series-level carrier expectation, not a carrier inspection.

## What the evidence establishes

The CSU finding aid has an item-level record for **“Altshuler, Ira, Selections from Eloise Symphony, 1948”** in the Records of the American Music Therapy Association, within the 1948–1997 audio/video media series and the audiotape subseries. This is evidence of an identifiable archived recording, not merely a score or concert announcement.

A contemporary United Press story reproduced in the *Ranger Daily Times* on 1 September 1948 reports that a symphony co-composed by a former Eloise Hospital inpatient and Dr Ira M. Altshuler was scheduled to be performed by the Detroit Symphony Orchestra under Walter Poole. The report names the former inpatient, but this dossier uses the identity only to link the public contemporary report to the work; no diagnosis-based label is repeated. The article says the performance **would** take place; it does not confirm that it occurred.

The title and date of the CSU tape are consistent with the same work, but no source yet proves that the archived tape is the Detroit Symphony performance described in the newspaper. The tape's performer, exact session date, recording venue, recording generation and relation to the scheduled performance remain unknown. The work was co-created in the hospital environment; the archive recording should **not** be described as a resident-made performance. If it is the orchestra performance, it is a professional/visiting performance of a patient–therapist co-composition.

## Access, rights and limits

- The CSU finding-aid item page is accessible and links to a digital-object record; the file itself was not opened.
- The collection record indicates no access restrictions, but physical items are offsite and require advance notice.
- CSU's copyright policy states that the repository cannot grant or deny permission if it does not clearly hold copyright. No rights clearance was obtained.
- No audio was played, downloaded or copied; no contact was made; no physical archive access occurred.
- The contemporary newspaper story is a scheduled-performance report, not proof of the performance or of the identity of the archived recording.

## Qualification and next action

This is one qualified new lead because an item-level audio recording is catalogued and the work has a directly documented institutional origin. It is **not** counted as a patient-made performance or as a second lead for the newspaper report.

**Next:** with owner approval, request the digital object and item label; ask CSU to identify performer, session date, recording location, carrier generation and whether the tape is the Detroit Symphony performance. Confirm the compositional contribution and any rights/consent conditions before considering public use.
