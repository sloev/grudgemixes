# IM-052 — ZeN, *Bakırköy Akıl Hastanesi'nde* (Istanbul, 23 June 1998)

**Status:** PROMISING — extant live album and institutional performance are documented; first-edition provenance and original recording chain need final verification.  
**Visibility:** PROMISING / medium — a niche Turkish underground improvisation recording, reissued in a limited vinyl edition in 2023; not a high-priority obscurity claim.  
**Pathways:** A — institutional music (visiting performance); F — marginal / underground scene.  
**Critical distinction:** this is a visiting underground band performing in a psychiatric hospital, **not** a resident-made or patient-composed recording.

## Recording and event identity

ZeN's *Bakırköy Akıl Hastanesi'nde* is a seven-track live album, with a 1999 cassette/CD release and a 2023 double-LP reissue. The 1998 event took place in the Mazhar Osman Hall of Bakırköy Psychiatric Hospital, Istanbul. The specialist Guts of Darkness discography gives the date and time as 23 June 1998, 14:30, and lists a six-person lineup: Levent Akman, Murat Ertel, Emre Onel, Fahreddin Fahri Aykut, Merih Nafi Öztaylan and William Macbeth. Its seven-track listing totals 54:22. Spotify's 1999 album listing confirms seven tracks and a duration of 54:21; the 2023 Zel Zele reissue page also confirms a seven-track album, although some current reissue listings swap the order/durations of “Arkadaşım Ateş” and “Dut Ali”. Treat the exact 1999-vs-2023 sequence as a version question until the original CD/cassette and reissue sleeves are compared.

A contemporary *Roll* magazine interview in July 1998 records Murat Ertel's first-person account: a nurse named Nurten is identified as the caller (the wording also refers to nurses collectively); the group visited the hospital, spoke with staff and prepared a performance approach suited to the setting. Ertel's 2024 interview describes two nurses inviting the group. Treat the exact number of inviting staff as unresolved. The band intentionally avoided its more aggressive, dark or chaotic improvisations. The interview also records that the audience included patients, nurses, doctors and other visitors, with some leaving early and many staying. A second contemporary *Roll* article (August 1999) says the group had released the concert recording and describes an audience of roughly 100–150 patients, nurses and interested visitors. These sources establish the event as more than a retrospective promotional anecdote.

Murat Ertel's 2024 interview independently confirms that two nurses invited the group, that the concert was free, that the group visited the hospital beforehand, and that the performance used collective improvisation with improvised words and music. This differs slightly from the 1998 *Roll* account's singular named caller; preserve the discrepancy. He recalls a mixed audience response and confirms that the concert was filmed. The video is a separate audiovisual record; it is not assumed to be the same object as the audio master.

## Environment-first and ethical framing

The institutional connection is direct: the album records ZeN performing live inside Bakırköy Psychiatric Hospital for an audience including patients, doctors, nurses and visitors. The record's makers were the visiting band. There is no evidence that the patients composed, performed or recorded the music, and none is inferred. The band's underground/experimental status is independently supported by its contemporary Turkish music-press context and the record's limited specialist circulation.

Do not use the album as evidence of any patient's diagnosis, treatment response or musical ability. Do not name or speculate about patients; no patient identities are needed to document the recording. Avoid sensational labels such as “mad music” or “asylum music”. The album's improvised form and the musicians' adaptation to the room are relevant production facts, not diagnostic evidence.

## Version and release distinctions

- **Live event / recording:** 23 June 1998, Mazhar Osman Hall, Bakırköy Psychiatric Hospital; 16-track analogue recording is reported by a 2026 secondary account, which attributes the mix to bassist Bill Macbeth. This technical chain is not yet corroborated by a tape box or session document.
- **First issue:** 1999 cassette and CD. Readdork's release database identifies Kod Müzik KOD 009 for the original cassette/CD; Spotify's metadata gives 6 May 1999 and © 1999 ADA, while other databases list an Ada edition in 1998. The first-edition label/date discrepancy remains open.
- **Later reissues:** Ada Müzik CD reissue (2002, per Readdork); Ada Müzik and Zel Zele vinyl reissues (2023, with the Zel Zele edition limited to 300 copies according to its Bandcamp listing). These are later release objects, not separate live performances.
- **Tracklist:** seven titles. Current listings disagree on the order/duration pairing of “Arkadaşım Ateş” and “Dut Ali”; verify against original 1999 carrier and 2023 reissue before asserting a definitive sequence.
- **Video:** Murat Ertel says a concert video survives in the band's archive; it is a distinct audiovisual object, not an audio master or a verified public access route.
