# IM-046 — Ata Kak, *Obaa Sima* (1991–1993 home recording / 1994 Ghana cassette)

**Status:** WELL-SUPPORTED — extant seven-track recording and 1994 cassette issue documented by the artist's official reissue page and specialist reporting  
**Pathways:** C — self-taught practice; D — DIY/home-recorded and self-released music; E — idiosyncratic musical practice (use precise descriptions, not a diagnosis)  
**Region:** Ghana / Toronto, Canada  
**Object:** *Obaa Sima*, seven-track recording, originally issued in a very small cassette run in 1994

## Recording identity and production

Yaw Atta-Owusu, recording as Ata Kak, began recording his own songs around December 1991 after moving to Toronto. The artist's official Bandcamp page, which carries an extensive biography and release history, describes him assembling a home studio from second-hand equipment: Atari computer with Notator software, synthesizer with built-in drum sounds, reel-to-reel recorder and 12-channel mixer. He recorded in his apartment's living room and washroom with technical help from friend Yanson Nyantakye; Atta performed the lead vocals/raps and replaced the initial backing vocal with Lucy Quansah.

The seven tracks combine rap, techno/electronic dance elements, highlife-style refrains and Twi-language lyrics. The page describes the rapping as among the earliest examples of Twi rap in the emerging Ghanaian hiplife period. The artist's 2015 account says around 50 cassette copies were made and released in 1994, and that he believed about three sold, with the rest scattered among family and friends. A later 2025 account by Awesome Tapes From Africa founder Brian Shimkovitz says he learned during a Kumasi visit that Atta's brother had sold “quite a few” copies and that people in Kejetia market remembered the songs at funerals and local dance parties. These accounts conflict on circulation; retain the approximately 50-copy production figure as reported, but treat sales, distribution and local reach as unresolved.

## Version and provenance issues

- The 1994 cassette was self-released in Ghana after recording in Toronto. It is not a Ghanaian institutional or resident-made recording.
- The original master DAT later degraded and could not be recovered. The official 2015 reissue used the cassette copy that label founder Brian Shimkovitz had found at a roadside stall in Cape Coast in 2002; the artist's own cassette copy and other located copies were reported as too damaged for use.
- The official 2015 reissue page notes a speed discrepancy: Atta's original was slower than the version widely circulated by the label. The 2015 issue retained the faster version because slowing it revealed sonic problems. The 2025 anniversary page again states that the original recording was made at a slower speed and says a slow-version download is included. These are label/artist-side statements about the intended speed, not an independent comparison performed here. Keep the faster and slower presentations distinct; neither has been auditioned in this research.
- The 2025 album *Batakari* is a later work and must not be merged with *Obaa Sima*.

## Insane Music relevance

The recording qualifies through independently documented self-taught development, self-produced home recording and an unusually small self-release with a distinct Twi-language synthesis of local and transnational styles. Its significance is not that the artist was mentally ill or clinically “outsider”; no psychiatric claim is made. The project should document the specific DIY production and circulation history rather than apply a diagnosis or romanticise obscurity.

## 2025 source-copy discovery (addendum, 2026-11-17)

The artist-authorised Bandcamp page for *Obaa Sima (Anniversary Remaster)* identifies a 7 November 2025 release and describes the source as a “new, cleaner source cassette” / “nearly pristine.” In the first-person account on that page, Awesome Tapes From Africa founder Brian Shimkovitz says that in early 2024 a new acquaintance in his Berlin neighbourhood offered him a clean copy of *Obaa Sima*. The account does not identify how that cassette was acquired, when it was duplicated, or whether it was one of the approximately 50 Ghana-issued copies from 1994. The same 2025 page states that the original recording was at a slower speed and that a slow-version download is included.

This adds a **separate source-copy witness** to the version history:
- **2015 reissue:** sourced from the cassette Shimkovitz bought at a roadside stall in Cape Coast in 2002; the artist's own cassette and other copies were described as too damaged, and the master DAT had degraded.
- **2025 anniversary remaster:** sourced from the clean cassette offered in Berlin in early 2024; its generation and relationship to the 1994 run are not stated.

Do not treat the Berlin copy as the original master or as a confirmed first-generation 1994 cassette. Bandcamp lists the 2025 seven-track durations as 5:38, 5:33, 4:54, 2:45, 6:08, 4:32 and 5:28. The 2015 page lists 5:38, 5:32, 4:54, 2:49, 6:07, 4:30 and 5:26. These small catalogue-duration differences are comparison clues only; without an audio comparison they do not prove edits, speed changes or different performances.

The 2025 credits say all songs were written and composed by Yaw Atta-Owusu and that the reissue is under exclusive licence from him; the page also marks it “all rights reserved.” This is evidence about the 2025 reissue arrangement, not blanket permission to reuse the original 1994 recording, either source copy or the remaster.
