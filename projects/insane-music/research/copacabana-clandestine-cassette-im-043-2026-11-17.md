# IM-043 — The Copacabana clandestine cassette compilation (“A FITA”), Rio de Janeiro, c.1975

**Status:** PARTIALLY VERIFIED — extant cassette and digital copy reported in a peer-reviewed study; original compilers, exact creation date and full provenance unresolved  
**Visibility:** HIGH PRIORITY for underground circulation history; reassess after item-level access  
**Pathway:** D — DIY / self-duplicated circulation; F — underground political music network; environment tag: clandestine opposition safehouse  
**Object type:** 17-track cassette compilation, reportedly one of 50 identical copies

## Identification and evidence

Regina H. Castro McGowan's peer-reviewed article, “Protest Music, Cassette Tapes, and a Hideout Place in Copacabana,” *Opus* 30 (2024), DOI 10.20504/opus2024.30.16, documents a box of 50 identical, unbranded cassettes found in 1984 behind a false wardrobe compartment in an apartment in Copacabana, Rio de Janeiro. The author reports that her father, the building contractor who found them, brought the box home; she and her brother listened to them, and she later took one cassette to the United States and digitised it to CD with her husband. The article catalogues 17 tracks.

The study argues that the tapes were made and copied for clandestine political distribution, probably around 1975, because the most recent identified track, Chico Buarque's “Milagre brasileiro,” dates from 1975. The date is an inference, not a documented manufacture date. The article explicitly says the makers are unknown and that the 50 copies were never distributed.

**Primary scholarly source:** Regina H. Castro McGowan, “Protest Music, Cassette Tapes, and a Hideout Place in Copacabana,” *Opus* 30 (2024), pp. 1–25. https://doi.org/10.20504/opus2024.30.16  
**Full open-access article (Portuguese PDF):** https://www.anppom.com.br/revista/index.php/opus/article/download/opus2024.30.16/pdf

## Why it matters to Insane Music

This is a rare, material example of anonymous, small-run, politically clandestine cassette compilation and duplication. It documents a specific physical hiding place, 50 identical copies, a cross-border Latin American protest repertoire and an attempted distribution project. The cassette itself is a DIY/underground artefact even though most of the music was originally recorded and released by known artists elsewhere.

**Important distinction:** this is not a collection of music composed or performed by residents of the safehouse, and the cassette's presence there does not make the source recordings safehouse-made. The project's relevance is the anonymous compilation, copying, concealment and intended circulation. Keep that distinction visible in any catalogue description.

## Ethical, access and rights cautions

- The makers remain unidentified. Do not speculate about their names, organisations, fate or political affiliation beyond the author's cautious interpretation.
- The article's author appears to retain at least one cassette and reports a CD digitisation. No public audio file or open listening copy was located in this pass.
- Contact the author respectfully to ask whether the digitised copy survives, whether she is willing to discuss access, and whether any description or excerpt can be shared. Do not assume permission from publication of the article.
- Underlying recordings, compositions, translations, cassette compilation/sequence, physical carrier and digitisation may have different rights holders and terms. No reuse is cleared.
- Do not reproduce substantial lyrics from the article; create new transcriptions only after obtaining an authorised listening copy and assessing rights.

## Decision

Retain as IM-043, PARTIALLY VERIFIED. The cassette corpus and its 17-track programme are documented in a substantial peer-reviewed study; the exact object is reported to survive in digitised form. Its relevance is the clandestine compilation and copying network, not original performance by the people who hid it. No audio was accessed or auditioned and no contact or rights request was made in this pass.


## Research update — IM-043 source-text audit (2026-11-17)

The full Portuguese Opus article was checked directly, not only its abstract. It describes the 1984 discovery of fifty identical unbranded cassettes in a false compartment in a Copacabana apartment, the author's family listening to them, and the author later taking one cassette to the United States and digitising it to CD. It says the creators never returned and the cassettes were never distributed; their connection to a clandestine opposition safehouse is presented as a contextual interpretation, not an identified organisation or proven operational history. The article's account supports an extant cassette/copy and a 17-track programme, but not the identity of the compilers, exact creation date or source media for each track.

Retain IM-043 as RESEARCHED-OPEN. The evidence establishes a specific surviving compilation artefact and its reported history; the identities, exact assembly date, track-source provenance and physical-object access remain unresolved. The lead is about clandestine compilation/circulation, not original performances by the people who concealed the tapes.
