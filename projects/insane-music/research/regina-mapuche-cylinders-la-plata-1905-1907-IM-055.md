# IM-055 — Regina (Mapuche), songs recorded in La Plata, 1905–1907

**Project:** Insane Music (*Music in Spite*); also a possible Grudge Mixes context lead · **Status:** lead, consent-gated · **Downloaded:** No · **Added:** 2026-11-20 by Claude

## What it is

- Wax cylinders recorded by Robert Lehmann-Nitsche at the La Plata Museum, Argentina, in 1905 and 1907.
- The singers are two Mapuche captives of the "Campaña del Desierto" (1878–1885): Regina and Juan Salva (Juan Salva Marinau). Both had been taken to La Plata.
- Canio Llanquinao and Pozo Menares (Universidad Católica de Temuco) call these the first recordings of Mapuche song. Their 2014 article in *Revista Musical Chilena* 68, pp. 70–88, transcribes and translates two of them and was published with audio.
- **Tracks as described by the article:**
  - Cylinder 1: a *ngillatun* (prayer) by Regina.
  - Cylinder 2: "Canto de machitun".
  - Cylinder 3: a different song, possibly *ül* or *llamekan*, an everyday song.
  - Cylinders 4 onward: Juan Salva's songs and conversation.
  - The authors think the songs were probably *tayül* or *pillañtun*, which have sacred connotations.
- **Archive:** the originals are in the Berliner Phonogramm-Archiv (Ethnologisches Museum, now shown in the Humboldt Forum). "Araukaner" cylinders 1905–07, La Plata, inventory VII WS 192 (Deutsche Digitale Bibliothek).
- **Published audio:** cylinder 2 was issued on *Walzenaufnahmen aus Argentinien 1905–1909* (Berliner Phonogramm-Archiv, BPhA-WA4/5, 2009, 2 CDs).

## Why it matters

- **Regina** has no known surname. She was the only woman among Lehmann-Nitsche's Mapuche collaborators, and she lived in his household as a servant; he appears in her texts as "the captain". She survives on cylinders made by the man whose house she lived in, after a military campaign took her people's land.
- Lehmann-Nitsche is now criticised for treating Indigenous people at the museum as virtual slaves and for exhibiting their remains (en.wikipedia).
- The article reports that Regina says in one song that people "were moved" (*fueron trasladados*) to that place. Get the exact verse from the article before quoting.

## Why it may not become a track

- These are sacred songs recorded under coercion.
- Use requires the consent of Mapuche communities and authorities; the Temuco researchers are the natural first contact. Archive permission is not enough.
- Without that consent, this belongs in the book's text about the archive, not on a mix.
