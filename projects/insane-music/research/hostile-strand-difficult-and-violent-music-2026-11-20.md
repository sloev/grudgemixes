# The *Hostile* strand: difficult music, fighting words and music treated as violence

**Added 2026-11-20 by Claude at the owner's request** ("expand with difficult music and hostile/violent music"). **Status: proposal.** Nothing has been listened to.

## How it fits

*Unwanted* asked who decides what music is unwanted. *Hostile* asks what happens when music is aimed at someone. That is the grudge at its sharpest. It has three branches:

1. **Difficult music.** Music that attacks the listener's comfort, ear or patience on purpose, and audiences that fought back. Admit only where hostility between music and audience is documented (riots, bans, walkouts). "Hard to listen to" alone is not enough.
2. **Fighting words.** Hostility made into ritual: song duels, insult-song wars and battle traditions in which communities let enemies wound each other with song instead of weapons, or in which the song itself became the casus belli. This is the grudge as a social institution, and the strand's centre.
3. **Music treated as violence.** Music that states, police or courts treated as a weapon: banned, enjoined or prosecuted for what it was said to do. We document the treatment and the argument, not the threat.

## Hard limits

- **No track that glorifies or incites violence against real people, and nothing made as propaganda for mass violence** (such as songs broadcast to drive a genocide). Such material may be discussed in the book's text with care, never put on a mix.
- **No military or prison "music torture"** (owner's limit on confinement material; see *Unwanted* note).
- **Living artists in criminalised genres:** use only with their participation and in their framing; never use their legal trouble as the hook.
- Insult traditions name real people. Use only historic recordings whose targets cannot be harmed, and only with the community's consent.

## How it connects to the rest

- Greenland duels and *haló* are the oldest, most institutional form of the book's subject: communities that gave grievance a stage and rules.
- Russolo is "music made where it was not tolerated" turned outward: music meant to provoke.
- Drill is the state deciding a song is a weapon. It is the modern mirror of the 1919 ban on Zaghloul's name (GM-EG-003): in both, authorities tried to stop a song because of what it was believed to do.
