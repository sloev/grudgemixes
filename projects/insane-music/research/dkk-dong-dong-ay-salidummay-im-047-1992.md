# IM-047 — Dap-ayan ti Kultura iti Kordilyera, *Dong Dong Ay. Salidummay* (c. 1992 cassette)

**Status:** PARTIALLY VERIFIED — a distinct one-cassette release object is documented by ethnomusicological bibliography and an independent exhibition cassette inventory; exact issue date, label and audio lineage remain unresolved  
**Pathway:** F — community-organised cultural production and local cassette circulation  
**Place:** Baguio City / northern Luzon highlands, Cordillera Region, Philippines  
**Language:** multilingual Cordillera repertoire; track-level language attribution not established  
**Object:** one cassette, DKK / DKK Salidumay group, c. 1992

## Object identity and distinctness

Michiyo Yoneno-Reyes' dissertation lists *Dong Dong Ay. Salidummay* as an audiovisual work consulted: circa 1992, produced by Dap-ayan ti Kultura iti Kordilyera (DKK), one cassette, attributed to the DKK Salidumay group. The same bibliography separately lists the first *Salidumay* cassette circa 1989 and the 1995 *Salidumay. Elalay* cassette. The author states that DKK's cassette albums in the 1990s circulated mainly in Baguio City and the northern Luzon highlands and were among the relatively few locally produced and commodified cassette albums in that market.

The 2023 *Lakbayan: Voices of Resistance from the Philippines* exhibition catalogue independently lists the exact title *Dong-Dong-Ay Salidummay*, dated 1992, as a cassette by Salidummay / DKK. It places DKK's albums among works blending 1970s folk and Cordillera Indigenous traditions. This independent physical-media inventory supports a distinct cassette object, not merely a later streaming tracklist.

## Community and music context

DKK is a cultural alliance/sector associated with the Cordillera Peoples Alliance. The dissertation describes DKK's songs as fixed compositions with titles, lyrics, tunes and vocal/instrumental arrangements, and situates the cassette releases within a locally organised cultural movement responding to mega-dam projects, commercial logging and struggles for Cordillera autonomy. It also cautions against treating “salidummay” as one uniform, ancient pan-Cordillera genre. This dossier follows that caution: the album is a specific DKK cultural-production object, not a proxy for all Cordillera communities or Indigenous music.

## Date, version and provenance limits

- The dissertation gives circa 1992 and the exhibition catalogue gives 1992. Exact release date remains unknown.
- No cassette catalogue number, label/J-card image, matrix, tracklist, session date or individual performer credits were found in the sources checked.
- The album is distinct from the 1987/1989 *Salidummay* cassette (IM-042) by title and bibliography entry. Do not merge the two.
- No evidence currently establishes whether the 1992 cassette was recorded in one session, assembled from prior recordings, or transferred from another source.

## Access, ethics and rights

The exhibition catalogue describes a collection built from purchases from artists and people's organisations, private collectors and thrift shops, but this does not establish that the specific 1992 cassette is currently available for researcher access. The dissertation's bibliography establishes that the author consulted a cassette, not that it is available for copying. No audio was auditioned, no cassette was physically inspected, no contact made, and no reuse rights cleared.

## Next verification steps

1. Locate the physical cassette/J-card and record label, catalogue number, exact tracklist and any recording/production credits.
2. Compare the dissertation's consulted cassette with the 2023 exhibition item and identify whether they are the same physical copy or independent copies.
3. Obtain an authorised transfer or item-level audio access from DKK, CPA or Dang A Dang Radio.
4. Map the songs and language per track; preserve local authorship credits and avoid generalising the album as representative of the entire Cordillera region.
