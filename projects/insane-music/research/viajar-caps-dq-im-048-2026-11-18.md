# IM-048 — CAPS-DQ Santo André, *Viajar – Ida e Volta no Caminho do Inconsciente Poético e Musical*

**Project:** Insane Music  
**Status:** RESEARCHED-OPEN  
**Pathway:** community mental-health / youth social-service workshop; mixed participant-authored and invited-musician performance  
**Extant recording requirement:** contemporary reports document a released CD, but no surviving copy or audio transfer has been inspected in this work.

## Identity and chronology

The CD was made through the CAPS-DQ (Centro de Atenção Psicossocial para Dependentes Químicos) programme in Santo André, São Paulo state, Brazil. A Diário do Grande ABC report dated **23 November 2001** announced a launch at the Santo André Municipal Theatre and described a nine-song CD with approximately 500 copies to be distributed free to institutions and projects connected with drug-prevention and AIDS-prevention work. A second report dated **13 January 2002** gives the full title *Viajar – Ida e Volta no Caminho do Inconsciente Poético e Musical*, again identifies nine tracks, and says the workshop lyrics were written by students during 1999; regional musicians supplied volunteer arrangements and interpretations.

The contemporary reports support a **23 November 2001 launch** followed by a **January 2002 distribution/reporting window**. Keep those dates distinct; do not collapse them into a single generic 2002 release date.

## Music-making and participant relationship

The reports describe participant-authored lyrics developed through workshops, but the CD is not a nine-track document of resident performance. The 23 November report says the only composition interpreted by the group itself was the title song. The 13 January report identifies “Viajar” as performed by Jefferson Sooma and the CAPS-DQ choir, while naming regional guest interpreters for other tracks: Kléber Albuquerque (“Vem Menino”), Vasco Faé (“Coração de Pedra”), Fegato (“Sopa de Sapato”), Renê de França (“Cadeiras e Cadeias”), Monica Marzola (“Meu Sol”), K.Ram.K. (“Vento Esperando”), Fábio Vieira (“Retirante”) and Uafro (“Qual é a Sua Mau Elemento”). This is a mixed-authorship / mixed-performance object and should not be reduced either to outsider diagnosis or to a resident-only recording.

The same 2002 report says the songs' lyrics were written by students in the workshops in 1999 and quotes a verse from “Vento Esperando.” The quoted material is evidence of the project's expressive and social context, not a reason to infer a single political or clinical message for the whole CD.

## Circulation, access and rights

The 500-copy figure is a contemporary distribution estimate, not a present-day survival count. The CD was explicitly non-commercial in the 2001 report and was distributed through institutions and related projects; the 2002 report says other associations could request it from the municipal service. No current municipal request route, surviving copy, catalogue/sleeve record or authorised transfer was located in this pass.

No audio was downloaded or auditioned. No physical CD was inspected. No archive or participant was contacted. No rights or reuse permission was cleared. Public reporting and historical distribution language do not create current reuse permission.

## Status and next action

**RESEARCHED-OPEN.** Identity, contemporary chronology, nine-track count, workshop provenance and mixed participant/guest performance are supported by two contemporaneous reports. Remaining gaps are the surviving carrier, complete sleeve/catalogue data, exact master/transfer identity, present custody, full credits and rights status.

**Next:** locate a surviving CD or municipal/participant-held sleeve; obtain a complete nine-track list and credits; preserve the 2001 launch and 2002 distribution reports as separate evidence; request owner approval before any transfer or reuse.
