# “Dalvorig” / “Dalvorigtsi” — Mihran Damadian; Tashjian Columbia recordings, 1909 and 1910

**Status: STRONG LEAD / NOT CLEARED.** This is now the strongest Armenian-language revenge lead in the current research set. A 1924 printed songbook attributes the words to Mihran Damadian and gives the title as “Dalvorigtsi — The Son of Dalvorig”; a 1962 Armenian national-song anthology's English translation explicitly describes the fedayi as “wreaking revenge” on the Kurds and refers to the Khanasor episode. Tashjian Columbia recordings titled “Dalvorig” are documented in both 1909 and 1910. The crucial unresolved question is whether the exact recorded performances preserve the revenge verses found in the later printed text.

## Recording trail

The Armenian Museum of America / Harout Arakelian documents two possible Tashjian recording instances:

1. **Mardiros Der Sarkis Tashjian solo, 1909**
   - Columbia discography lists “Dalvorig” among his six 1909 Armenian-language solo recordings.
   - Matrix number is explicitly unknown in the museum discography.
   - Exact issue/catalogue number, take, session date and recording side remain unestablished.
2. **Tashjian Brothers, 1910**
   - Mardiros with brothers Nishan and Levon recorded 28 songs in autumn 1910; the museum says these were released in 1911.
   - “Dalvorig” is listed on Columbia E656, paired with “Grvetsek Dgherk.”
   - Individual matrix/take and side assignment are not supplied in the museum's discography.

Do not conflate these two recording instances. The 1909 solo item may be a different performance/arrangement from the 1910 trio version.

## Composition and text evidence

### 1924 printed score

The National Library of Armenia digital scan of *Ergaran* (1924), item No. 10, identifies:
- Title: **DALVORIGTSI — THE SON OF DALVORIG**
- Words: **M. DAMADIAN**
- Arrangement: **K. H. AIGOUNI**
- Copyright notice: 1923 by The Armenian Educational Foundation.

This is strong pre-cutoff evidence for a printed version and attribution of the words. It does not alone prove that the exact Columbia performance uses this precise arrangement or every verse in the 1924 score.

### Author eligibility

Mihran Damadian (1863/64–1945) was an Armenian writer, teacher and political activist. The 1945 death date is supported by Armenian biographical sources and a French biographical reference. This satisfies the project's author-death threshold for lyrics attributable to him. The composition's musical author/arranger should be kept distinct: K. H. Aigouni is credited as arranger in the 1924 score, not necessarily composer. The recording's arrangement may differ.

### 1962 lyric translation and revenge content

The 1962 *Armenian National Songs*, second edition, includes “Dalvorig” and prints the following English translation (James G. Mandalian translation, per the volume's introduction):
- “Torrents of hail pelted the plain of Khanasor”
- “Wreaking revenge, Dashnaktzakan Fedayee”
- “The soldiers of Andranik / Are wreaking revenge on the Kurds”
- The Mazrig Kurd is described as panic-stricken and being killed by fedayi bullets.
- A later stanza states that the Mazrig tribe was wiped out and Khanasor's green was painted red with blood.
- Another stanza says the “avenging Armenian brave” does not harm a Kurdish woman's hair.

The same anthology explains the 1897 Khanasor expedition as an ARF company attack against the Mazrig Kurdish tribe following an earlier ambush and destruction of a company of Armenian fedayees near the Monastery of St. Bartholomew. This is the anthology's historical framing; independent historical corroboration and the exact song's textual history should be assessed separately.

**Taxonomy:** **collective retaliation / revenge**, in a national-revolutionary song narrative. The song's own wording, as translated in 1962, frames the action as revenge for an earlier attack. It is not evidence that the song is a contemporaneous eyewitness account of the 1897 operation. The 1924 score and 1962 anthology are later witnesses to a song associated with a pre-1926 Columbia recording; do not silently project every later verse onto the 1909/1910 master.

### Related titles that must remain separate

- “Grvetsek Dgherk” (Tashjian Brothers Columbia E656) is a distinct paired title; Susan Paul Pattie's *The Armenian Legionnaires* gives its title gloss as “Fight on, lads!” This supports resistance/mobilization context, not necessarily revenge.
- “Kailerg Khanasori” (“March of Khanasor”) is a separate song in the 1962 anthology, with its own text and contextual note. It is not listed among the Tashjian Columbia 1909/1910 tracks and must not be conflated with “Dalvorig.”
- The 1909 solo and 1910 trio “Dalvorig” entries are separate recording instances until matrix/label evidence establishes their relation.

## Publication / fixation gate

- 1909 solo Columbia recording is documented by the museum, but its exact issue/catalogue evidence and matrix are incomplete.
- 1910 trio session is documented by the museum, with 1911 release year reported there. Seek contemporary advertisements/catalogue confirmation.
- The 1924 printed score establishes that a version of the song and Damadian attribution existed by 1924. It is not itself proof of the recording's exact text.
- The composition author death gate is provisionally satisfied for Damadian's words (d. 1945), subject to verifying that the Columbia performance uses his text. Arrangement credit must not be confused with composition authorship.

## Audio and rights

- No exact direct archival audio file for either Tashjian “Dalvorig” performance has been located.
- No original-shellac transfer chain, processing status or audio format is verified.
- No commercial rights are cleared for master, composition, performance, transfer, digitization, repository terms or territories.
- Museum discography, the 1924 score and 1962 songbook are evidence and discovery resources, not commercial audio source masters.

## Additional “Dalvorig” recording witnesses: Torcom Bezazian (1915–1916)

A cross-catalogue search finds at least two further pre-1926 recordings titled “Dalvorig,” performed by Torcom Bezazian. They must be treated as separate recording witnesses, not assumed to be the Tashjian/Damadian text.

1. **Columbia E2296, matrix 39813-1** — Recording Pioneers' Columbia E-Series spreadsheet lists “Dalvorig,” author **H. Simanian**, performer **Torcom Bezazian, baritone**, orchestra accompaniment, Armenian language, recorded **2 February 1915, New York City**. The paired matrix 39812-1 is “Voh, Intch Anouche,” also credited to H. Simanian. The spreadsheet is a specialist secondary discography assembled from scattered surviving sources; seek the physical label, runout and contemporary catalogue to confirm issue and exact title/credit.
2. **Victor 69189, matrix B18806-3** — 78opera's Torcom Bezazian discography lists a New York Victor session on **6 December 1916**: B18806-1 and -2 “Dalvorig (Sinanian)” unpublished; B18806-3 issued as **69189**. This independently establishes a later issued take according to that discography. No direct archival audio or primary label image located in this pass.

The 1915 and 1916 entries both credit **Sinanian**, not Mihran Damadian. “H. Simanian” in the Columbia list and “Sinanian” in the Victor list may refer to the same person or a related transliteration/credit, but that identity is not established here. Do not silently merge this attribution with the 1924 *Ergaran* score's “M. Damadian” lyrics. It is possible that “Dalvorig” is a shared title used for distinct songs, or that the later records represent an alternate text/arrangement; only lyric comparison can decide.

**Research implication:** the Bezazian Victor 69189 issue may offer a more tractable issued discographic object than the Tashjian items, but it still needs exact lyrics, composition-author identity/death evidence, a raw archival transfer and rights review. Keep it as a separate **LEAD / NOT CLEARED** until those checks are complete.
