# Bengal's early recorded *Bande Mataram* cluster (1905–1908)

**Status:** C — LEAD / archival cluster; not yet a mix-ready recording.
**Region/language:** Bengal; Bengali (with later multilingual variants).
**Editorial question:** can a recording whose political meaning lies in public performance, boycott and colonial suppression qualify under the expanded defiance/resistance branch, even though the lyric itself is primarily an invocation/praise of the motherland rather than a direct accusation or revenge narrative?

## Why this cluster matters

*Bande Mataram* became a rallying cry in the anti-partition and Swadeshi movement in Bengal. Government authorities restricted public chanting and singing in schools and colleges; contemporary historical accounts describe fines, threats to students' future employment, police violence and arrests. The song's political function therefore exceeded the text on the page: collective performance itself became a form of defiance. The 20 May 1906 Barisal procession and the restrictions imposed on public singing are described in the Government of India's Press Information Bureau historical note; use official statements cautiously and cross-check with scholarly histories of the Swadeshi movement. https://www.pib.gov.in/PressNoteDetails.aspx?ModuleId=3&NoteId=155910&id=155910&lang=2&reg=48 ; see also the historical study *Hindu-Muslim Relations in Swadeshi Bengal, 1903–1908*, https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001946467200900203 .

The National Institute of Science Communication / Indian Journal of History of Science article by Suvobrata Sarkar gives unusually useful recording-industry evidence. It says Hemendramohan Bose founded his phonographic business in 1905; in early 1906, at the height of anti-partition agitation, H. Bose's first batch of cylinder records was offered for sale, comprising patriotic songs sung by Rabindranath Tagore, Dwijendralal Roy and others. Bose later partnered with Pathé to transfer some cylinders to discs. One Pathé–H. Bose record has Tagore's recitation of *Sonār Tarī* on one side and *Bande Mataram* on the other; Sarkar identifies it as the oldest existing voice recording of Tagore. https://insa.nic.in/writereaddata/UpLoadedFiles/IJHS/Vol48_3_4_SSarkar.pdf (2013, pp. 463–64; online lines 184–186).

Suresh Chandvankar's 2014 Scroll article says H. Bose recorded patriotic songs and political speeches on cylinders and discs during the 1905 agitation; after police sealed his premises and destroyed equipment and records, only a short fragment of Tagore singing *Bande Mataram* survived. The article embeds the audio player. It also describes a separate National Grand Record: one side has Surendranath Banerjee speaking on Bengal's partition, the other a speech on *Bande Mataram*; the label survives, but the article reports no access to its audio. https://scroll.in/article/674383/record-discs-that-helped-defeat-the-british-empire-tagore-sings-bande-mataram (15 Aug 2014).

## Keep these recording branches separate

### 1. H. Bose cylinders / Tagore and other patriotic singers

- H. Bose cylinder catalogue numbers reported for Tagore's *Bande Mataram*: **250/350**. A cylindrography compiled from a 16 March 1906 newspaper list and later secondary reference is reproduced in the study *H. Bose — The Father of Indian Sound Recording*. https://www.academia.edu/30052851/H_Bose_The_Father_of_Indian_Sound_Recording .
- Sarkar's peer-reviewed article gives the stronger broad chronology: first batch of H. Bose cylinders offered early 1906, patriotic songs by Tagore, Dwijendralal Roy and others.
- The cylinders should be treated as lost/unavailable unless a specific surviving cylinder with playable audio is identified. Suresh Chandvankar's 1992 collector-journal essay explicitly asks where the celebrated Tagore cylinder recording is and whether it was preserved/accessibly held; it does not establish that a playable cylinder survives. https://dsal.uchicago.edu/books/trn/pdfs/trn_05.pdf (pp. 12–13; especially lines 326–342).

### 2. Pathé–H. Bose disc transfer of Tagore's *Bande Mataram*

- A surviving 2:46 excerpt of Tagore singing *Bande Mataram* is reported by Chandvankar as the sole surviving audible remnant from the H. Bose patriotic recording programme.
- Sarkar independently documents the two-sided Pathé–H. Bose disc with *Sonār Tarī* recitation on one side and *Bande Mataram* on the other.
- A collector discography compiled by Suresh Chandvankar and circulated on the Society of Indian Record Collectors list reports Pathé/H. Bose numbers **3511**, **36-250** and **36-350**, circa 1908, but this list is secondary and the relation among the variants needs physical-label confirmation. https://groups.google.com/g/rec.music.indian.misc/c/0wuPjw-unSs .
- A Scroll article includes an audio player for the short surviving Tagore fragment, but that is not proof of access to a complete, unedited archival master or to the original shellac/cylinder transfer chain. Inspect the recording and identify the source carrier before claiming exact issue/transfer provenance.

### 3. National Grand Record: Surendranath Banerjee / *Bande Mataram* speech

- Chandvankar reports a Beka-associated National Grand Record with Surendranath Banerjee's speech on the partition of Bengal on one side and a speech on *Bande Mataram* on the other.
- A few copies reportedly survive; the article provides a label image but says it has no audio access. This is spoken political material, not the song itself. Keep it as a distinct spoken-word lead, not as an alternate pressing of the Tagore song.
- Need original label photograph, catalogue number, matrix, recording/issue date, side transcription and a verifiable audio transfer.

### 4. Binapani Recording Company advertisement, January 1908

Michael Kinnear's article “The First Indian Disc Record Manufacturers,” *The Record News* 5 (Jan. 1992), documents an advertisement in *The Bengalee* dated 23 January 1908. It promotes “Swadeshi Binapani Records” under a prominent “Bande Mataram!” heading, explicitly sells them as indigenous records, and invokes the pride of the country and glory of the motherland. The advertisement is direct evidence of a planned/marketed *Bande Mataram* record product and nationalist commercial framing, but the source does not identify an issue number, performer, exact recorded content or surviving copy. Kinnear says the company and its activities became obscure and that it is uncertain whether the advertised venture continued. The same article says government proscription of public performances of *Bande Mataram* and *Amar Desh* called for prohibitions on recording, playing and selling discs, but explicitly acknowledges gaps in the documentation. https://dsal.uchicago.edu/books/trn/pdfs/trn_05.pdf (pp. 39–42; lines 820–887, 914–921).

### 5. Nicole / Gramophone / other catalogue variants

A discography attributed to Suresh Chandvankar and circulated on a collector discussion list reports:
- Nicole Records **C 465**, Narain Chandra Mukherjee, c.1905;
- Nicole Records **C 436**, same singer, c.1907;
- Gramophone Monarch **012112**, R. N. Bose, 1906;
- Gramophone Concert **5-12638** and **5-12639**, Narain Chandra Mukherjee, 1906;
- National Grand Record **20290**, Surendranath Banerjee;
- Beka Grand **21125**, Hem Chandra Sen, 1908;
- National Record **462/541**, Narain Chandra Mukherjee, 1907;
- Odeon **94131**, Satya Bhushan Gupta, 1911;
- Viel-O-Phone **T 6003**, Desh Dass, 1912.
The list is a useful search index, not sufficient proof for each date, recording identity, or survival. Its author expressly says the list may be incomplete. https://groups.google.com/g/rec.music.indian.misc/c/0wuPjw-unSs . Cross-check issue numbers against the original labels, catalogues, matrix records and audio; do not merge these variants.

## Thematic and editorial assessment

- **Strong contextual defiance/resistance:** singing and distributing *Bande Mataram* in the anti-partition movement; public performance was itself politically charged and subject to colonial suppression.
- **Textual nuance:** the well-known lyric is chiefly an invocation and praise of the motherland. It is not, in its familiar opening stanzas, a direct narrative of a particular injury, an accusation against a named oppressor, or a revenge vow. Its eligibility rests on its historically documented performative role as protest/defiance, not on treating every patriotic song as a grudge song.
- **Candidate level:** C — LEAD for the cluster. The Tagore fragment is the strongest audio lead, but exact source carrier, full version, lyric completeness and rights are not established here. The National Grand Record is a separate spoken-word lead. Binapani is an advertisement-only issue lead.
- **Rights:** no rights clearance asserted. A historic recording may have different rights in the performance, composition, transfer, physical master and territories. Public availability of a short excerpt does not imply reuse clearance.

## Priority verification tasks

1. Obtain the Tagore fragment's source and carrier metadata; identify whether the surviving audio derives from the Pathé–H. Bose disc and match its label/issue number.
2. Locate an extant original Pathé–H. Bose disc (reportedly numbers 3511 / 36-250 / 36-350) and inspect both sides, label, matrix/runout and transfer provenance.
3. Ask the National Grand Record disc's current holder for both-side photographs and whether any transfer exists; verify number/matrix and date.
4. Locate the 23 January 1908 *Bengalee* original advertisement and any Binapani catalogue, prospectus, label or disc; do not assume the advertised product necessarily reached market.
5. Reconcile the Nicole C465/C436 and Gramophone 012112 / 5-12638 / 5-12639 catalogue entries against the original 1904–1912 Indian catalogues and extant discs.
6. Transcribe the exact recorded stanza(s) in Bengali and compare the sound with the source text; identify who selected/composed the tune for each version.
