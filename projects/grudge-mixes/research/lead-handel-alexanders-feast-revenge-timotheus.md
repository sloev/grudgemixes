# Research lead: “Revenge! Timotheus cries” — Handel, *Alexander’s Feast*

**Status: LEAD — not a verified candidate; NOT CLEARED.**

## Why it matters

The aria is a direct thematic fit: its text explicitly stages revenge and vengeance. It is a literary/classical representation, not evidence of an actual grievance or a contemporary act of revenge.

Two early commercial recording paths have now surfaced:

1. **HMV / Gramophone Company, London, 24 October 1921** — HMV D589 / side 02927 is associated with matrix **Cc584**, but the performer and take are disputed. The Alan Kelly matrix catalogue lists Cc584-1 as the issued matrix and credits **The Gresham Singers (p Mrs Baker)**. The Robert Radford discography instead lists Cc584-2 for the same issue under Radford's discography. A third sessionography marks Cc584-1 unpublished and Cc584-2 issued. Do not assign performer or take until the original label/runout or company ledger is checked.
2. **Vocalion X-9560** — the 1925 UK catalogue lists bass Malcolm McEachern with the Aeolian Orchestra, 10-inch. A specialist session listing tentatively places the recording in London in October 1924, matrices/session numbers 03724/03725.

Neither exact master has yet been located as an archival audio object.

## Evidence located

### HMV 1921 — preferred search target

- The Alan Kelly-compiled *Gramophone Company Limited / His Master's Voice Matrix Series prefixed Bb/Cc* catalogue lists **Cc584-1**, recorded 24-10-21, issued as **02927 D589**, and attributes the selection to **The Gresham Singers (p Mrs Baker)**: https://www.gramophonecompanydiscography.com/PDF/File%20D%20-%20BB.pdf
- The *Robert Radford Discography* lists item 302, “Alexander’s Feast: Revenge, Timotheus cries (Handel),” with **Cc584-2, 24 Oct 21, 02927 D589**. Since this is a Radford discography, it implies Radford performed the selection, but conflicts with the Kelly matrix catalogue's take and performer attribution: https://www.musicweb-international.com/hooey/radford-discog.htm
- The *78opera* sessionography marks **Cc584-1** unpublished and **Cc584-2** as D589 / 02927, agreeing with the Radford discography on the issued take but not the Kelly catalogue: https://www.78opera.com/files/ARTIST_R.pdf
- The current safe conclusion: HMV D589 / 02927 is a genuine pre-1926 issue lead; performer and exact take are unresolved pending primary label/runout or ledger evidence.

### Vocalion 1924/1925 — alternate recording

- The cited Vocalion catalogue is a **1926 edition**, not a catalogue published in 1925; its foreword says it covers records issued through November 1925. The X-9560 entry gives the title, work, performer, accompaniment and catalogue number: https://78rpm.club/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/Vocalion-Records-Catalogue-1925-UK.pdf
- The catalogue's artist entry identifies **Parts I & II**, says “to complete aria replay Part I,” and credits bass Malcolm McEachern with the Aeolian Orchestra. Its alphabetical title index independently repeats X-9560, 10-inch, performer and accompaniment. This is strong issue-level catalogue evidence, but not a physical inspection of the disc.
- The specialist Vocalion sessionography groups **03724** (“Revenge Timotheus cries, pt 1 & 3”) and **03725X** (“d:o”) under a tentative “Vocalion, London 1924-10?” session; it lists **03725** (“Behold a ghastly band, pt 2”) as unpublished: https://www.78opera.com/files/ARTIST_M.pdf. The sessionography's “pt 1 & 3” wording is not transparently consistent with the catalogue's “Parts I & II”; do not normalize this discrepancy without examining the original record and matrices. The X suffix is presented as a matrix variant/issued take in that discography, not proof of a specific physical pressing.
- A secondary McEachern discography lists X-9560 with 03724/03725 and a combined “pt 1 & 3 pt 2” description: https://classicmusiccds.com/product/australian-bass-malcolm-mceachern-1883-1945-vol-1-cdr/. It is not an independent primary source and does not resolve the side/part wording.
- AWA's *Arias* track listing includes “Alexander's Feast: Behold, A Ghastly Band . . . Revenge Timotheus Cries” at 4:51: https://prd-s.awa.fm/track/95f94923e616e638ed09. This is a later digital-listing lead only. The page does not identify Vocalion X-9560, matrix, source carrier, transfer engineer, processing or rights chain; it cannot yet be treated as an authenticated reissue of this exact 78.
- A commercial CDR seller lists the X-9560 / 03724 / 03725 sequence on a McEachern compilation: https://classicmusiccds.com/product/australian-bass-malcolm-mceachern-1883-1945-vol-1-cdr/. This is a discovery clue, not reliable transfer provenance or permission to reuse.
- No item-level physical label photograph, original X-9560 carrier, archive audio object or documented transfer chain was located in this search pass. Exact matrices, part sequence, take and audio identity remain unresolved.


### Composition and text

- The aria text begins “Revenge, revenge, Timotheus cries” and describes the Furies and the unburied Greek dead: https://www.vmii.org/hwv-75-alexanders-feast/21-revenge-timotheus-cries
- Newburgh Hamilton wrote the text; Handel (1685–1759) composed the work. Both died well before the composition cutoff: https://www.vmii.org/hwv-75-alexanders-feast/21-revenge-timotheus-cries

## Taxonomy

**Represented revenge / collective vengeance rhetoric.** The aria describes the dead Greek soldiers as returning in spectral form and calls for revenge. The wider oratorio frames Alexander’s destruction of Persepolis after the Persian invasion of Greece; avoid flattening this into a simple historical record. The text is an eighteenth-century literary treatment of an ancient episode.
