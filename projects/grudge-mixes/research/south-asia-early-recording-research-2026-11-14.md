# South Asia early-recording research note — 2026-11-14

## Scope and result

This pass examined the early commercial recording infrastructure in India and the catalogue routes most likely to reveal grievance-related songs. It did **not** verify a specific eligible revenge, retaliation, spite, punishment or grudge recording. No candidate is admitted from this pass.

## Why the region is a high-value search area

The Gramophone Company's first major Indian recording expedition took place in 1902–03. A scholarly account reproducing the expedition chronology reports 554 master recordings in Calcutta in November 1902, followed by sessions in Tokyo, Shanghai, Hong Kong, Singapore, Bangkok and Rangoon. It notes that wax masters were shipped to Hannover for pressing and that finished records returned to Asian markets several months later. This is important for separating the recording/fixation date from the commercial issue date.

## Search routes and boundaries

1. **Gramophone Company Discography:** searchable by performer, title, matrix, issue number, recording date and location. It is global rather than India-specific, and its own documentation warns that title/name normalisation is imperfect. Query alternate spellings and transliterations; verify against specialist Indian discographies and label/runout evidence.
   https://www.gramophonecompanydiscography.com/

2. **Kinnear's India discographies:** the 1899–1908 volume covers the early tours and catalogue history; the 1908–1910 volume is described as a complete numerical catalogue by matrix serials. These are key references for issue and matrix verification, though full access may require library consultation.

3. **UCSB discographical guide:** its India section provides a chronology and bibliography and flags the complexity of the Indian recording market. Use it to identify label and regional bibliographic leads, not as a substitute for a specific matrix record.
   https://adp-assets.library.ucsb.edu/Ethnic_and_Vernacular_Music.pdf

4. **Archive of Indian Music:** a current preservation initiative reporting more than 2,000 restored recordings and including historically important early commercial recordings, theatre and multilingual material. Treat its modern restorations as discovery/listening leads until transfer provenance, source disc and commercial-use terms are established.
   https://fihcr.com/archive-for-indian-music/

5. **Young India / Endangered Archives Programme:** valuable catalogue and audio digitisation, but the National Gramophone Record Manufacturing Company operated 1935–1955. This collection is outside the present pre-1926 master cutoff and should not be mistaken for an early-recording source.
   https://southasiacommons.net/collections/40855/digitising-archival-material-pertaining-to-young-india-label-gramophone-records-archival-material-relating-to-the-young-india-gramophone-record-label-catalogues-and-promotional-material/

## Search findings and caution

- The 1902–03 expedition's large output makes India and the wider Asian tour a high-priority region. But a session date does not establish that a particular master was issued before 1926; pressing and distribution could follow months later.
- The expedition also recorded artists in several Asian markets. Search results should not be geographically assigned solely from the expedition itinerary: establish the artist, language, recording location and matrix for each item.
- The Young India archive's large number of titles is not useful for the current date gate because the label's production began in 1935.
- Search terms must include original scripts and genre-specific terms. English words such as “revenge” and generic transliterations such as “badla” are inadequate by themselves. Obtain language-specialist review of title, lyrics, idiom and cultural category before assigning taxonomy.
- No audio object reviewed in this pass was established as an unedited archival transfer of the original shellac disc, and no commercial reuse clearance was established.
