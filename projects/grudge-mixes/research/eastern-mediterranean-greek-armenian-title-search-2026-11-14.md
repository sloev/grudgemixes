# Eastern Mediterranean title-search pass: Greek and Ottoman leads (2026-11-14)

## Scope
Follow-up to the Ottoman/Eastern Mediterranean matrix map. Searched early Turkish/Greek/Armenian/Arabic grievance and revenge terminology, Oriental HMV matrix chronology, Greek 78 catalogue records, and the Gramophone Company Discography. This is a discovery pass, not a candidate clearance exercise.

## Results

### Discographical infrastructure
- The Gramophone Company Discography exposes searchable fields for performer, title, matrix, issue number, recording date and location. Its own introduction cautions that the source Kelly files were unstructured and that names/titles are not consistently standardized. It is therefore a useful index, not a substitute for labels, ledgers or audio. https://gramophonecompanydiscography.com/
- Strötbaum's Oriental HMV matrix chart covers Turkish, Greek, Arabian, Hebrew, Sephardic, Armenian, Albanian, Serbian, Bulgarian, Kurdish and Laz repertoire, with sessions from Constantinople in 1900 onward and later campaigns in Cairo, Beirut, Smyrna, Salonica and Athens. The chart gives session/matrix ranges, but not proof of publication for an individual master. https://www.recordingpioneers.com/docs/Oriental-HMV-CHARTS-1900-1919.pdf
- Strötbaum's Turkish/Greek terminology guide is explicitly a preliminary, non-exhaustive glossary. It notes that early label transliterations and descriptions can be imprecise, and that repertoire circulated across multilingual Ottoman urban centres. Treat title transliterations and ethnic labels as hypotheses to verify, not settled identifications. https://www.recordingpioneers.com/docs/grurks/TERMINOLOGY1.pdf

### Greek archive search: no eligible candidate
Searches surfaced grievance-sounding Greek titles, but the identifiable records were too late for the current master cutoff:
- “Ainte na pethaneis” (HMV AO-200; matrix BF-748), Antonis Ntalgkas, Athens, 1927. The title is a hostile death-wish, but 1927 is outside the before-1926-01-01 rule. https://vmrebetiko.aegean.gr/en/item-en/?id=10112
- “Ante re chira aponi” (Columbia 8277; matrix W 20355), Dimitris Arapakis, Athens, 1928–29; archive tags widowhood. Outside cutoff. https://vmrebetiko.aegean.gr/en/item-en/?id=9552
- “Mi thes na tyrannioumai” (Orthophonic S-415-B; matrix BS-023125), Athens, 1938. Far outside cutoff; useful only as later contextual material. https://vmrebetiko.gr/en/item-en?id=9732
- Other surfaced Greek records (e.g. “Mparmpa Giannis,” Victor 68701-B, New York 1925-02-24) do fall before the cutoff, but the displayed lyrics concern hashish and death, not a grievance. Do not admit merely because death appears in the lyric. https://vmrebetiko.aegean.gr/en/item-en/?id=4250

The Kounadis Archive records can expose original-language lyrics, performer, composer, matrix, catalogue number, date, location and sometimes a direct audio file. However, these search results do not yet establish that a pre-1926 title concerns retaliation, revenge, punishment or a maintained grievance. Continue title/lyric-level searches rather than relying on tags or translated titles.

### Armenian-American diaspora recordings
Yıldız's 2024 article documents a substantial body of Armenian migrant commercial 78s, many recorded in the United States and performed largely in Turkish, with Armenian and Kurdish also represented. It lists Harput-region repertoire and performers including Vartan Margosian, Kaspar Janjanian and Kemany Minas; examples include “Darsim” and “Daghlar Daghladi Beni.” These are valuable leads for searching songs of displacement, accusation, lament and local conflict, but the article's displayed examples do not establish a revenge/grudge lyric. Nor should a Turkish-language performance by an Armenian migrant be relabelled as ethnically Turkish. https://journals.uni-goettingen.de/wom/article/download/1868/1571?inline=1

## Status and decisions
- No new candidate admitted.
- No existing candidate upgraded.
- No exact raw archival transfer or commercial rights basis established in this pass.
- Do not infer revenge from a title, a death-wish, widowhood tag, or generic lament. Require original-language lyric evidence and contextual interpretation.
- Keep recording date, issue/publication date, and later reissue date separate.
