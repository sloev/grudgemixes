# Arabic early-recording screening — first pass (2026-11-15)

Status: **NO CANDIDATE ADMITTED**. This is an archive and title-screening note, not a claim that the Arabic early-recording field has been exhausted.

## Scope and method

The pass began with AMAR Foundation for Arab Music Archiving & Research and Harvard Loeb Music Library's Arabic 78 collection. Searches used Arabic and transliterated forms associated with revenge, retaliation, blood, feud and grievance (including ثأر, انتقام, دم, خصومة), alongside archive-specific title searches. Search-engine indexing is incomplete; absence of a hit is not evidence that the archives contain no relevant song. The next pass should use archive-native filters and manual lyric review.

## Archive reach and limits

- **AMAR** describes a collection of approximately 10,000 78rpm discs, mainly from the Nahda era (1903–1930), and 6,000+ hours of reel/tape recordings. Its public record-company overview presents a selection of 205 discs with short audio excerpts; its radio service streams selections from the archive. This is a large research collection, but not all holdings are exposed as searchable item-level audio online. Sources: https://www.amar-foundation.org/about/ ; https://www.amar-foundation.org/record-companies/ ; https://www.amar-foundation.org/listen/ .
- **Harvard Loeb** describes nearly 600 catalogued Arab and Arab-American recordings, spanning approximately 1903 through the 1950s. Its public Aviary release is a small subset. Harvard notes that some discs published before 1923 can be shared in the United States under the federal pre-1972 recording rules, but this is not a worldwide clearance statement. Sources: https://archive.blogs.harvard.edu/loebmusic/2022/01/19/early-arabic-sound-recordings-and-the-public-domain/ ; https://harvardmusiclib.aviaryplatform.com/ .
- Harvard's 2017 overview lists forms including al-mawwāl, qaṣīdah, muwashshaḥ, ṭaqṭūqah and taqsīm, and documents multinational labels alongside Baidaphon and Fabrik Mechian. These local forms and label histories are useful search paths, but do not themselves imply grievance content. Source: https://archive.blogs.harvard.edu/loebmusic/2017/09/05/arabic-78-rpm-records-collection-a-newly-catalogued-treasure/ .

## Items inspected as controls / non-candidates

1. **“Asl al-Gharam nazra”**, Odeon, 1905, Harvard Loeb item AWM 78-101. Harvard highlights this as one of the earliest records in its collection. The title is a love-song phrase (“the origin of love is a glance”); no revenge mechanism has been established. It is a useful control showing that words around love/longing should not be mistaken for grievance. No candidate.
2. **“Khallayānī bilawʻātī”**, Gramophone Company master recorded 1910, later appearing under the New York pirate label Opera Disc in the early 1920s; Harvard identifies original matrix 11-12490 under the label. This is a useful discographical case for separating original master, later label and issue history. No lyric transcription or revenge evidence was established in this pass. Do not infer taxonomy from the title alone. No candidate.
3. **Salāmah Ḥijāzī repertoire**: Harvard identifies him among the early Egyptian theatrical performers represented in its collection; AMAR provides a research biography and early artist/record listings. His theatrical-song repertoire is a promising next search route because recorded play excerpts can preserve dramatic revenge plots, but no exact pre-1926 revenge number/master was verified here. No candidate.

## Decisions

- No Arabic item is upgraded to LEAD or candidate status in this pass. There is not yet an exact track with verified pre-1926 issue/fixation, identifiable composition author/provenance, relevant lyrics, an exact archival transfer and a rights assessment.
- Keep **spite**, **retaliation**, **revenge**, **punishment**, **lament** and **protest** distinct. A word such as دم (blood) may occur in love poetry, elegy, religious poetry or political rhetoric; it is not sufficient by itself to classify a song.
- Harvard's U.S. public-domain guidance for pre-1923 sound recordings must not be carried over as global or EU commercial clearance. AMAR and Harvard access terms and digitisation rights need separate review.

## Next actions

1. Search Harvard Aviary's downloadable subset by Arabic title, artist and record label; inspect item metadata and listen where accessible. Prioritise dramatic excerpts and qaṣīdah / mawwāl texts with explicit prior injury and payback.
2. Use AMAR's artist pages and listening room to follow Salāmah Ḥijāzī, early theatre singers and songwriters; request catalogue or item-level access from the archive for relevant pre-1926 holdings not exposed online.
3. Search Arabic printed songbooks and theatre libretti for exact lyric matches before assigning a recording.
4. For any promising title, establish original label, matrix, recording session, issue date, exact audio source, author/composer death or traditional provenance, and territory-specific rights before candidate admission.
