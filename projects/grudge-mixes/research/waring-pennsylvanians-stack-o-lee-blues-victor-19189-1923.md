## Exact recording identification

The 2026 edition of *Jazz and Ragtime Records (1897–1942)* lists Fred Waring's Pennsylvanians, “Stack O'Lee Blues,” matrix **28665-5**, Victor **19189**, recorded in Camden, New Jersey, **16 October 1923**. Its session personnel listing includes Fred Waring (director), Tom Waring (piano), and the Pennsylvanians; the listing is session-level and does not by itself prove a vocal part on this specific title.

A specialist Stagger Lee chronology also identifies the 16 October 1923 Waring recording and says the first two 1923 “Stack O'Lee Blues” recordings were instrumentals. The North American Ballad and Song Database indexes Waring's Victor 19189 recording under **Stagolee (Stackerlee), Laws I15**, and summarises the underlying narrative: Lee Shelton shot Billy Lyons after a dispute in which Lyons knocked off Shelton's hat. The recording title is therefore linked to the Stagolee ballad family, but the Waring issue is a dance-band fox trot / instrumental rather than a lyric-bearing version.

## Thematic assessment

The Stagolee ballad family preserves a violent grievance narrative, but this specific 1923 Waring recording is described by the historical sources as an instrumental fox trot. It has no verified sung text on which to assess accusation, retaliation, punishment or spite. The historical incident itself is a killing after a personal dispute, not automatically a revenge narrative. Under the project's core–narrative–context distinction, this recording is at most a **context/version witness** that shows how a Black American folk narrative entered mainstream dance-band recording. It should not be described as an audible revenge song or counted as a qualified new lead without further evidence and editorial approval.
