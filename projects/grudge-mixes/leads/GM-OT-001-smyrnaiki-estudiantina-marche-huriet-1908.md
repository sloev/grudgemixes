## Lyrics and translation

The Kounadis record page publishes Turkish lyrics and an English translation. The text includes the lines:

- “Mazlumların feryadı” — “The cry of the oppressed”
- “Mahvetti istibdadı” — “Destroyed despotism”
- “Hafiye belâsının / Dillerde kaldı yâdı” — roughly, “The scourge of the informers / remains only in memory”
- “Şimdi hürriyet bulduk” — “Now we have found freedom”
- “Esaretten kurtulduk” — “We have escaped from bondage”

The refrain celebrates Niyazi and Enver and patriotic soldiers. The archive's translation is the starting point; commission Turkish-language review before publication, especially for the political vocabulary and the historical meaning of “Hurriyet.”

## Historical and thematic assessment

This is a clear anti-despotism / liberation text associated with the 1908 Young Turk Revolution and the restoration of constitutional government. Its explicit references to the oppressed, informers, despotism, freedom and bondage make the grievance/protest content textual rather than inferred from the title.

The title's reference to Niyazi and Enver connects the march to the revolutionary officers associated with the constitutional movement. Avoid flattening the political context into a simple modern democratic narrative: the Young Turk movement was heterogeneous, and the later history of the Committee of Union and Progress was complex. The song's immediate 1908 celebratory viewpoint is not a neutral verdict on the movement's subsequent rule.

The performing ensemble is labelled Smyrnaiki Estudiantina. Preserve that archive credit and the Turkish-language lyric even though the record was made in Constantinople; do not assume the ensemble's name establishes its ethnicity or the language community of every musician.
