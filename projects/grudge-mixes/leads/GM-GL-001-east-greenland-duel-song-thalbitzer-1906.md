# GM-GL-001 — K'iwingatāk', duel song (*pisit* / drum-song duel), Tasiilaq (Ammassalik), East Greenland, 1906

**Project:** Grudge Mixes (pre-1926 field recording) and the *Hostile* strand · **Research grade:** B (recording identified on a published reissue; original cylinder number not yet traced) · **Downloaded:** No · **Proposed mix:** PROPOSAL Vol. 12 *Fighting Words*, opening track; or Vol. 4 · Added 2026-11-20 by Claude.

## Recording

- **Singer:** K'iwingatāk' (Thalbitzer's spelling), a woman, Tasiilaq (then Ammassalik), East Greenland.
- **Recorded by:** William Thalbitzer, Danish linguist, on wax cylinder in 1906, during his 1905–06 stay at the newly founded Ammassalik trading post.
- **Genre:** duel song (*inngerutit* drum-song tradition; East Greenlandic *pisit*). Two people with a quarrel met before the community and sang mocking songs at each other with the drum; the audience's laughter decided who won. A grievance settled in song instead of by violence.
- **Published:** *Inuit: 55 Historical Recordings – Traditional Music from Greenland 1905–1987*, compiled by Michael Hauser (ULO, Nuuk / Sub Rosa, 2000; 2LP reissue). MusicBrainz lists it as disc 1 track 4, "[inngerutit: duel-song]", 1:06. A second duel song (track 2, 3:07) and another (track 13) are also listed; check their singers.
- **Original cylinder:** probably held by the Danish Folklore Archives / Royal Library, Copenhagen, which holds the Thalbitzer cylinders. Item number to trace.

## Why it fits

This is the grudge as an institution in its purest form: a community gives the aggrieved a stage, a drum and an audience, and the song is the verdict. It also gives the series:

- the Arctic;
- an Indigenous woman's voice from before 1926;
- a Danish-archive connection close to the owner.

## Ethics and consent

- These are East Greenlandic (Tunumiit) cultural recordings made in the first years of colonial contact.
- Use needs agreement from Greenlandic heritage bodies (Nunatta Katersugaasivia Allagaateqarfialu / Greenland National Museum and Archives), and ideally descendants in Tasiilaq. ULO, the Greenlandic label that issued the reissue, is the natural partner.
- Do not frame it as "primitive" or exotic. It is a legal and social institution.
