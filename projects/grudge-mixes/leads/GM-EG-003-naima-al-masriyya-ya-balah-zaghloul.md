# GM-EG-003 — Naʿīma al-Miṣriyya, "Yā Balaḥ Zaghlūl" (Mechian, c.1919–1922; date disputed)

**Project:** Grudge Mixes (and Insane Music: a song written to get round a ban) · **Research grade:** B (song, singer, writers, context and label well attested; exact disc, date and matrix unresolved) · **Downloaded:** No · **Proposed mix:** PROPOSAL, Vol. 4 *The Collective Account* (would be its first Arabic-language track and second woman's voice)

Added 2026-11-20 by Claude. Nobody has listened to an attributed transfer yet.

## Recording

- **Arabic title:** يا بلح زغلول. **Transliteration:** "Yā balaḥ Zaghlūl". **Gloss:** "O Zaghloul dates!" (*Zaghlūl* is also the name of a variety of date.)
- **Singer:** Naʿīma al-Miṣriyya (نعيمة المصرية), born Zaynab Muḥammad Idrīs, Cairo 1894–1976. She was nicknamed "queen of the records" and made about 150 discs (ar.wikipedia; Ṣabāḥ al-Khayr).
- **Music:** Sayyid Darwīsh (d. 1923). **Lyrics:** Badīʿ Khayrī (d. 1966) (arz.wikipedia; al-Wafd 2019; Copts United).
- **Label:** Mechian (مشيان; Khawaga Stroak Mechian, Cairo). Frédéric Lagrange, as reported by Bāb Miṣr in Nov 2026, lists it in Naʿīma's first Mechian set, about eight discs of *ṭaqāṭīq*.
- **Date: disputed.**
  - Lagrange places that Mechian set in **1915**.
  - Hiba Fareed (Naʿīma archive project) ties the recording to the **1919** revolution, after Zaghloul's exile and the British censorship order.
  - A 1915 date cannot fit a coded tribute to a man exiled in March 1919. Either the set is misdated, or the song was recorded at Naʿīma's second Mechian session in **1922**, which Lagrange describes as theatrical songs by Darwīsh with lyrics by Khayrī. Resolve this from the disc label and the EAP1682 catalogue before using any date.
- **Catalogue / matrix:** unknown. The Bāb Miṣr article gives no numbers.

## Story

When the British exiled Saad Zaghloul in March 1919, the military authorities, by Egyptian accounts, made it an offence to name him: six months' hard labour and twenty lashes (al-Wafd, 2019); Copts United adds a ban on his name in songs and on Darwīsh singing. Khayrī and Darwīsh answered with a street song apparently about dates. The refrain cries "Zaghloul dates, O sweet dates", calls them "the crop of my country" and asks when the "spirit of the land" will come back. Darwīsh gave it to Naʿīma, whose voice was strong enough to carry it; she is said to have sung it from her fourth-floor balcony during the revolution (Ṣabāḥ al-Khayr). Everyone who sang along named the banned man in public without saying his name.

## Why it fits

- **Grudge Mixes:** a grievance against an occupying power (the exile of the national leader) answered by a collective song, before 1926. This is the book's "collective account" in its wittiest form: the injury is public, and so is the evasion.
- **Insane Music / *Music in Spite*:** music made against an explicit prohibition by a woman performer in a scene where women singers were judged harshly.
- It fills the series' biggest gap: an Arabic-language track on a main volume, by a woman.

## Rights and ethics

- Darwīsh died in 1923, so the music is public domain.
- Khayrī died in 1966. Egypt protects for life plus 50 years (expired 2016); the EU protects for life plus 70 years (until end of 2036). ⚠ Lyrics need clearance for EU release.
- The phonogram (before 1926) has expired in the US and EU. Restoration rights in an AMAR or AUC transfer must be negotiated with them.
- No consent issues; the singer was a commercial star.
