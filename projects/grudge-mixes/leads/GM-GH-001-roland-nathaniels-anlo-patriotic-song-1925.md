# GM-GH-001 — Roland C. Nathaniels, “Anlo Patriotic Song” / “Ewe Battle Hymn” (Zonophone 3972, 1925)

**Status: C-level research lead — exact commercial recording identified; thematic fit and exact performed text unverified. NOT selected, NOT cleared.** This is a Ghanaian Anlo-Ewe recording made in London, not a field recording. Keep the two sides together in the evidence record.

## Exact recording identity

| Field | Current evidence |
|---|---|
| Performer | Roland C. Nathaniels, Anlo-Ewe composer, pianist and organist from Keta (present-day Ghana); based in Europe/UK during the 1920s |
| Label / catalogue | Zonophone 3972, general overseas 3000 series / West African releases |
| Side 1 | “Anlo Patriotic Song,” Ewe; song with piano and whistling chorus |
| Side 1 matrix / take | Zonophone side X-4-42856; Gramophone Company matrix Yy7340-1 |
| Side 2 | “Ewe Battle Hymn,” Ewe; described by the discography as “Native melody” |
| Side 2 matrix / take | Zonophone side X-4-42857; Gramophone Company matrix Yy7339-2 |
| Recording date | 20 November 1925, according to the Afrodisc specialist Zonophone listing |
| Recording location | London is consistent with the Gramophone Company's 1925 West African sessions; verify exact studio from matrix/session register |
| Issue date | Not independently established yet; catalogue number and series establish a commercial issue lead, not a fully verified publication date |
| Medium | 10-inch / 25 cm, 78 rpm shellac, single-play disc |

The Afrodisc listing gives the exact catalogue, sides, languages, matrices/takes and session date. John Cowley's study independently describes the Gramophone Company's 1925 West African recording programme and the role of Roland Nathaniels. Ernest Owusu-Poku's 2022 history of recording in Ghana identifies Nathaniels as an Anlo-Ewe musician from Keta and specifically notes “Anlo Patriotic Song” as an Ewe work later published as sheet music by Eclipse Music Publishing on 1 January 1926.

## Thematic reading — do not overclaim

The titles “Anlo Patriotic Song” and “Ewe Battle Hymn” invite investigation of collective identity, resistance and historical grievance, but neither title proves an anti-colonial message. “Battle hymn” may be a conventional religious or ceremonial description; the discography calls it a “Native melody,” not a political protest song. “Patriotic” may refer to Anlo community identity without a specific grievance or adversary.

**Current thematic grade: C / unproven.** Obtain the Ewe text, identify the referent of “patriotic,” and compare the words actually sung on both sides with the 1926 sheet-music witness before upgrading. Preserve Ewe orthography and consult an Ewe/Anlo language specialist; do not infer meaning from English catalogue titles.

## Chronology and exclusions

- Cowley dates the 1925 West African session programme and identifies Nathaniels' Ewe work; the Afrodisc listing supplies the exact 20 November date for matrix Yy7340-1 / Yy7339-2.
- “Speech on the Tribes of Anlo” and “Speech on Woman's Rights,” Zonophone 3983, are a separate Nathaniels recording. Afrodisc gives the “Woman's Rights” matrix Yy10319-1 and recording date 18 March 1927; this is outside the pre-1926 core. Do not backdate it because some secondary bibliography lists Zonophone 3983 under 1925.
- Nathaniels' 1927 Ewe hymns on Zonophone 3978–3982 are also later sessions (18 March 1927) and must not be conflated with the 1925 3972 disc.
