## Text and thematic evidence

The Hebrew University / Jewish Music Research Centre commentary identifies this as a truncated version of the Sephardic romance “El sentenciado del bajá” (CMP H23), known in only a few printed and recorded sources in longer versions. It explains that the protagonist is sentenced to death after refusing the advances of an anonymous “Turk”; in some versions the protagonist is female, in others male. The fuller romance develops into a dialogue with the protagonist's mother, who is asked to save the condemned child but cannot help. The commentary explicitly notes that this dialogue is missing from Mlle Mariette's recording.

The recorded text, as transcribed in the scholarly booklet, begins:

- “Yo se venido de Brusa, / me fue para’l hukiumat” — “I came from Bursa; I went to the government/court.”
- “El turco pašá me vido / con él me quijo llivar” — “The Turkish pasha saw me; he wanted to take me with him.”

The following verses describe an order/imperial decree (fermán), enemies following the protagonist, and the condemned person being taken before the court. The exact semantics of Ottoman legal terms such as *hukiumat*, *fermán* and *estindak* require specialist Ladino/Ottoman Turkish review; retain the original forms rather than forcing an overconfident English translation.

This is a strong narrative of sexual coercion backed by official power, followed by state punishment/death sentence. It is highly relevant to accusation, abuse of authority and gendered grievance. It is a ballad/romance narrative, however, not proof of a specific historical court case or a documentary account of an identifiable pasha. The version on this record is truncated, and the gender of the protagonist is not fixed across the tradition.
