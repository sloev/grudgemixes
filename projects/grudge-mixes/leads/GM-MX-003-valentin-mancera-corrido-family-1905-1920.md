# GM-MX-003 — Valentín Mancera corrido family (Victor 97040 / Victor 72590-A, 1905–c.1920)

**Programme status:** IN PROGRESS  
**Grudge evidence level:** C — LEAD (qualified discovery lead; not a promoted track or candidate)  
**Deduplication unit:** one underlying corrido family, not one count per recording version or print witness  
**Region/language:** Guanajuato / Mexico; Spanish

## Why it qualifies for investigation

The printed corrido family tells of Valentín Mancera, a Guanajuato bandit whose lover and her sister betray him for money, leading to capture and execution. The 2016 analysis treats the song as an accusatory, didactic narrative about betrayal and punishment. That is within the project's grievance/punishment remit. The link from that narrative to the exact wording of each early recording remains incomplete, so this stays at C level.

## Recorded witnesses — keep separate

1. **Victor 97040 — Abrego y Picazo, “Valentín Mancera”**. John R. Bolig's 2025 Mexican Victor discography places this in the seven-inch Victor catalogue: matrix **12-Y**, recorded in **Mexico City, July 1905**, catalogue/issue number **97040**. The entry is not marked “NR” in the discography, but no surviving copy, label image or transfer was located in this pass. Treat the issue as a catalogued recording, not as an inspected or heard object.
2. **Victor 72590-A — Trio González, “Valentín Mancera”**. Ramírez-Barradas's 2016 article cites this as a Victor recording, circa **1920** (footnote 18). No matrix number, label scan or direct item record was found. The date is approximate and secondary.
3. **Comparison only: Columbia C2193, matrix 13787 — Abrego y Picazo**. UCLA's Strachwitz Frontera Collection has a distinct 78 record entry and audio excerpt for “Valentín Mancera.” It is **not** the Victor 97040 master: the label and matrix differ, and the Frontera record has no recording date in its item metadata. Do not use this later/undated Columbia item as proof that the 1905 Victor copy survives.
4. COLMEX also catalogs two separate printed witnesses: *Versos de Valentín Mancera: traídos del estado de Guanajuato; Adiós, adiós: canción moderna* (1914; Hoja 0202-0203; handle 10041713) and an undated *Corrido de Valentín Mancera* (Hoja 0046; handle 10041582). These are print objects, not recordings, and should not be conflated with one another or with either Victor master.

## Date, fixation and text

- The COLMEX 1914 date belongs to that specific broadside, not to either Victor recording.
- The 2016 article analyzes Vanegas Arroyo and Guerrero print versions and cites the circa-1920 Trio González Victor side. It does not provide a transcription tied to Victor 97040 or 72590-A.
- The 1905 Victor and circa-1920 Victor are separate recording versions of the same song family. They are **one** new lead for the programme, not two.
- The Frontera Columbia C2193 is a further separate recorded version with an undated fixation; it is a comparison witness only and is not counted as another lead.

## Provenance, access and rights

- The strongest early-record evidence is a 2025 specialist transcription of Victor's Mexican catalogue/matrix data and the 2016 article's citation of Victor 72590-A. No original Victor label scan, surviving Victor disc, matrix card or authorized transfer was located.
- The Frontera item page is an item-level catalogue record with an audio excerpt, but the excerpt was not played.
- The COLMEX item pages were not directly opened in this pass; their catalogue records and handles were visible in the repository search results. The print objects themselves were not inspected.
- No recording was listened to, downloaded or copied; no archive was contacted; no rights clearance was sought. A catalogue entry is not permission to reproduce a transfer.

## Remaining uncertainty and next action

**Open:** exact text performed on Victor 97040 and Victor 72590-A; whether either Victor issue survives; exact date/location/matrix for 72590-A; whether the 1914 broadside is textually related to the recorded versions; rights in any modern transfer.

**Next:** locate Victor label scans or matrix-book images for 97040 and 72590-A; seek a lawful transfer or archive item record for each; collate the 1914 and undated COLMEX texts against any authenticated recording transcript. Keep the lead at C until the grievance/punishment text is tied to an exact eligible master.
