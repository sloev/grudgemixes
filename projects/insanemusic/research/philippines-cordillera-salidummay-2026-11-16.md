# Philippine Cordillera — Salidummay / Dap-ayan ti Kultura iti Kordilyera cassette research

**Research date:** 2026-11-16  
**Proposed catalogue ID:** IM-042  
**Status:** PARTIALLY VERIFIED — recording/release documented; exact first-cassette date and edition linkage unresolved  
**Visibility:** PROMISING / medium visibility  
**Pathway:** F — marginal/under-documented community cultural scene; also community-organised cassette production  
**Place:** Baguio City and the northern Luzon highlands, Cordillera Region, Philippines  
**Language:** multilingual Cordillera/local-language repertoire; exact language-by-track mapping for the 1987/1989 cassette has not been verified

## Candidate

**Salidummay**, produced by **Dap-ayan ti Kultura iti Kordilyera (DKK)**, a cultural alliance/sector associated with the Cordillera Peoples Alliance (CPA). A cassette album is documented in both an academic ethnomusicological dissertation and the 2023 *Lakbayan: Voices of Resistance from the Philippines* exhibition catalogue. A current streaming listing also presents a ten-track album called *Salidummay*, with a platform release date of 24 April 1987 and ℗ 2023 DKK Cultural Alliance.

This is a community-based cultural and activist music project, not an institutional recording and not evidence of psychiatric history. Its relevance is the specific social and musical setting: locally organised cultural workers, regional circulation on cassette, Indigenous-rights and land-defence movements, and music that combines local musical materials with contemporary political expression. Do not label the collective or every performer “outsider”; describe the concrete community/scene context.

## What the sources establish

### 1. Academic ethnomusicology — first cassette, 1989

Michiyo Yoneno-Reyes' dissertation states that DKK released its first cassette album, *Salidumay*, in **1989**, containing **ten songs**. It says the lyrics promoted an autonomous Cordillera Region and a “unity in diversity” regionalism, in the context of movements opposing mega-dam projects and commercial logging in the northern Luzon highlands. The dissertation reports five DKK cassette albums in the 1990s (1989, 1992, 1995, 1999 and one undated) and five CD albums in the 2000s. It says the cassettes circulated mainly in Baguio City and the northern Luzon highlands and were among the few locally produced and commodified cassette albums in that market.

The dissertation's audiovisual bibliography separately identifies:
- *Salidumay* [1989], one cassette, produced by DKK / DKK Salidummay group;
- *Dong Dong Ay. Salidummay* [1992], one cassette, produced by DKK / DKK Salidummay group;
- *Salidumay. Elalay* [1995], one cassette, produced by DKK / DKK Salidummay group.

It notes that individual songs had titles, fixed lyrics, corresponding tunes and vocal/instrumental arrangements; some songs were attributed to particular writers. This is important evidence against treating the album as an anonymous or undifferentiated “folk” recording.

### 2. Exhibition/archive catalogue — first cassette, 1987

The Savvy Contemporary / MaerzMusik 2023 exhibition handout *Lakbayan: Voices of Resistance from the Philippines* lists the DKK cassette **Salidummay (1987)**, followed by *Dong-Dong-Ay Salidummay* (1992), *Diway* (2000), *Ay-Ay Salidummay* (2003), and *Salidumma-ay* (2004). It describes its displayed cassette/CD materials as a collection built by Dang A Dang Radio through purchases from artists and people's organisations, private collectors and thrift shops. It explicitly describes the Salidummay-DKK albums as blending 1970s folk and Cordillera Indigenous traditions.

This is a strong independent exhibition-catalogue record of the cassette, but the handout does not reproduce a cassette label, catalogue number, matrix or tracklist for the 1987 item.

### 3. Current digital listing

Spotify currently lists an album titled *Salidummay* by Salidummay with ten tracks, 32:46 total duration, and a platform date of **24 April 1987**. The metadata displays © 2023 / ℗ 2023 DKK Cultural Alliance. Track titles as presented include “Donglala,” “Ili Mi’d Kaigorotan,” “Autonomy Alelay,” “Ditay Koma Malipatan,” “Bumangon Tay Amin,” “Bumangon Kordilyera,” “Bebsat san Kaigorotan,” “Istorya nan Kordilyera,” “Mentedted nan Luluak,” and “Ahay.”

This is an authorised-looking current platform listing associated with DKK's artist profile, but platform metadata alone does not prove that this digital object is a bit-identical transfer of the 1987 or 1989 cassette. No audio was played in this research pass.

### 4. Movement and community context

The CPA's official “About” page describes it as an independent federation of progressive people's organisations, most grassroots-based among Indigenous communities in the Cordillera Region. It says CPA was founded in June 1984 by delegates from 27 organisations, many of whose leaders had participated in opposition to the Chico dams project and commercial logging by Cellophil Resources Corporation. Its current description identifies cultural workers as one of its sectoral federations and DKK as the regional alliance of cultural organisations.

Yoneno-Reyes describes DKK as a cultural sector of CPA. The 2023 *Lakbayan* handout frames protest music as part of a broad, intergenerational archive of social movements, with cassettes and CDs circulating among artists, people's organisations, collectors and thrift shops.

## Date and identity cautions

**Do not collapse 1987 and 1989.** The 2023 exhibition catalogue and Spotify metadata give 1987; the ethnomusicological dissertation gives 1989 for the first cassette. Possible explanations include a distinct earlier edition, a later first physical cassette, a date transcription error, or platform backdating; none is established. The original cassette's label, J-card, catalogue number, matrix, and track sequence are needed.

The name is variously written *Salidumay* and *Salidummay*. The dissertation distinguishes the DKK album title spelling from the broader term *salidummay*, which is used variably for a family of songs/refrains and a modern popular genre. A 1999 ICTM conference abstract cautions that treating salidummay as a uniform regional legacy can be an “outsider's view” for some rural Igorot communities, whose own identities may be expressed through other local chant traditions. Do not describe it as an ancient, uniform pan-Cordillera genre or assume all Cordillera communities share one musical identity.

A 2021 *Cordillera Review* article on Kalinga ethnopop discusses the wider hybrid musical context—local-language lyrics, Indigenous and foreign instruments/melodic patterns, and contemporary community concerns—but it is not item-level evidence about this specific DKK cassette.

## Rights, access and ethics

- The Spotify listing is a listening route, not a download, master-use or commercial-reuse licence.
- The exhibition catalogue documents a curated archive but does not grant reuse rights to the original recordings or artwork.
- No cassette was physically inspected; no audio was downloaded or auditioned; no archive, collective or rights holder was contacted; no permissions were requested or granted.
- The material is politically situated. Represent the artists and cultural workers as active makers, not as generic symbols of “Indigenous protest.” Avoid reproducing lyrics or sacred/traditional material without contextual and rights review.

## Next verification steps

1. Find and inspect the original *Salidummay* cassette/J-card/label and establish whether the 1987 and 1989 dates refer to different editions or a source error.
2. Compare the ten-track Spotify listing with the dissertation's 1989 ten-song description; obtain an authorised transfer or a tracklist from DKK.
3. Verify the distinct 1992 *Dong Dong Ay. Salidummay* cassette and the 1995 *Elalay* cassette from physical/catalogue evidence before assigning separate IDs.
4. Seek an item-level source from DKK / CPA / Dang A Dang Radio for producer, performers, songwriters, language per track, recording location and circulation.
5. Keep the wider Cordillera cassette scene in view, including individual local-language artists and community groups, rather than treating DKK as representative of the whole region.
