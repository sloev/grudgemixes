# Research method

**Project:** Soviet Film Music  
**Status:** Initial protocol, 2026-11-16

## 1. Purpose and scope

Investigate music created for films produced in the Soviet Union, with special attention to science fiction and animation. The archive should ultimately help listeners and researchers understand not just who composed a film's music, but what was written, performed, recorded, heard, preserved, released and reinterpreted.

The project is not limited to feature films or Russian-language productions. Include short animation, television films, documentaries, silent cinema with later scores, films from all Soviet republics and relevant co-productions. Record the production context precisely rather than assuming a single homogeneous “Soviet sound”.

Priority starting points:
- `Кин-дза-дза!` / `Kin-dza-dza!` (1986), music credited to Giya Kancheli.
- `Тайна третьей планеты` / `The Mystery of the Third Planet` (1981), music credited to Aleksandr Zatsepin.

## 2. What counts as a research object

Keep these object types distinct:
1. **Film** — a specific production and cut/version.
2. **Score** — notated or otherwise documented compositional material.
3. **Cue** — an individual piece or passage used in the film.
4. **Soundtrack recording** — the actual recorded music as heard in a specific film version.
5. **Song** — vocal work, with lyricist/composer/performer details where known.
6. **Sound design** — effects, electronic sounds, ambience and other designed audio; distinguish from music when possible, but document hybrid cases.
7. **Release** — LP, EP, 45, cassette, CD, digital album, promotional disc or other issued recording.
8. **Archive object** — manuscript, tape, disc, cue sheet, session document, soundtrack master or preservation transfer.
9. **Later version** — restoration, re-recording, dub, edit, compilation or fan-made reconstruction.

A credit reading “music by” is evidence of a credited composer, not proof of a surviving standalone soundtrack album or a complete score. Film audio is not automatically equivalent to an album master.

## 3. Evidence labels

Apply labels to individual claims or fields, not just whole files.

- **VERIFIED** — directly supported by a primary source or multiple strong independent sources.
- **WELL-SUPPORTED** — reliable secondary evidence, with no known contradiction.
- **PROVISIONAL** — credible lead but incomplete or indirect evidence.
- **DISPUTED** — sources conflict or identification remains unresolved.
- **UNKNOWN** — not established; do not fill by inference.

For every disputed claim, preserve both versions, cite each source and explain what would resolve it. A lack of evidence is not evidence that a score, recording or release never existed.

## 4. Source strategy

Search and record sources in Russian and relevant republic languages as well as English. Useful original-language search terms include:
- `музыка к фильму`, `композитор фильма`, `киномузыка`
- `саундтрек`, `музыкальное оформление`, `звукооператор`
- `партитура`, `клавир`, `партии`, `фонограмма`, `магнитная фонограмма`
- `мультфильм`, `научная фантастика`, `фантастический фильм`
- Studio and archive terms: `Мосфильм`, `Союзмультфильм`, `Госфильмофонд`, `Российская государственная библиотека`, `РГАЛИ`.

Preferred sources: original film credits and production documents; studio/archive catalogues; national libraries and cinematheques; composer/estate archives; scholarly publications; contemporaneous interviews and press; reputable discographies and specialist databases. General web databases, fan pages and upload descriptions are leads, not final proof.

Record source title, creator/institution, publication date, URL, access date, relevant page/track/item and exactly what it supports. Prefer stable catalogue records, DOI links and archived citations where possible.

## 5. Musical analysis

Do not infer instrumentation or compositional technique from a generic description or memory. When listening:
- identify the exact film version or audio release;
- note timestamp and scene/cue boundaries;
- describe audible features before interpreting them;
- distinguish music from effects, dialogue, room tone and designed sound;
- identify instruments only when reasonably audible or confirmed by documentation;
- mark uncertain timbres as “possibly” and explain the basis;
- avoid claiming a cue title unless a score, cue sheet or release supplies it.

For electronic music, record the instrument/model only with evidence. Distinguish a named commercial instrument from a homemade device, processing chain or sound effect. Where the history of an instrument or technique is relevant, cite a technical or participant source.

## 6. Film and version control

For each film, record original title, transliteration, translated titles, year, studio, director, running time and source/version. Note differences among theatrical release, television edit, export version, dub, restoration and home-video release. Music can be replaced, shortened, remixed or omitted in later editions; never assume the currently accessible upload reproduces the original soundtrack.

## 7. Rights and access

Research status and rights status are separate. For each recording or document, record:
- copyright/ownership status of the composition and lyrics;
- sound-recording/master rights;
- archive or collection access terms;
- digitisation/transfer rights;
- permissions for reproduction, quotation, streaming, sampling or commercial use;
- territory and intended use, where known.

Use explicit states: UNKNOWN, RESEARCH NEEDED, PERMISSION REQUIRED, PERMISSION OBTAINED, PUBLIC-DOMAIN EVIDENCE FOUND, RESTRICTED ACCESS, LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED. A public stream is not permission to download or republish. Do not upload audio, scans, sheet music or artwork without clearance.

## 8. Research file template

Each case study should include:
- Title variants and identification
- Production facts and version notes
- Music credits and personnel
- Score / cue / song evidence
- Listening notes (version, timestamp, method)
- Soundtrack and release history
- Archival holdings and access
- Musical analysis
- Rights and permissions
- Sources
- Research status
- Open questions
- Next tasks

## 9. Collaboration and change control

Follow [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md). Every contributor reads the shared log, claims a task, writes focused research and appends a dated hand-off. The repository owner sets priorities. Do not silently change the scope or methodology. Keep the website out of scope until explicitly requested.

## 10. Initial strategic direction

Start with the two priority films, but use them to open broader comparative paths:
- Kancheli and Daneliya's collaborations and the sound world of late-Soviet cinematic satire.
- Zatsepin's electronic experiments, studio practice and work for Soyuzmultfilm.
- Soviet sci-fi scores from silent-era `Aelita` and `Kosmicheskiy reys` through the 1950s–1980s.
- Animation composers and studios, including music written for shorts as well as features.
- The relationship between composed score, songs, electronic effects and sound design.

## 2026-11-17 — SF-TJ-001 Tajik-language source reconciliation

Local-language sources now distinguish the Tajik film title «Ман бо духтаре вохӯрдам» from the song title «Дидам ҷамоле» and Russian song title «Я встретил девушку».
