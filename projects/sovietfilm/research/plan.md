# Initial research plan

**Working date:** 2026-11-16  
**Phase:** Research and Markdown only  
**Owner priorities:** `Кин-дза-дза!` and `Тайна третьей планеты`

## Research objective

Build a richly sourced account of Soviet film music: who made it, how it was composed and recorded, what it sounds like, how it functions in the film, what recordings survive, how soundtrack releases differ, and where the material can be heard or studied lawfully. Treat the eventual website as a later publishing layer over a well-documented research base.

## Phase 1 — Two anchor films

### A. `Кин-дза-дза!` (1986)
- Confirm production and music credits from film credits and studio/archival sources.
- Research Giya Kancheli's score: surviving manuscripts, orchestral parts, recordings, cue sheets, conductor/performers and recording studio.
- Establish whether there is an official soundtrack release, and document every known edition separately.
- Watch/inspect a reliable original-language version and make timestamped cue notes.
- Study the relationship between score, songs, silence, dialogue, sound effects and the film's absurdist/dystopian world.
- Trace Kancheli's collaborations with Georgiy Daneliya and related film work.
- Compare original release with restorations, dubs and home-video versions.

### B. `Тайна третьей планеты` (1981)
- Confirm production, music and sound credits through Soyuzmultfilm and specialist animation sources.
- Research Aleksandr Zatsepin's score and reported electronic experiments.
- Identify instruments and devices only through reliable documentation or careful listening; test claims about homemade electronic instruments and Minimoog-related experiments.
- Map themes, songs, cues and their relation to characters, creatures, spacecraft and environments.
- Locate official soundtrack releases, film audio, archival materials and alternate versions.
- Investigate the Wende Museum's note that a later US dub/re-edit removed the original soundtrack; establish which version and release it describes.
- Trace Zatsepin's wider film/animation work and studio relationships.

**Deliverable:** two evidence-rich case studies, with a separate source list and unresolved-question list for each.

## Phase 2 — Comparative Soviet science-fiction film music

Build a chronological film list, then research scores and soundtracks:
- Silent-era and early sound: `Аэлита` / `Aelita` (1924), `Космический рейс` / `Kosmicheskiy reys` (1935).
- 1950s–1960s: science-fiction cinema, orchestral language, theremin/electronic timbres and changing studio technologies.
- 1970s–1980s: electronic experimentation, synthesizers, hybrid orchestral/electronic writing, television and animation.
- Include all Soviet republics and co-productions; do not equate Soviet film music solely with Moscow studios.

For each film, distinguish original score from later accompaniment, replacement music and restoration soundtrack.

## Phase 3 — Soviet animation music atlas

- Map major animation studios and their music/sound departments, beginning with Soyuzmultfilm but expanding regionally.
- Identify composers with substantial animation filmographies and notable one-off scores.
- Include short films, serials, feature animation and television specials.
- Track recurring performers, orchestras, conductors, arrangers, lyricists and sound directors.
- Investigate songs and themes that circulated independently of their films.
- Compare musical approaches across puppet, drawn, stop-motion and experimental animation.

Potential composer paths include Aleksandr Zatsepin, Gennady Gladkov, Vladimir Shainsky, Mieczysław Weinberg, Sofia Gubaidulina, Eduard Artemyev, Mikael Tariverdiev, Andrey Petrov and others. This is a discovery list, not a verified inclusion list.

## Phase 4 — Composer, studio and technology dossiers

Create connected research files for:
- composers and their filmographies;
- studios and production structures;
- recording studios, orchestras, conductors and engineers;
- instruments, synthesizers, electronic devices and studio techniques;
- sound effects and the boundary between score and sound design;
- soundtrack labels, pressing plants, broadcasters and archival repositories.

Investigate how production constraints, state studio systems, regional musical traditions and composer career paths shaped the resulting sound.

## Phase 5 — Archives and release history

Search:
- film studio archives and official film pages;
- national film archives and cinematheques;
- national libraries and music manuscript catalogues;
- composer estates and personal archives;
- radio/TV archives;
- record catalogues and physical release databases;
- academic writing, interviews and oral histories.

For each recording, document carrier/format, label/catalogue number, matrix if available, issue date, country, track list, credits, transfer source and access conditions. A streaming copy is not a preservation master. A film soundtrack is not automatically a commercially released album.

## Phase 6 — Synthesis and future website readiness

Only after the research corpus is stable:
- reconcile names, transliterations and film identifiers;
- resolve duplicate and contradictory claims;
- decide which entities deserve structured records;
- prepare concise editorial summaries with links to deeper notes;
- propose a website information architecture to the owner.

No website code or framework is in scope yet.

## Priority questions to resolve first

1. What exact music and recording materials survive for `Кин-дза-дза!`?
2. What did Kancheli write specifically for the film, and what other musical material is used?
3. What electronic instruments/devices did Zatsepin use for `Тайна третьей планеты`, and what is the documentary basis?
4. How do the original film soundtracks compare with soundtrack albums, dubs, restorations and online versions?
5. Which Soviet sci-fi and animated film scores have the most interesting or distinctive musical and technical histories?

## Working principles

- Breadth in discovery, depth in verification.
- Keep original-language research central.
- Record sources and uncertainty at claim level.
- Never mistake an interesting lead for an established fact.
- Build a useful map before deciding what the website should look like.

### Uzbek SSR: *Мафтунингман* / *Maftuningman* (1958), cue “Do‘ppi tikdim”

Add this as one cue-family research target, not as a soundtrack album. The 2014 printed score labels “Do‘ppi tikdim” as from the film and credits Turob To‘la / “Monas Leviev”; the Uzbek Composers’ Union biography credits Klara Jalilova’s performance and places the song among Mutal Burhonov’s film work while also describing collaboration with Leviev and Ikrom Akbarov. Preserve this composer-credit tension until original production documentation is found.

The film is identified as a 1958 Uzbekfilm musical comedy by Shaxs.uz and in the TelecomTV Milliy catalogue. A RizaNova video title says the song is from the film, but the video page did not load; do not infer that it is the original film mix. The film-print cue, later video excerpt and any standalone soundtrack release must remain separate objects. See the SF-UZ-001 dossier and SF-AUD-008 queue row.
