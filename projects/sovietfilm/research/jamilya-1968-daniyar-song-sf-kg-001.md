# SF-KG-001 — “Песня Данияра” / *Джамиля* (*Jamilya*, 1968/1969)

**Status:** IN PROGRESS — provisional cue-family lead.  
**Count:** One cue family within one film; not a separate soundtrack release.

## Film identity and version boundary

Mosfilm’s official film record identifies *Джамиля* as a 1968 feature, 83 minutes, mono, directed by Ирина Поплавская, photographed by Кадыржан Кыдыралиев and composed by Николай Сидельников. Its synopsis explicitly refers to the wounded Daniyar’s songs.

A regional Kyrgyz-film catalogue places the same film under 1969 and describes it as a Lenfilm production with a German co-producer and Kyrgyzfilm participation. It gives the same director, cinematographer and composer. This is a date/production-credit discrepancy for one film, not evidence of two separate films. Do not resolve 1968 versus 1969 without a primary print, release record or production file.

KM.RU’s film-level entry credits Николай Сидельников as composer and Б. Сарногоев for song lyrics. It does not map either credit to the specific Daniyar cue.

## Cue-level evidence

An OK.ru page uploaded on 11 December 2023 presents a 6:12 excerpt under the title “Джамиля (1968) — Песня Данияра”. Its description calls it a fragment of the feature film and repeats the Mosfilm 1968 credit line. This is a later user-uploaded excerpt and a working cue label, not a primary cue title or an authenticated film-print source.

The title is consistent with Mosfilm’s synopsis mentioning Daniyar’s songs, but the exact cue wording, timecode, language, singer, lyricist mapping and source print remain unverified. Do not infer that an actor listed in the cast is the singer. The excerpt must not be conflated with a soundtrack album, later dub, re-recording or reconstruction.

## 2026-11-20 — Kyrgyz-language and local film-critic source check

The official Mosfilm record continues to identify **Джамиля** as a 1968, 83-minute, mono feature with composer **Николай Сидельников**; its synopsis explicitly says that wounded Daniyar's songs teach the narrator to notice beauty. This confirms the film-level context, not the identity or performer of a particular cue.

The Qarash Lab tag points to a contemporary Kyrgyz creative film laboratory, not by itself to an archive or rights holder. Treat the two uploads as separate digital objects until compared; do not count them as two cues or assume either is an original-print transfer.
