# SF-TJ-001 — «Я встретил девушку» / *I Met a Girl* (1957): Tajikfilm musical and bilingual soundtrack versions

**Status:** PROVISIONAL film/cue-family lead; contemporary film publicity and specialist scholarship identify the score, songs and cross-language re-recording process, but item-level audio objects and exact credits need primary-source reconciliation  
**Region:** Tajik SSR / Stalinabad (now Dushanbe), with cross-republic production and distribution  
**Film:** «Ман бо духтаре вохӯрдам» (Tajik title; exact title-card orthography still to confirm) / «Я встретил девушку» (Russian title) / *I Met a Girl*.\n**Signature song:** «Дидам ҷамоле» (Tajik) / «Я встретил девушку» (Russian). Do not use the song title as the film's Tajik title.  
**Director:** Rafail Perelstein  
**Composer:** Andrei Babaev (Андрей Бабаев)  
**Lyricist:** Mirzo Tursunzade (Мирзо Турсунзода); Russian-language adaptation credited to Gabriel El-Registan in the specialist essay  
**Singer attribution:** **DISPUTED; likely version-specific.** Tillo Neqqadamov (2019, pp. 154–155) says Rashid Behbudov first sang “Дидам ҷамоле” on stage and in a feature film; Sharofat Arabova (2020) specifically assigns Behbudov the Russian-language re-sung version. An archived 2011 song-history page and later secondary discussions instead attribute the film's off-screen vocal to Rauf Atakishiyev. A verified Gosteleradiofond 1958 Central Television studio performance confirms a separate Behbudov performance, not the film vocal. No film print or vocal-credit sheet has been inspected; do not collapse the two singers or versions.

## Contemporary and specialist evidence

A contemporary 1957 article in the Soviet youth magazine *Смена*, titled «Я встретил девушку», describes the film as the first Tajik musical film and states that its songs and dance music were written by composer Andrei Babaev:
https://smena-online.ru/stories/ya-vstretil-devushku

Sharofat Arabova's specialist essay “Soviet Politics and the Musicals of Tajikistan” places the film in the 1957 Tajikfilm programme and explains the cross-republic production system. It states that Tajik-language lyrics by Mirzo Tursunzade were translated into Russian, and that songs were re-recorded with well-known singers for all-Union distribution. It identifies the signature song by its Tajik rendering “Didam jamole” and Russian title “Ya vstretil devushku”; the Russian version used lyrics by Gabriel El-Registan and was sung by Azerbaijani singer Rashid Behbudov. The essay describes the film's score as by Andrei Babaev:
https://eefb.org/retrospectives/soviet-politics-and-the-musicals-of-tajikistan/

A separate filmography listing attributes the soundtrack song to Andrey Babaev, lyrics to Mirzo Tursun-Zade and Garold Registan, and performance to Rauf Atakishiyev; because transliterations and singer assignments conflict, this source is treated as a lead, not a definitive credit:
https://www.imdb.com/title/tt0314912/

## Version-control significance

This is a useful regional Soviet musical case because it involves at least two language/performance contexts. The Tajik original and Russian distribution versions must not be conflated. The specialist account describes re-recording for Russian-language distribution, which means the Russian vocal may not be the same performance or mix as the original Tajik version.

The exact title spelling, original singer(s), lyricist/adaptor roles, and the relationship between film-print sound, radio/record recordings and commercial releases remain to be verified. No standalone soundtrack release or archival carrier has yet been identified.

## 2026-11-17 — Tajik-language source reconciliation and later Gulshan performance lead

### Film title, song title and chronology
A 2019 Tajik-language film-history book by Tillo Neqqadamov, published by the Ministry of Culture's Research Institute of Culture and Information, distinguishes the film and signature song clearly. It gives the Tajik film title as **«Ман бо духтаре вохӯрдам»** and the song as **«Дидам чамоле»**; the Russian-language song title is **«Я встретил девушку»**. The book says production began in 1956 and the film appeared in 1957. The dossier's earlier wording that used “Didam jamole” as the film's Tajik title is corrected above.

### Composition and lyric credits
Neqqadamov's *Бозтоби чеҳраҳои дурахшони кино* (Dushanbe: Arzhang, 2019; ISBN 978-99947-43-85-6), pp. 154–156, describes Andrei Babaev (Андрей Бобоев) and Mirzo Tursunzade (Мирзо Турсунзода) as the composer/poet collaborators on the film's songs, prints the Tajik and Russian texts of “Дидам чамоле” / “Я встретил девушку,” and quotes a 2008 presidential speech attributing the song's text/music to Tursunzade and Babaev. The book also reproduces a 22 September 1957 letter from Soviet servicemen asking Tursunzade for the film's songs in Russian. That letter supports Russian-language circulation in 1957, but does not identify a singer or prove which soundtrack print they heard.

A 31 May 2025 Tajik-language article in Farah independently describes Tursunzade writing six or seven songs for the film and Babaev being assigned the music. It also notes debate about the melody's cultural origin and says Babaev became familiar with Eastern musical detail through contact with Ziyodullo Shahidi. By contrast, a 29 January 2022 Sputnik Tajikistan article attributes the melody of “Дидам ҷамоле” directly to Shahidi. This is an outlier against the 2019 Ministry research-institute book, the 2025 Farah account and the Tajik Writers Union's 2024 article, all of which credit Babaev. Do not silently promote Shahidi to co-composer; preserve the discrepancy pending a score, contemporary credit or primary production record.

### Singer attribution
Neqqadamov says Rashid Behbudov (Рашид Бейбутов) first performed “Дидам ҷамол” both on stage and in the film. This strengthens the Rashid attribution already present in regional reporting. The competing Rauf Atakishiyev claim remains in secondary online filmographies; neither a film-credit sheet nor a checked 1957 print has settled the actual vocal track. Keep the film-print singer provisional.

### Later Gulshan repertoire version — separate recording lead
The Tajik Writers Union's 18 December 2024 cultural-history article “Нақши созгор” by Ziyo Olimi documents the State Estrada Orchestra “Гулшан” (Gulshan): founded in 1962, renamed in May 1964 and transferred to the Tajik SSR State Committee for Television and Radio; Orifsho Orifov led it from 1967 to 1985. The article says Burhon Muhammadqulov (Бурҳон Муҳаммадқулов), in collaboration with Gulshan, performed Andrei Babaev's “Дидам ҷамоле” from the film. It does **not** give a performance date, recording/session number, carrier, tracklist, or surviving audio route. Treat this as a later repertoire/performance witness, not as the 1957 film vocal and not yet as a verified recording object. It is recorded separately as SF-AUD-007 / IDENTITY UNRESOLVED in the audio queue.

## 2026-11-17 — version-specific singer-attribution audit

### Contemporary film evidence
Rafail Perelshtein's first-person article in *Смена*, issue 723 (July 1957), describes the production as a musical film, says Babaev wrote its songs and dance music, and names several young cast members. It does not identify the vocalists. This is useful contemporary production evidence, but it is not a full credit sheet: https://smena-online.ru/stories/ya-vstretil-devushku

### Conflicting and potentially version-specific singer evidence
- Neqqadamov's 2019 book (pp. 154–155) says Rashid Behbudov first sang “Дидам ҷамоле” both on stage and in a feature film. It does not specify which language print or performance object the sentence refers to: https://pitfi.tj/sites/default/files/Tillo.pdf
- Arabova's 2020 specialist essay describes the production workflow: films were shot in Tajik, then dialogue and songs were re-recorded in Russian for all-Union distribution. For this film, it says the Russian text by Gabriel El-Registan was sung by Rashid Behbudov. This is the clearest version-specific secondary attribution located in this pass: https://eefb.org/retrospectives/soviet-politics-and-the-musicals-of-tajikistan/
- An archived 9 March 2011 song-history page instead says the film's off-screen singer was Rauf Atakishiyev and that Behbudov's rendition made the song especially famous. The same page separately lists Rauf and Behbudov as performers, but gives no recording dates or film-print identifiers: https://web.archive.org/web/20110309001257/http://www.100pesen.ru/index/ah_eta_devushka/0-186
- A 2017 Kultura.uz text reposted on OK.ru says online attributions conflict and reports a voice comparison favouring Atakishiyev over Behbudov for the off-screen film vocal. This is a secondary listening-based claim, not a checked production record: https://ok.ru/group/51882624155853/topic/67376203361229
- A 2022 OK.ru-posted discussion likewise says the film credits do not name the singer and favours Atakishiyev by ear. I have not independently inspected the film's closing credits, so the statement about their contents remains reported rather than verified: https://ok.ru/group/55860692189327/topic/154953101010319
- The *Красная книга российской эстрады* Babaev page lists separate “Я встретил девушку” entries for both Rauf Atakishiyev and Rashid Behbudov. It confirms that both performers' versions circulated, but does not identify either as a particular film-print vocal: https://kkre-2.narod.ru/babaev.htm

### Separate Behbudov television performance
The verified Gosteleradiofond channel's record for Behbudov's 1958 performance identifies it as a Central Television studio/pavilion recording. It is a distinct 1958 television performance of the film song, not the 1957 film soundtrack: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sle5KJywf6Y

Regional reporting in 2018 also describes the Russian-language song in Behbudov's performance as the version that became popular across the USSR, but does not independently identify the singer on a particular film print: https://pressa.tj/darguzashti-saodat-raeva-ofarandai-na-shi-zebo/ ; https://sputnik.tj/20180920/Darguzashti-Saodat-Juraeva-ofarandai-naqshi-Zebo-1026850406.html

### Assessment and next verification
A plausible reconciliation is that Atakishiyev sang the original Tajik-language film vocal while Behbudov sang the Russian-language re-recording for wider distribution; the 2020 essay supports the latter, and the archived 2011 source supports the former. This remains a **hypothesis**, not a verified crosswalk: the 2019 book's wording is broader, later voice comparisons are secondary, and no original print, title/credit sheet, session document or separate-language film copy has been inspected.

Keep the singer field **DISPUTED** and the two vocal/recording objects separate. Next decisive evidence would be a lawful comparison of the Tajik and Russian film versions, original soundtrack/session paperwork, or a contemporary production document naming each vocalist. No listening, audio comparison, download, archive contact or rights clearance was performed in this pass.
