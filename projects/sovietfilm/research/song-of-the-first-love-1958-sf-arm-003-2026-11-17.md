# SF-ARM-003 — «Առաջին սիրո երգը» / *Song of the First Love* (1958): Armenian film-song object family

**Status:** PROVISIONAL / PARTIALLY VERIFIED — Armenian Radio Archive catalogues multiple distinct recording entries associated with the film; film-print relationship and original recording provenance remain unknown  
**Film:** «Առաջին սիրո երգը» / *Song of the First Love* / «Песня первой любви» (1958)  
**Composer:** Arno Babajanian (Арно Бабаджанян)  
**Lyrics:** Hovhannes Ghukasyan appears on one archive item; credits are not complete across all entries  
**Primary catalogue:** Armenian Radio Archive, Public Radio of Armenia

## Catalogue objects located

1. **Sergey Davidyan, “Love Song”** — 1958, 2:58, composer Arno Babajanian, no lyrics supplied on the item page. Archive object: https://armradioarchive.am/en/song/erg-siro-masin-arajin-siro-ergy-k-f-97
2. **Eduard Sergey Baghdasaryan, “Love Song”** — 1958, 3:13, composer Arno Babajanian, lyrics Hovhannes Ghukasyan. Archive object: https://armradioarchive.am/en/song/erg-siro-masin-arajin-siro-ergy-k-f-4824
3. The archive's Sergey Davidyan performer catalogue also lists 1958 entries for “Song of the First Love”, “Song About Yerevan” and “Yerevani Sirun Aghjik”, including repeated title entries. The 1950s soundtrack index separately lists multiple *Song of the First Love* items and identifies the film as the source. These entries are not assumed to be separate original film takes until their item pages and identifiers are reconciled.

The archive describes its soundtrack corpus as music from Soviet Armenian feature films and states that Arno Babajanian's songs for *Song of the First Love*, sung by Sergey Davidyan, made it the first post-war Soviet film in which jazz was heard. This is archive-level contextual description, not a claim that every listed recording is an original film-print mix.

## Version and source distinctions

- The two item pages above are separate catalogue records with different performers and durations; retain them as distinct archive objects.
- Different singers do not prove a separate film scene or alternate film-print cue. The archive does not identify recording date, studio, orchestra, conductor, carrier, tape generation or relationship to the original film soundtrack.
- Do not merge the 1958 items with the 1960 and 1963 entries also indexed under the same film title. Those later dates may indicate later recordings, re-recordings, broadcast/archive transfers or metadata conventions; exact semantics remain unresolved.
- No audio comparison or film-print listening was performed.

## Why this qualifies as a new Soviet Film Music lead

This is a new 1958 Armenian film-song/cue family, not one of the five baseline film-level targets and not a duplicate of SF-ARM-001 or SF-ARM-002. It contains at least two distinct archive item records with named performers, composer, date and durations. It broadens the project beyond Russian-language productions and documents Armenian SSR film-music practice. Count it once as one cue family; do not count each archive page or performer version as a separate new lead.
