# `Космический рейс` / Cosmic Voyage (1935; released 1936): music and soundtrack versions

## Identification and scope

- Original title: `Космический рейс`
- Transliteration: `Kosmicheskiy reys`
- English titles: *Cosmic Voyage*, *The Space Voyage*
- Director: Vasily Zhuravlyov (Василий Журавлёв)
- Production: Mosfilm; production year commonly given as 1935, with a premiere/release date reported as 21 January 1936.
- This note separates the music associated with the original film from later compositions and re-scorings. No film version or soundtrack has been auditioned for this research pass.

## Original-era music: Valentin Kruchinin and classical excerpts

**WELL-SUPPORTED:** The original-era musical accompaniment is associated with Valentin Kruchinin (Валентин Кручинин), the composer also associated with *Аэлита* (1924). The Tsiolkovsky site’s film entry credits “музыкальное оформление — В. Кручинина” (musical accompaniment/design — V. Kruchinin). The Sound Museum of St Petersburg gives a more detailed account: it identifies Kruchinin as composer and describes a compilation score using excerpts from works by Franz Liszt, Felix Mendelssohn and Ludwig van Beethoven.

**Sound-format question — WELL-SUPPORTED as a silent film accompanied by music; exact sound-carrier method remains UNKNOWN.** A particularly important near-contemporary source has now been located:

- Nikolay Mayorov's specialist film-history page reproduces and transcribes the film's entry from *Вечерняя Москва*, 20 January 1936, no. 16, p. 4, the day before its reported general release. The entry explicitly describes the film as “немой (озвучен музыкой)” (“silent [accompanied/sounded with music]”), while crediting sound operator A. Zapadensky and “музыкальное оформление В. Кручинина” (musical design by V. Kruchinin). This is the strongest period evidence found so far for the film's exhibition classification and the relationship between its silent image and music. The original newspaper clipping is reproduced as an image on the page, but that image could not be independently retrieved in this session: https://cinemafirst.ru/kosmicheskij-rejs-1935/ (accessed 2026-11-16; see the citation to the 20 January 1936 newspaper at the top of the page).
- A separate *Вечерняя Москва* advertisement dated 27 January 1936 (issue no. 21) has been reported as placing the film under “ЗВУКОВОЙ ХУДОЖ. ФАНТАСТИЧ. ФИЛЬМ” (“sound feature fantasy film”): https://opac.nekrasovka.ru/books/NEWSPAPERS/VM/1936/VM_1936_021.pdf. The PDF could not be opened in this session. This is a genuine counter-indication, but its wording and context need direct inspection; it may refer to a cinema programme category or a different print/version, and should not outweigh the explicit 20 January classification without checking the actual page.
- The 1961 *Советские художественные фильмы: аннотированный каталог*, vol. 2, is bibliographically titled “Звуковые фильмы (1930–1957 гг.)” (“Sound Films, 1930–1957”) and a later filmography cites *Космический рейс* at pp. 67–68. The catalogue is a strong institutional bibliographic lead, but the actual entry text has not yet been read; inclusion in a volume is not proof that the original 1936 print had an optical soundtrack. National Electronic Library record: https://rusneb.ru/catalog/000199_000009_005451162/ ; Russian State Library record: https://search.rsl.ru/ru/record/01005451162 (accessed 2026-11-16).
- The Russian State Film Fund's 2020 report calls the film silent when discussing newly commissioned modern accompaniment. The San Francisco Silent Film Festival also describes it as silent and says it was made silent to maximise access in regions without sound-projection equipment: https://silentfilm.org/cosmic-voyage/ (Michael Atkinson, Festival 2014; accessed 2026-11-16).
- Musicologist M. E. Zolnikov places it among “late silent era” films in a 2018 peer-reviewed article; N. D. Irza's 2026 article explicitly frames Yusupova's later score as accompaniment to silent cinema. These are scholarly descriptions, not direct examination of the 1936 sound carrier.
- Mosfilm's current catalogue gives “Звук: моно” (“sound: mono”) but also misdates the film's release as 1933, so its technical field is a lead requiring clarification: https://www.mosfilm.ru/cinema/films/kosmicheskiy-reys/ (accessed 2026-11-16).
- The Tsiolkovsky film entry lists sound operator A. Zapadensky alongside Kruchinin's musical-design credit: https://www.tsiolkovsky.org/ru/video/kosmicheskiy-reis/ (accessed 2026-11-16). A sound-operator credit alone does not establish optical synchronization or dialogue.
- The Sound Museum of St Petersburg says there were no spoken scenes and that audiences heard instrumental music; it associates Kruchinin with excerpts from Liszt, Mendelssohn and Beethoven: https://soundmuseumspb.ru/events/2012/retrofantastics (accessed 2026-11-16).

**Best current formulation:** describe the original film as a **silent film accompanied by music**, following the explicit 20 January 1936 newspaper listing. Do not call it a conventional dialogue sound film. Whether the music was performed live at screenings, supplied on a separate synchronized medium, or existed in more than one exhibition format remains unresolved. The 27 January “sound feature” advertisement and the later catalogue classification warrant checking, but do not cancel the more specific contemporary “silent (accompanied by music)” entry. No sound negative, optical track, edge code, projection instructions, or original technical passport has been inspected.

**UNKNOWN:** The exact Liszt, Mendelssohn and Beethoven work titles, movements, cue order, orchestration, performers, conductor, recording method, and whether the reported compilation score survives. Do not infer any of these details from the composers' names alone.

## Later music and distinct versions

### Iraida Yusupova

**PROVISIONAL:** A programme page from the New Jerusalem Museum lists a “retro-futuristic soundtrack” for *Космический рейс* dated 1998 and credits Iraida Yusupova (Ираида Юсупова). A 2026 scholarly article by N. D. Irza specifically studies Yusupova's music for the 1935 film as a modern scoring of silent cinema. Its abstract describes a multi-layered score combining orchestral music, solo and choral vocal numbers, electronic sounds that imitate noises or form melodies, and spoken lines; it discusses leitmotifs and contrapuntal organization. This is evidence about Yusupova's later soundtrack, not Kruchinin's original-era accompaniment.

**UNKNOWN:** The exact premiere date and edition history of Yusupova's score, whether the 1998 date on the museum page refers to composition, first performance, or soundtrack release, and whether an authorized recording is commercially available.

### Mumiy Troll / `Великий немой` (2006)

**VERIFIED for the band's own announcement:** Mumiy Troll's official website announced a DVD release on 26 September 2006 of *Космический рейс* with a newly created soundtrack by Ilya Lagutenko, Yuri Tsaler, Pavel Shevchuk and Dmitry Plavshudin, together with three Mumiy Troll songs: “Инопланетный гость,” “Чёрная дыра,” and “Не звезда.” The announcement says the soundtrack was presented on 16 May 2006 as part of the `Великий немой` (“Great Silent”) project, which paired silent black-and-white films with new music by contemporary performers.

This 2006 soundtrack is a separate modern re-scoring. It is not evidence of the original 1930s score and should not be merged with Yusupova's work.

### Sergey Letov, Vladimir Goloukhov and Vladimir Kitlyar (2014)

**WELL-SUPPORTED by the performers' project page:** A live accompaniment was commissioned for the Jewish Museum and Tolerance Center in Moscow and first presented on 18 June 2014. The page identifies Sergey Letov, Vladimir Goloukhov and Vladimir Kitlyar, and lists contemporary electronic wind controllers/synthesizers, a MIDI vibraphone or keyboard, and thereminvox among the performance equipment. The page documents this specific modern performance ensemble; it does not identify instruments used in the 1930s film production.

### TWINS Piano Duo and Festival Symphony Orchestra (2019)

**VERIFIED by Gosfilmofond's institutional report:** Gosfilmofond reported on 25 March 2020 that a new composition had been written for the film by pianist-composers Igor Sviridov and Maxim Markov (TWINS Piano Duo). It says the score was presented with the Festival Symphony Orchestra of St Petersburg and that the original notation was being prepared for transfer to Gosfilmofond's archive in Belye Stolby, to be attached to the film's dossier. The article places the *Cosmic Voyage* performance on 15 November, following a separate performance for *Обломок империи* on 14 November; the context indicates the 2019 St Petersburg Cultural Forum period.

This is an archival accession lead for a modern commissioned score. It is not Kruchinin's original score. The article's phrase “original sheet-music score” means the original manuscript/notation of the new TWINS composition in this context, not the lost 1930s music.
