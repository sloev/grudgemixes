# SF-UZ-002 — «Дилором» / *Dilorom* (Uzbekfilm, 1968)

**Status:** IN PROGRESS — qualified regional film-music lead.  
**Count:** one film-opera production/music object; not a separate soundtrack release or a count for the underlying stage opera.

## Production identity

The 1995 *Советские художественные фильмы: аннотированный каталог 1968–1969 гг.* entry identifies «ДИЛОРОМ» as a 1968 Uzbekfilm film-opera, widescreen and colour, nine parts / 2,540 metres, and says it was not released to the all-Union screen. It names K. Yashen and A. Khamraev as scriptwriters, Ali Khamraev as director, Khatam Fayziev as cinematographer and M. Ashrafi as composer. The entry lists the State Academic Bolshoi Theatre of Opera and Ballet named for Alisher Navoi symphony orchestra and choir, conducted by M. Ashrafi, and gives character/singer pairings for the principal roles.

The 2009 *Аннотированный каталог художественного кино Узбекистана (1925–2008)*, published by the Academy of Sciences of Uzbekistan's Institute of Art Studies, independently lists «ДИЛОРОМ» as a 1968 Uzbekfilm adaptation, credits M. Ashrafi as composer, and states that it is based on the opera of the same name and dedicated to the 525th anniversary of Alisher Navoi. These records support one 1968 Uzbekfilm production, not separate 1967 and 1968 films. A Shaxs.uz page labels the film “1967” in its heading but its own description says it was filmed in 1968; retain the 1967/1968 catalogue discrepancy as a metadata conflict, with 1968 supported by both film catalogues.

## Music-object boundaries

This is an opera-film adaptation, not a conventional newly composed film score. Keep separate:

1. Mukhtar Ashrafi's stage opera «Дилором» (the 1958 stage production and later versions are separately documented).
2. The 1968 Uzbekfilm film-opera adaptation and its specific film soundtrack / performance.
3. Any later stage revival, concert suite, recording or soundtrack release.

The 1995 film catalogue names a symphony orchestra and choir, conductor M. Ashrafi, and singer/role pairs. For example, it identifies G. Khamraeva as Dilorom, with S. Kabulova singing; Sh. Ergashev as Moni, with S. Yarashev singing; and Z. Muhamedjanov as Shah Bahram, with S. Benjaminov singing. These are film-level performance credits, not proof that a soundtrack LP/CD exists or that any surviving digital audio corresponds to the original print.

## Regional and cultural context

The production is a valuable Uzbek SSR case rather than a Moscow-centred example. Its source opera is based on Alisher Navoi's poem «Семь планет» / «Sab’ai sayyor» and was produced for the 525th anniversary of Navoi's birth. The film catalogue's synopsis describes Dilorom and the painter Moni's love and separation. Do not flatten the Uzbek opera and performance tradition into generic “Soviet film music”; retain Uzbek-language title forms and local institutional names as they are found.
