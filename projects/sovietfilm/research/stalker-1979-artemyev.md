# Сталкер (1979): Eduard Artemyev's soundtrack and the album recording of «Медитация»

**Research status:** WELL-SUPPORTED for Artemyev/Tarkovsky's score collaboration and the existence of later album reworkings; DISPUTED for the exact issue/track metadata of «Сталкер: Медитация»; UNKNOWN for the precise correspondence between any album version and the original 1979 film soundtrack.  
**Audio-collection status:** SF-AUD-005 — STREAM ONLY (no permitted downloadable file route verified).  
**Accessed:** 2026-11-16.

## Identification

- Film: Андрей Тарковский, *Сталкер* / *Stalker* (1979).
- Composer: Эдуард Артемьев / Eduard Artemyev.
- Track-level candidate: «Сталкер: Медитация» / “Stalker: Meditation” on the later album programme *Солярис / Зеркало / Сталкер* / *Solaris. The Mirror. Stalker*. Track number and duration are release-dependent and currently DISPUTED: Nostalghia's review of the 2002 Electroshock ELCD 012 CD gives track 9 / 5:21; MusicBrainz's ELCD 012 record dated 1999 gives track 10 / 5:26; Milan/Spotify metadata and the 2013 Mirumir LP give track 9 or side-A track 4 / 5:26.
- The programme contains 13 tracks on the CD/digital issues and six tracks across both sides of the 2013 LP. Do not copy one track number or duration across editions without specifying the release object.

## Recording-object distinction

Do **not** identify the album track as the original 1979 film-mix recording. Petr Gajdošík's Nostalghia review of the 2002 Electroshock edition says the album material was newly developed, supplemented, recomposed and remixed by Artemyev; it is an authorial album version, not a simple transfer of the film soundtrack. The same review describes “Meditation” as newly mixed, though close to the film's conception.

The 2013 Mirumir LP MIR100709 is a later vinyl issue of this 1990 album-re-recording programme, not evidence of a new 2013 performance. Mirumir/Vinylology's 2013 release announcement says the pieces had first been issued in 1990; the retailer's detailed notes identify the tracks as re-recorded versions of music from the films and give a 1990 phonogram copyright. Exact master identity across the 1990 Torso Kino CD, 1999/2002 Electroshock CD issues, Milan CD and 2013 LP still requires physical-package and audio comparison.

The 2013 LP's «Meditation» is side A, track 4, 5:26. Its notes credit Artemyev with recording and mixing that track at his own studio; tracks A1–A3 and B1–B2 are credited to Studio Le Roy. This is release-production metadata, not proof that the LP track is identical to any specific film-print passage.

## Film-score production context

Criterion's 2017 *Stalker* press notes report that Tarkovsky first rejected a conventional orchestral score Artemyev had prepared, with the tar leading the melody. Artemyev then developed the accepted music through the Synthi 100's effects channels and altered the recorded tar by slowing playback. This is a documented account of the film-score production process, not proof that every album track or the 5:21 / 5:26 album version reproduces the original film soundtrack unchanged.

## 2026-11-18 cue-level advance — Artemyev's first-person account

A first-person account by Eduard Artemyev, published from Maya Turovskaya's 1991 book and transcribed by Nostalghia, supplies the strongest cue-level evidence found in this pass. Artemyev says Tarkovsky liked the final film theme that appears in **Stalker's dream and in the opening titles**. The final version used synthesizer timbres (he compares the spatial sound to a tampura-like resonance) with **tar and flute**. During dubbing, Tarkovsky selected only one episode from roughly ten musical episodes.

The same account separates the rejected first attempt from the final film score: Artemyev first tried a 14th-century Catholic chant, **“Pulcherrima Rosa,”** in a four-hour session; Tarkovsky rejected that version. Do not treat this chant prototype as a cue in the final 1979 film print. It is a separate compositional experiment.

### Object boundaries

1. **1979 film-print audio:** the final theme in Stalker's dream and the opening titles. Exact timecode and start/end points remain unverified.
2. **Rejected prototype:** “Pulcherrima Rosa,” a discarded first attempt, not established as present in the released film.
3. **Later album/re-recording objects:** including the separately catalogued “Сталкер: Медитация” item (SF-AUD-005). The interview does not equate that later recording with the film-print cue.

Mosfilm's official film page currently lists a 2025 4K restoration, 163 minutes, mono, and a viewing route. This is a useful authorised film-print route, but the page does not establish that the restoration's audio is byte-identical to the original 1979 release. No playback or audio audition was performed.

**Baseline status: RESEARCHED-OPEN (2026-11-18).** The composer interview, official film record, cue placement, recording palette, rejected prototype, version boundaries, access route and rights limitations have been checked as far as reasonably available. Remaining unknowns: exact cue timecodes in an authorised print; whether the 2025 restoration preserves the original 1979 mono mix; original score/cue-sheet or dubbing records; exact mapping, if any, between the film cue and later album tracks; current rights and permitted research-copy terms.

**Next action:** inspect the official Mosfilm viewing copy without copying it, log the dream/opening-title cue timecodes, and seek the original 1979 score, cue sheet or dubbing/session documentation. Keep SF-AUD-009 separate from SF-AUD-005.

## 2026-11-18 source-provenance audit — Artemyev/Turovskaya interview

A second Nostalghia transcription was checked against the existing cue-level note. It identifies the text as taken from Maya Turovskaya's *7½, ili filmy Andreia Tarkovskogo* (Moscow: Iskusstvo, 1991, ISBN 5-210-00279-9). The passage explicitly names the relevant visual episodes as **Stalker's dream and the opening titles**, describes a synthesizer timbre compared to the Indian tampur, and names the **tar and flute** as the acoustic instruments brought into the final solution. It also says Tarkovsky sometimes retained only one episode from roughly ten during dubbing. This strengthens the provenance of the interview evidence but still does not supply cue timecodes, a score/cue sheet, or proof that the later «Сталкер: Медитация» album track is the same recording object.

## 2026-11-19 — Gosfilmofond media-archive context: Tarkovsky's stated sound intention

A Gosfilmofond-hosted Tarkovsky media-archive article, “1970. Весна — Знакомство с Эдуардом Артемьевым”, was opened and checked. In its passage on Stalker, it quotes Tarkovsky speaking with Olga Surkova about the still-undecided sound design: he did not want conventional music at the beginning, imagined noises and a short musical fragment, and wanted the sound world to develop gradually rather than introduce a conventional overture/theme. This is a valuable director-intent witness for the film's sound concept and reinforces the need to treat sound design, musical fragments and later album tracks separately.

The quotation is not a cue sheet, score, recording log or proof of what was finally retained in the 1979 print. The article's precise original publication date/source for the Surkova conversation is not given in the opened page, so the wording is recorded as a Gosfilmofond-hosted quotation, not as a contemporaneous document independently inspected. It does not resolve the exact timecodes or the relation between the film mix and «Сталкер: Медитация».

**Research effect:** substantive contextual advance to the baseline case; status remains RESEARCHED-OPEN. Next action remains authorized-print timecoding plus original score/cue-sheet/dubbing documentation. No film was played and no audio was auditioned or copied.
